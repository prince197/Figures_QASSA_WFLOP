## Summary of changes relative to the earlier answers

- **Thesis changed:** the earlier paper proposed LX-SSA-VNS as a better algorithm. The new paper, *Baseline Configuration and Random-Sampling Controls in Metaheuristic Comparisons for Wind Farm Layout Optimization*, makes three findings on the discretized Kusiak–Song benchmark with random starts at 6,030 evaluations:
  - **M1** The PSO setting w = 0.7, c1 = c2 = 2 lies beyond the order-2 (variance) convergence boundary; in a sweep of c1 = c2, feasibility starts to degrade there and falls progressively beyond it. With it, PSO ranks behind both salp-swarm hybrids in the method pool of the earlier comparison; with the constriction setting, it ranks first there.
  - **M2** A salp-swarm first phase adds nothing beyond equal-cost random sampling in the farm (a gain above 0.05 pp of wake loss is ruled out across seeds and cases), LX-SSA as published is worse, and a PSO phase gives a clear gain; at this budget the VNS phase acts essentially as one compass-search descent. The earlier answer ("an SSA phase gives only a small gain over random sampling, an LX-SSA phase none, and the Laplace step makes the hybrid worse") is replaced: the small SSA gain existed only against the weak square-sampling control, and the Laplace step cannot be separated from LX-SSA's double evaluation cost.
  - **M3** PSO-VNS has the best average rank with 15°, 5° and 1° direction bins, also under a Gaussian wake at 1°, but its average advantage over a well-configured PSO is small: equivalent within ±0.05 pp across seeds and cases but not farm clusters under the 15° rose, a small significant advantage under a 1° rose, heterogeneous per case (both directions). PSO-VNS reaches feasibility more reliably on the Horns Rev 1 block (30/30 vs 19/30 runs). The earlier answer ("the most consistent performer; not significantly different from PSO over all cases; PSO first under the Gaussian re-evaluation") is replaced by these equivalence levels and the direction-resolution result.
  This affects R1-4, R1-5, R1-7, R1-8, R2-1, R2-3, R3-1 and R3-2.
- **Methodological contribution:** the controlled evaluation protocol (equal budgets counting every objective and gradient evaluation, seed-paired runs, feasibility-aware ranking, Holm-corrected tests), equivalence at three levels with minimal margins, equal-cost random-sampling controls (RSD-VNS, RS-VNS) for hybrids, direction-resolution robustness (1° re-evaluation), supporting theory (order-2 stability, box sampling, feasible starts, equivalence), diagnostics from instrumented reruns, recommendations for benchmarking WFLOP methods, and more than 200 automated claim checks.
- **Corrections made during the revision:** Horns Rev 1 direction binning corrected and all Horns Rev 1 runs repeated; PyWake reference recomputed at identical bins (the stale reference value and the rotor-averaging explanation were dropped; the difference is due to C_T at the free-stream speed); the statement that optimized Horns Rev 1 layouts exceed the installed block was removed (none does at 1°); IEA37 gaps recomputed with published layouts projected onto the boundary.
- **Not, or only partly, addressed in the new manuscript:**
  - R1-2: 5D/6D re-optimization of the proposed method (only an archived SSA/LX-SSA check).
  - R1-9: the full 80-turbine Horns Rev 1 farm (dropped; 16-turbine block plus IEA37 instead).
  - R2-3: φ/χ sensitivity (moot for the proposed method).
  - R2-5: published Kusiak–Song results are discussed in the supplement but not compared numerically (protocols differ; values not verified against the original papers).
  - R3-3: CMA-ES and L-SHADE are not in the pool (stated in *Limitations*).
  - R3-4: verification of the new references.
  - R1-12: final proofreading of the version without placeholders.
- **New material not requested by the reviewers:** the baseline-setting analysis with the c-sweep and bound-handling variants (*Effect of the Baseline Setting*, `tab:baseline`, `sec:S-csweep`); the random-sampling controls RSD-VNS and RS-VNS; equivalence analysis at three levels and inference-robustness analyses; the direction-resolution re-evaluation (1° bins) of the benchmark and Horns Rev 1; the PyWake check; algorithm diagnostics (PSO dynamics, VNS cycle shares, feasible-start stall); the theory supplement; feasibility-preserving initialization and budget scaling; IEA37 Case Study 1; the budget-split study; *Discussion and Recommendations*; public code and data.
