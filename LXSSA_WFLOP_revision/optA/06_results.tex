\section{Results on the Benchmark}
\label{sec:results}
This section reports the 68 benchmark cases (\NRunsBenchmark{} runs, 6,030 evaluations each); per-case means and standard deviations are given in Tables~\ref{S-tab:res-1-500}--\ref{S-tab:res-2-1000} of the supplementary material.

\subsection{Effect of the Baseline Setting}
\label{sec:psosetting}
\label{sec:baseline}
\IfFileExists{analysis/mpce_tab_baseline.tex}{\input{analysis/mpce_tab_baseline.tex}}{}
The old setting violates the order-2 (variance) stability condition~\eqref{eq:poli}. Run on the same cases with the same budget and seeds as the constriction setting~\eqref{eq:constriction}, it returns a feasible layout in only \NOldPSOFeas\% of the runs (fewer than 15 feasible runs in \NOldPSOBelowHalf{} cases), is significantly worse in \NOldPSOWorse{} of the 68 cases and never better (Wilcoxon, 30 seed-paired runs), and has a mean wake loss \NOldPSODL{} percentage points higher over the \NOldPSOCases{} cases in which both settings have at least 15 feasible runs. Instrumented reruns show the mechanism (Table~\ref{S-tab:D-psodyn}): with the old setting the final swarm is \NDSpreadRatio{} times as wide and \NDVelRatio{} times as fast, \NDClipOld\% of the coordinates are clipped (constriction: \NDClipNew\%), and only \NDFeasPartOld\% of the particle evaluations in the second half of a run are feasible (\NDFeasPartNew\%). Its infeasible final layouts mostly violate only the spacing constraint (\NDStoredSpacingOnlyOld{} of \NDStoredInfeasOld): as $g^{\rm b}$ is in m$^2$ and $g^{\rm s}$ in m, the best particle lies inside the farm, with turbines too close. In Table~\ref{tab:friedman68}, the old setting would place PSO \NOldPSOPos{} (average rank \NOldPSORank), not \NPSOPos.
% CHECK-FINAL [C12]: pso_setting: constriction_vs_old L == 0, W >= 34; positions old > constriction == 2 (PSOC ahead of SSA, LXSSA, SSABV: now only via Table I / next paragraph, C27)
% CHECK-DIAG [D03] spread/|V| ratios; [D04] clipped share; [D05] feasible particle evaluations; [D06] stored infeasible finals mostly spacing-only (m^2 vs m reading: W3 note)

Table~\ref{tab:baseline} shows the effect on a comparison: it ranks the methods of Table~\ref{tab:friedman68}, with LX-SSA-VNS in place of PSO-VNS, once with each PSO setting; all other methods use identical runs in both columns. With the old setting, \NBaseOldBest{} has the best average rank and PSO ranks behind a salp-swarm hybrid; with the constriction setting, PSO ranks ahead of every salp-swarm method, and the W/T/L of SSA-VNS against PSO changes from \NBaseOldSSAVNSvsPSO{} to \NBaseNewSSAVNSvsPSO. The constriction coefficients are standard values~\cite{Clerc2002}, not tuned on these cases, nor are those of the other methods. The old setting was run only at 6,030 evaluations; with more evaluations the order changes again: on the six largest cases at 120,030 evaluations, SSA-VNS ranks ahead of PSO (average ranks \NBudRankSSAVNSOneTwentyK{} and \NBudRankPSOOneTwentyK; Section~\ref{sec:budget}).
% CHECK-FINAL [C27]: baseline: under the old PSO setting at least one salp-swarm hybrid (SSA-VNS or LX-SSA-VNS) ranks ahead of PSO; under the corrected setting PSO ranks ahead of every salp-swarm method (SSA, LX-SSA, SSA-VNS, LX-SSA-VNS)
% CHECK-FINAL [C45]: at 120,030 evaluations SSA-VNS ranks ahead of PSO on the six largest cases
A PSO baseline that violates~\eqref{eq:poli} thus makes methods compared with it, including our own LX-SSA, appear stronger than they are; hence PSO is the first phase of PSO-VNS.

The run-level W/T/L counts (Table~\ref{tab:wtl}; Holm-adjusted within each case over the seven comparisons with PSO-VNS) are descriptive. Against PSO, PSO-VNS is significantly better in \NWtlPSOW{} cases, tied in \NWtlPSOT{} and worse in \NWtlPSOL. On the case means the two are practically equivalent at $\pm\NEqMargin$ percentage points: $\overline{\Delta L}=\NCmPSOVNSvsPSODL$ (90\% bootstrap CI \NEqPSOVNSvsPSOCI; TOST $p=\NEqPSOVNSvsPSOP$; minimal equivalence margin \NEqPSOVNSvsPSOMin; Bayesian signed-rank $P(\mathrm{ROPE})>0.99$; Table~\ref{S-tab:equivalence}), \NXEnPSOVNSvsPSOPct\% of the benchmark AEP, and PSO has the lower case mean in \NCmPSOVNSvsPSOLosses{} of the 68 cases. Neither the per-case standard deviation nor the worst feasible run differs significantly ($p=\NSdP$ and \NWorstP). The advantage of PSO-VNS is concentrated in the larger layouts of Data Set~II (post hoc split): with $N\ge10$ it has the lower mean wake loss in \NCmPSOVNSvsPSOdsIILargeWins{} of \NCmPSOVNSvsPSOdsIILargeN{} cases (Wilcoxon $p=\NCmPSOVNSvsPSOdsIILargeP$; $p=\NXHolmPSOVNSvsPSOdsIILargeP$ Holm-adjusted over all \NXMultN{} case-mean tests of the paper; \NXEnPSOVNSvsPSOdsIILargePct\% of AEP), significantly more than with $N<10$ (permutation within clusters, $p=\NXIntDsIIP$), and the gain grows with $N$ within every Data Set~II farm (mean Spearman $\rho=\NXSpearDsIIStrat$, $p=\NXSpearDsIIStratP$). Data Set~I shows no such effect ($p=\NXIntDsIP$; PSO is lower in \NCmPSOVNSvsPSOdsILargeLosses{} of the \NCmPSOVNSvsPSOdsILargeN{} cases with $N\ge10$), and pooled over both data sets the $N\ge10$ subgroup is not significant after the Holm correction ($p=\NXHolmPSOVNSvsPSOLargeP$). For $N<10$ both reach nearly the same wake loss (mean absolute difference \NAbsDiffPSOSmall{} percentage points). Against SSA-VNS (a salp-swarm first phase), PSO-VNS is significantly better in \NWtlSSAVNSW{} cases and never worse ($p_{\rm Holm}=\NPWSSAVNS$). Against SSA, LX-SSA, DE, VNS and MS-SLSQP, it is significantly better in most cases; \NLossesPhrase.

PSO-VNS is thus a simple reference method with limits: it has the best average rank but is, on average, practically equivalent to PSO; it adds feasibility in every run (Horns Rev~1: \NHRFeasPSOVNS{} vs \NHRFeasPSO{} for PSO, Section~\ref{sec:hornsrev-site}) and gains in the larger Data Set~II layouts. On the six largest cases it ranks best at all three budgets (exploratory; Section~\ref{sec:budget}). The order of the leading methods depends on the conditions: with feasible initial layouts, \NFbFeasBestRank{} ranks first (Section~\ref{sec:feasinit}), and with a Gaussian wake model, \NBestGauss{} ranks narrowly ahead of PSO-VNS (Section~\ref{sec:gaussian}).
