A population method keeps $N_p$ candidate vectors $\mathbf X^i\in\mathbb R^{n}$ with components $X^i_m$ and bounds $lb_m=-r$, $ub_m=r$; unless stated otherwise, one iteration costs $N_p$ objective evaluations. This section states the methods exactly as implemented (Algorithms~\ref{alg:pso}--\ref{alg:vns}); Table~\ref{tab:params} collects all settings. None of them was tuned on the benchmark.

\subsection{PSO and Its Stability Conditions}
\label{sec:pso}
We use the canonical global-best PSO~\cite{Kennedy1995} in the inertia-weight form~\cite{Shi1998} (Algorithm~\ref{alg:pso}). Particle $i$ has a position $\mathbf X^i$, a velocity $\mathbf V^i$ and a personal best $\mathbf P^i$, the swarm keeps the global best $\mathbf G$, and a particle moves by
\begin{align}
\mathbf V^i&\leftarrow w\,\mathbf V^i+c_1\boldsymbol\rho_1\circ\bigl(\mathbf P^i-\mathbf X^i\bigr)+c_2\boldsymbol\rho_2\circ\bigl(\mathbf G-\mathbf X^i\bigr),\label{eq:pso-v}\\
\mathbf X^i&\leftarrow\mathbf X^i+\mathbf V^i,\label{eq:pso-x}
\end{align}
where $\boldsymbol\rho_1,\boldsymbol\rho_2$ are vectors of independent $U(0,1)$ numbers, drawn anew for every move, and $\circ$ is the componentwise product. The constriction PSO of Clerc and Kennedy~\cite{Clerc2002} with their standard choice $\varphi_1=\varphi_2=2.05$ (constriction factor $\kappa=0.7298$) is identical to \eqref{eq:pso-v} with
\begin{equation}
w=0.7298,\qquad c_1=c_2=\kappa\varphi_1=1.49618 .
\label{eq:constriction}
\end{equation}
PSO denotes \eqref{eq:pso-v}, \eqref{eq:pso-x} with these coefficients, fixed a priori from the literature, and the \emph{old setting} the same algorithm with $w=0.7$, $c_1=c_2=2$, i.e., the acceleration coefficients of the original PSO~\cite{Kennedy1995} combined with an inertia weight. Both are run as implemented: velocities start at zero and are not clamped (no $V_{\max}$), a coordinate clipped to the square (Section~\ref{sec:constraints}) keeps its velocity, and $\mathbf P^i$, $\mathbf G$ are updated after each evaluation (asynchronous global best).

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{PSO (global best, inertia-weight form) as implemented; $N_p(T+1)$ evaluations}
\label{alg:pso}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State Draw (seeded) and evaluate $N_p$ layouts $\mathbf X^i$; $\mathbf V^i\gets\mathbf 0$, $\mathbf P^i\gets\mathbf X^i$, $\mathbf G\gets\arg\min_i F_p(\mathbf P^i)$
\For{$t=1,\ldots,T$ and $i=1,\ldots,N_p$}
    \State Draw $\boldsymbol\rho_1,\boldsymbol\rho_2\sim U(0,1)^n$; update $\mathbf V^i$ by \eqref{eq:pso-v} \Comment{no velocity clamping}
    \State $\mathbf X^i\gets\operatorname{clip}(\mathbf X^i+\mathbf V^i,-r,r)$ \Comment{box repair; $\mathbf V^i$ is kept}
    \State Evaluate $F_p(\mathbf X^i)$; if better, replace $\mathbf P^i$ and then $\mathbf G$ \Comment{strict improvement}
\EndFor
\State \Return $\mathbf G$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\emph{Stability under stagnation.} Consider one coordinate $x=X^i_m$ with $v=V^i_m$, $p=P^i_m$ and $g=G_m$, and assume (A1) \emph{stagnation}, $p$ and $g$ fixed, and (A2) \emph{no active bound}, so that $v_t=x_t-x_{t-1}$. With $\varphi_{k,t}=c_k\rho_{k,t}$ and $\varphi_t=\varphi_{1,t}+\varphi_{2,t}$, every coordinate then follows, independently of the others, the random linear recursion
\begin{equation}
x_{t+1}=(1+w-\varphi_t)\,x_t-w\,x_{t-1}+\varphi_{1,t}\,p+\varphi_{2,t}\,g ,
\label{eq:pso-rec}
\end{equation}
whose random coefficient $\varphi_t$ has mean $\mu=(c_1+c_2)/2$ and variance $\sigma^2=(c_1^2+c_2^2)/12$. The update is \emph{order-1 stable} if $\mathrm E\,x_t$ converges, for all initial values $(x_0,x_1)$, to a limit that does not depend on them, and \emph{order-2 stable} if, in addition, $\mathrm E\,x_t^2$ does~\cite{Poli2009}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:pso-stability}
Let $c_1,c_2\ge0$, $c_1+c_2>0$, and assume (A1)--(A2).
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $\mathrm E\,x_t\to x^*=(c_1p+c_2g)/(c_1+c_2)$ for all initial values if and only if $\lvert w\rvert<1$ and $0<c_1+c_2<4(1+w)$.
\item[(b)] The update is order-2 stable if and only if $\lvert w\rvert<1$ and
\begin{equation}
\chi(1):=2\mu(1-w^2)-(1-w)\mu^2-(1+w)\sigma^2>0 .
\label{eq:pso-o2}
\end{equation}
For $c_1=c_2$, \eqref{eq:pso-o2} is Poli's condition~\cite{Poli2009}
\begin{equation}
c_1+c_2<\frac{24\,(1-w^2)}{7-5w};
\label{eq:poli}
\end{equation}
for $c_1\ne c_2$ with the same sum $c_1+c_2$, it is stricter.
\item[(c)] Under (b), $\operatorname{Var}x_t\to V_\infty=(1+w)\,q/\chi(1)$ with $q=c_1^2c_2^2(p-g)^2/[6(c_1+c_2)^2]$; for $c_1=c_2=c$,
\begin{equation}
V_\infty=\frac{c\,(1+w)}{4\,[12(1-w^2)-c\,(7-5w)]}\,(p-g)^2 ,
\label{eq:pso-vinf}
\end{equation}
which is zero if and only if $p=g$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
(a) As $\rho_{1,t},\rho_{2,t}$ are independent of $(x_t,x_{t-1})$, $e_t=\mathrm E\,x_t-x^*$ obeys $e_{t+1}=(1+w-\mu)\,e_t-w\,e_{t-1}$; both roots of $\lambda^2-(1+w-\mu)\lambda+w$ lie strictly inside the unit circle if and only if $\lvert w\rvert<1$ and $\lvert 1+w-\mu\rvert<1+w$ (Schur--Cohn), i.e., $0<\mu<2(1+w)$. (b) With $y_t=x_t-x^*$, $z_t=(\mathrm Ey_t^2,\mathrm Ey_ty_{t-1},\mathrm Ey_{t-1}^2)^{\mathsf T}$ obeys $z_{t+1}=Mz_t+b_t$ with a $3\times3$ matrix $M(w,\mu,\sigma^2)$ and $b_t\to(q,0,0)^{\mathsf T}$, so order-2 stability means a spectral radius of $M$ below one; of Jury's four conditions for its characteristic polynomial $\chi$, one is $\lvert w\rvert<1$, one holds for all such $w$, and one follows from $\chi(1)>0$. For $c_1=c_2=c$, $\chi(1)=c\,[12(1-w^2)-c(7-5w)]/6$; for fixed $c_1+c_2$, $\sigma^2$ is smallest at $c_1=c_2$. (c) $V_\infty$ is the first component of the fixed point of $z\mapsto Mz+(q,0,0)^{\mathsf T}$. Full proof: Section~\ref{S-sec:S-th-pso}.
\end{proof}
The order-1 condition involves the \emph{mean} $(c_1+c_2)/2$ of $\varphi_t$; the constant-coefficient condition $\varphi<2(1+w)$ with $\varphi=c_1+c_2$ would wrongly classify the old setting as order-1 unstable ($4>3.4$). For $c_1=c_2$, the region also holds under a weaker, non-stagnant assumption~\cite{Cleghorn2018}. The constriction setting is order-2 stable ($c_1+c_2=2.99<3.35$; spectral radius of $M$ 0.94): in the limit, a coordinate with $P^i_m\ne G_m$ is sampled with mean $(P^i_m+G_m)/2$ and standard deviation $1.04\,\lvert P^i_m-G_m\rvert$, so the swarm keeps sampling around $\mathbf G$ unless $\mathbf P^i=\mathbf G$. The old setting is order-1 stable ($4<6.8$) but beyond the order-2 boundary ($4>3.50$; $c_1=c_2>c^*=\NSBoundC$ at $w=0.7$; spectral radius 1.12), so its second moments grow without bound under stagnation (Fig.~\ref{fig:S-th-stability}; Proposition~\ref{S-prop:S-settings}). Neither condition says how good $\mathbf G$ is, and clipping violates (A2): a clipped coordinate keeps its outward velocity and stays at the bound while it points outward (Lemma~\ref{S-lem:S-clip}), so in the code the unbounded moments appear as turbines pinned at the box, not as growing positions.

\subsection{Salp Swarm Algorithm and Its Laplacian Variant}
\label{sec:ssa}
\label{sec:S-ssa}
The SSA~\cite{Mirjalili2017} (Algorithm~\ref{alg:S-ssa}) keeps the best solution found so far as the food source $\mathbf H$ and splits the population, sorted by $F_p$, into leaders and followers. A leader $i\le N_p/2$ updates each coordinate by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leader}
X_m^i = H_m\pm\xi_1\big((ub_m-lb_m)\xi_2+lb_m\big),
\end{equation}
with either sign with probability $1/2$, $\xi_2\sim U(0,1)$ and $\xi_1=2\exp[-(4t/T)^2]$ decreasing with the iteration $t$ out of $T$; with $lb_m=-r$, $ub_m=r$, a leader thus samples $H_m+U(-\xi_1r,\xi_1r)$ independently in every coordinate, and $\xi_1\le0.037$ for $t\ge T/2$. A follower $i>N_p/2$ moves to the midpoint of its current position and the \emph{new} position of the preceding salp (already updated in the same iteration). The leaders thus perturb $\mathbf H$ uniformly with a shrinking radius, and the followers only average~\cite{Castelli2022}. LX-SSA~\cite{Solanki2023} gives each follower a second candidate $Y_m^i=X_m^i+\gamma(H_m-X_m^i)$ with one Laplace step~\cite{Deep2007} for all coordinates,
\begin{equation}
\gamma=\phi-\chi\,\mathrm{sgn}\bigl(z-\tfrac12\bigr)\ln\bigl(1-2\lvert z-\tfrac12\rvert\bigr),\quad z\sim U(0,1),
\label{eq:laplace}
\end{equation}
$\phi=0$, $\chi=1$, and keeps the better candidate. As published, it evaluates both candidates of each follower and then re-evaluates the kept ones with the new population. With the $N_p$ candidate evaluations of the $N_p/2$ followers, an LX-SSA iteration therefore costs $2N_p$ evaluations and an SSA iteration $N_p$: at 6,030 evaluations, stand-alone SSA runs for $T=200$ and LX-SSA for $T=100$ iterations, and in the hybrids with $\omega=0.5$, Phase~1 runs for $T=100$ (SSA) and $T=50$ (LX-SSA) iterations, 3,030 evaluations each, with $\xi_1$ decreasing over these $T$ iterations. Comparing the two thus tests LX-SSA as published, not the Laplace step alone. Both use the code of the previous study, stand-alone and inside the hybrids.

% verified: analysis/authors_optimizers.py SSA/LXSSA: leaders = pop_size // 2 = 15; follower = (pop[i] + new_pop[i-1])/2; LX: one scalar gamma per follower, candidate = pop[i] + gamma (food - pop[i]); both clipped and evaluated, then whole population evaluated (2 N_p per iteration)

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{SSA and LX-SSA (lines marked LX: LX-SSA only); evaluations per line in brackets}
\label{alg:S-ssa}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State Draw and evaluate $N_p$ salps; sort them by $F_p$ and set the best as food source $\mathbf H$. \Comment{[$N_p$]}
\For{$t=1$ to $T$}
    \State Update $\xi_1=2\exp[-(4t/T)^2]$.
    \For{salp $i=1,\ldots,N_p$}
        \If{$i\le N_p/2$}
            \State Update leader $i$ using \eqref{eq:leader} (new position $\tilde{\mathbf X}^i$).
        \Else
            \State Form the follower candidate $\mathbf Z^i=\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf X^i+\tilde{\mathbf X}^{i-1})$ and set $\tilde{\mathbf X}^i\gets\mathbf Z^i$.
            \State LX: form $\mathbf Y^i$ using \eqref{eq:laplace}, box-repair $\mathbf Y^i$ and $\mathbf Z^i$, evaluate both and set $\tilde{\mathbf X}^i$ to the better one. \Comment{[2 per follower]}
        \EndIf
    \EndFor
    \State Box-repair $\tilde{\mathbf X}$, set $\mathbf X\gets\tilde{\mathbf X}$, evaluate $F_p$ for all salps, sort them, and update $\mathbf H$. \Comment{[$N_p$]}
\EndFor
\State \Return $\mathbf H$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Basic Variable Neighborhood Search}
\label{sec:vns}
The basic VNS~\cite{Mladenovic1997,Hansen2001}, in the continuous form of~\cite{Mladenovic2008} (Algorithm~\ref{alg:vns}), improves one incumbent $\mathbf X$ with $k_{\max}=5$ nested $\ell_\infty$ neighborhoods $\mathcal N_k(\mathbf X)=\{\mathbf X':\delta_{k-1}<\lVert \mathbf X'-\mathbf X\rVert_\infty\le\delta_k\}$, $\delta_k=0.1\,k\,r$, of the whole layout. Shaking draws a uniform point of $\mathcal N_k(\mathbf X)$ (by rejection), clipped to the box, and a best-improvement local search improves it; if the result is better than $\mathbf X$, it becomes the incumbent and $k$ returns to 1, otherwise the next, larger neighborhood is tried. As the top-hat wake makes $F_p$ discontinuous, the local search is a compass search: it evaluates all $2n$ moves $\pm h\,\mathbf e_m$ ($\mathbf e_m$ the $m$th unit vector), moves to the best one if it strictly improves, and halves $h$ (from $h_0=0.05r$) when none does, until $h<h_{\min}=10^{-3}r$. One sweep costs $2n$ evaluations (60 for $N=15$). A completed descent ends at a point that no move $\pm\bar h\,\mathbf e_m$ with $\bar h=h_0/2^5=0.0015625\,r$ improves (Proposition~\ref{S-prop:S-ls}): a coordinate-wise optimality at one step length, not a local minimum in the usual sense. VNS alone starts from the best layout of the seeded initial population. The settings are the defaults of the framework, not tuned; every call counts against the budget, and a run returns the best layout evaluated.
% CHECK-THEORY [T12]: compass search final step h0/2^5 = 0.0015625 r (Proposition S-prop:S-ls); verified: analysis/original_vns.py (k_max = 5, rho_k = 0.1 k r, h0 = 0.05 r, h_min = 1e-3 r; move to the best of the 2 dim moves if it improves the current point, else halve h; shaking by rejection in the l_inf shell, then clip)

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{Basic VNS from an incumbent $\mathbf X$ (Phase~2 of all hybrids; stand-alone VNS starts from the best initial layout)}
\label{alg:vns}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $k_{\max}=5$, $\delta_k=0.1\,k\,r$, $h_0=0.05\,r$, $h_{\min}=10^{-3}r$; the run stops when the budget $B$ is used up
\State $\mathbf X\gets\textsc{LocalSearch}(\mathbf X)$; $k\gets1$
\Loop
    \State Draw $\mathbf d\sim U[-\delta_k,\delta_k]^n$ until $\lVert\mathbf d\rVert_\infty>\delta_{k-1}$; $\mathbf X'\gets\operatorname{clip}(\mathbf X+\mathbf d)$; evaluate $F_p(\mathbf X')$ \Comment{shaking}
    \State $\mathbf X'\gets\textsc{LocalSearch}(\mathbf X')$
    \State \textbf{if} $F_p(\mathbf X')<F_p(\mathbf X)$ \textbf{then} $\mathbf X\gets\mathbf X'$, $k\gets1$ \textbf{else} $k\gets k\bmod k_{\max}+1$
\EndLoop
\Function{LocalSearch}{$\mathbf y$} \Comment{compass search, best improvement}
    \State $h\gets h_0$
    \While{$h\ge h_{\min}$}
        \State Evaluate $\operatorname{clip}(\mathbf y\pm h\,\mathbf e_m)$, $m=1,\ldots,n$, and let $\mathbf y'$ be the best of these $2n$ points
        \State \textbf{if} $F_p(\mathbf y')<F_p(\mathbf y)$ \textbf{then} $\mathbf y\gets\mathbf y'$ \textbf{else} $h\gets h/2$
    \EndWhile
    \State \Return $\mathbf y$
\EndFunction
\State \Return best layout evaluated (when the budget is used up)
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Two-Phase Swarm--VNS Design and PSO-VNS}
\label{sec:hybrid}

\subsection{Two-Phase Template}
\label{sec:template}
All hybrids follow one sequential (relay) template. For a budget of $B$ evaluations and a split $\omega\in(0,1)$, Phase~1 starts from the seeded initial population, runs a population method for about $\omega B$ evaluations and passes its best layout to Phase~2, the basic VNS (Algorithm~\ref{alg:vns}), which applies the local search to it and then iterates until $B$ evaluations are used. Only Phase~1 differs: PSO in \emph{PSO-VNS}; SSA or LX-SSA in \emph{SSA-VNS} and \emph{LX-SSA-VNS} (with $\xi_1$ compressed to the Phase-1 iterations); and, in the controls, $\operatorname{round}(\omega B)$ layouts: the initial population, then layouts drawn uniformly in the farm disc (\emph{RSD-VNS}) or in its bounding square (\emph{RS-VNS}). VNS alone is the limiting case in which Phase~1 is the initial population. PSO and SSA run for $T_1=\operatorname{round}((\omega B-N_p)/N_p)$ Phase-1 iterations, LX-SSA for $\operatorname{round}((\omega B-N_p)/(2N_p))$. A swarm phase is useful only if it gives VNS a better start than random sampling with about the same number of evaluations (at $\omega=0.5$, 3,015 for the controls and $N_p(T_1+1)=3{,}030$ for the swarms); the component analysis (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}) tests this for each Phase-1 choice against RSD-VNS, with RS-VNS as a weak minimum bar, as the following result shows.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:S-box}
Let the $2N$ coordinates be independent and uniform in $[-r,r]$, and let $P_N$ be the probability that the layout is feasible (all turbines in the disc $\mathcal D$ of radius $r$ and all pairwise distances $\ge s$). Then (a) all $N$ turbines lie in $\mathcal D$ with probability $(\pi/4)^N$; (b) $P_N\le(\pi/4)^N\prod_{k=1}^{N-1}\max\bigl\{0,\ 1-\max\{A(s),\,k\,A(s/2)\}/(\pi r^2)\bigr\}$, where, for $0<\rho\le2r$,
\begin{equation}
A(\rho)=r^2\arccos\Bigl(1-\frac{\rho^2}{2r^2}\Bigr)+\rho^2\arccos\frac{\rho}{2r}-\frac{\rho}{2}\sqrt{4r^2-\rho^2}
\label{eq:S-th-lens}
\end{equation}
is the area of the part of $\mathcal D$ covered by a disc of radius $\rho$ centred on the boundary of $\mathcal D$; (c) among $M$ such layouts, the expected number of feasible ones is $MP_N$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) The turbines are independent and uniform in the square, and each lies in $\mathcal D$ with probability $\pi r^2/(2r)^2$. (b) Conditional on all turbines in $\mathcal D$, the positions $\mathbf p_1,\ldots,\mathbf p_N$ are independent and uniform in $\mathcal D$. Given that $\mathbf p_1,\ldots,\mathbf p_k$ are pairwise at least $s$ apart, $\mathbf p_{k+1}$ must avoid $U_k=\mathcal D\cap\bigcup_{j\le k}B(\mathbf p_j,s)$. The area of $B(\mathbf c,\rho)\cap \mathcal D$ for $\mathbf c\in \mathcal D$ is nonincreasing in $\lVert\mathbf c\rVert$ (its derivative with respect to the distance of the centres is minus the length of the common chord), hence at least $A(\rho)$, its value at $\lVert\mathbf c\rVert=r$; so $\lvert U_k\rvert\ge A(s)$, and since the discs $B(\mathbf p_j,s/2)\subset B(\mathbf p_j,s)$ are pairwise disjoint, also $\lvert U_k\rvert\ge kA(s/2)$. The conditional probability that $\mathbf p_{k+1}$ keeps the spacing is thus at most $1-\max\{A(s),kA(s/2)\}/(\pi r^2)$, and the chain rule gives (b). (c) Linearity of expectation.
\end{proof}
The bound (b) is rigorous but loose. Monte Carlo estimates show that for the largest benchmark cases ($N=12$ and 15) a square sample is feasible with probability below $10^{-6}$ (for $N=10$, none of $10^7$ samples is feasible), so the 3,015 samples of RS-VNS contain a feasible layout with probability of order $10^{-3}$ or less (Table~\ref{S-tab:S-th-box}), and RS-VNS hands VNS the least-violating sample rather than one selected for low wake loss. The spacing, not the square, is the main cause: the Monte Carlo values lie orders of magnitude below $(\pi/4)^N$; \NRSRunsNoFeasSample{} RS-VNS runs have no feasible sample, and sampling in the disc gives one in only \NRsSquareExplains{} of them.

\subsection{PSO-VNS}
PSO-VNS (Algorithm~\ref{alg:hybrid}) is not a new PSO variant: its Phase~1 is the plain PSO of Algorithm~\ref{alg:pso} and, as the coefficients do not depend on the iteration, reproduces exactly, for a given seed, the first $N_p(T_1+1)$ evaluations of a PSO run (Proposition~\ref{S-prop:S-prefix}); comparing PSO-VNS with PSO thus isolates the effect of Phase~2. This does not hold for SSA-VNS and LX-SSA-VNS, whose $\xi_1$ depends on $t/T_1$.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{PSO-VNS for a prescribed turbine count $N$}
\label{alg:hybrid}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require budget $B$, split $\omega$, $N_p$, $w$, $c_1$, $c_2$, radius $r$, $k_{\max}$
\State Draw and evaluate $N_p$ particles $\mathbf X^i$; $\mathbf V^i\gets\mathbf 0$; $\mathbf P^i\gets\mathbf X^i$; $\mathbf G\gets\arg\min_i F_p(\mathbf P^i)$ \Comment{Phase 1}
\For{$t=1,\ldots,T_1$ and $i=1,\ldots,N_p$}
    \State Move $\mathbf X^i$ by \eqref{eq:pso-v}, \eqref{eq:pso-x}; box repair; evaluate $F_p(\mathbf X^i)$; update $\mathbf P^i$, $\mathbf G$
\EndFor
\State $\mathbf X\gets\textsc{LocalSearch}(\mathbf G)$; $k\gets1$ \Comment{Phase 2}
\While{fewer than $B$ evaluations have been used}
    \State $\mathbf X'\gets\textsc{LocalSearch}(\text{uniform point of }\mathcal N_k(\mathbf X))$
    \State \textbf{if} $F_p(\mathbf X')<F_p(\mathbf X)$ \textbf{then} $\mathbf X\gets \mathbf X'$, $k\gets1$ \textbf{else} $k\gets k\bmod k_{\max}+1$
\EndWhile
\State \Return best layout evaluated
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


PSO-VNS uses $N_p=30$, the coefficients \eqref{eq:constriction} and the VNS settings above, and adds one parameter, the split $\omega$. With the default $\omega=0.5$ and $B=6{,}030$, PSO runs for $T_1=100$ iterations (99.5 rounded half to even) and VNS receives the remaining 3,000 evaluations. None of these values was tuned; Section~\ref{sec:split} studies $\omega$. At this budget, PSO-VNS is essentially PSO plus one compass-search descent: the first descent is unfinished in \NDDescentUnfinishedLargePSOVNS{} of \NDRunsLargePSOVNS{} runs with $N\ge12$, and shaking-plus-search cycles take \NDCycleEvalsPctPSOVNS\% of the Phase-2 evaluations (pooled; median run \NDCycleEvalsPctMedianPSOVNS\%) for \NDCycleSharePSOVNS\% of the Phase-2 gain (accepted cycles), so the neighborhoods matter only for small $N$.

\subsection{Settings of All Methods}
\label{sec:params}
Table~\ref{tab:params} lists the settings of all methods of this study. Every method uses $N_p=30$ where applicable, the same seeded initial population, the same box repair and, except MS-SLSQP, the same objective $F_p$; the coefficients are the published defaults of each method (PSO~\cite{Clerc2002}, SSA~\cite{Mirjalili2017}, LX-SSA~\cite{Solanki2023}, DE~\cite{Storn1997}, VNS~\cite{Mladenovic2008}), not tuned on the benchmark. DE/rand/1/bin clips the mutant to the box before the binomial crossover and replaces a member as soon as its trial is better (asynchronous one-to-one selection). MS-SLSQP (SciPy's SLSQP~\cite{Kraft1988}) minimizes the wake loss under the explicit constraints of~\eqref{eq:wflop}, scaled as $(\ell_{ij}^2-\ell_{\min}^2)/\ell_{\min}^2\ge0$ and $(r^2-x_i^2-y_i^2)/r^2\ge0$, with forward-difference gradients (step 1~m, every call counted); it starts from the 30 initial layouts in increasing order of $F_p$ and then from new random layouts until the budget is used, and returns the best evaluated layout whose scaled constraints are all $\ge-10^{-9}$ (details: Section~\ref{S-sec:S-slsqp}). For the larger budgets of Section~\ref{sec:budget} (30,030 and 120,030 evaluations), the iteration counts scale as $(B-N_p)/N_p$ ($2N_p$ in the denominator for LX-SSA), and the split rule of Section~\ref{sec:template} applies unchanged.
