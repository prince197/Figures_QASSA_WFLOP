The variants of Table~\ref{tab:ablation} combine a Phase~1 (PSO, SSA, LX-SSA, random sampling or none) with or without the same VNS phase (6,030 evaluations, same seeds; convergence: Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation}); their average ranks differ (Friedman $p=\NAblP$). Here $p$ is from the Wilcoxon test on the 68 case means (unadjusted; Holm over the \NAblNContrasts{} contrasts in Table~\ref{tab:ablation}) and $\overline{\Delta L}$ is their mean difference (percentage points, pp); it is practically equivalent to zero if its 90\% bootstrap interval (in brackets) lies within $\pm\NEqMargin$~pp.

\emph{VNS phase.} The VNS phase significantly improves both salp-swarm methods: SSA-VNS is \NAblSSAVNSvsSSA{} (W/T/L) against SSA and LX-SSA-VNS \NAblLXSSAVNSvsLXSSA{} against LX-SSA ($\overline{\Delta L}=\NAblSSAVNSvsSSADL$ and \NAblLXSSAVNSvsLXSSADL). The PSO contrast is clean, as PSO-VNS and PSO share their first 3,030 evaluations (Section~\ref{sec:hybrid}): switching to VNS gives a small gain ($\overline{\Delta L}=\NAblPSOVNSvsPSODL$, $p=\NPWPSO$) that is practically equivalent to zero (\NEqPSOVNSvsPSOCI). At this budget the VNS phase is essentially one compass-search descent: for PSO-VNS, the first descent yields \NDDescentSharePSOVNS\% of the Phase-2 gain, shaking uses \NDShakeEvalsPctPSOVNS\% of the evaluations, and the median run with $N\ge12$ never shakes (Table~\ref{S-tab:D-vns}). The analysis thus measures the value of a local-search phase; the VNS neighborhoods can matter only for small $N$.

\emph{Swarm phase.} Three controls isolate Phase~1: VNS alone and random sampling of equal cost, uniform in the site disc (RSD-VNS) or in its bounding square (RS-VNS). RS-VNS is weak: a square sample has all $N$ turbines inside the circle only with probability $(\pi/4)^N$, so \NDRsRunsNoFeasSample{} of its 2,040 runs draw no feasible sample (RSD-VNS: \NRSDRunsNoFeasSample); RSD-VNS beats it (\NAblRSDVNSvsRSVNS, $\overline{\Delta L}=\NCmRSDVNSvsRSVNSDL$) and ranks third (\NRankRSDVNS). A PSO phase clearly beats every control: PSO-VNS is \NAblPSOVNSvsRSDVNS{} against RSD-VNS ($\overline{\Delta L}=\NCmPSOVNSvsRSDVNSDL$), \NAblPSOVNSvsRSVNS{} against RS-VNS (\NCmPSOVNSvsRSVNSDL) and \NAblPSOVNSvsVNS{} against VNS. Salp-swarm phases add nothing beyond disc sampling: against RSD-VNS, SSA-VNS is \NAblSSAVNSvsRSDVNS{} and slightly worse on the case means ($\overline{\Delta L}=\NCmSSAVNSvsRSDVNSDL$, $p=\NCmSSAVNSvsRSDVNSP$), and LX-SSA-VNS is \NAblLXSSAVNSvsRSDVNS{} and clearly worse (\NCmLXSSAVNSvsRSDVNSDL, $p=\NCmLXSSAVNSvsRSDVNSP$). The small SSA gain over RS-VNS (\NAblSSAVNSvsRSVNS, \NCmSSAVNSvsRSVNSDL, $p=\NCmSSAVNSvsRSVNSP$) thus only reflects that control's weakness; LX-SSA-VNS and RS-VNS are practically equivalent (\NEqLXSSAVNSvsRSVNSCI).

\emph{Laplace step.} The Laplace step makes the salp swarm worse. Without VNS, LX-SSA is \NAblLXSSAvsSSA{} against SSA ($\overline{\Delta L}=\NAblLXSSAvsSSADL$, $p=\NCmLXSSAvsSSAP$); in the hybrid, SSA-VNS is \NAblSSAVNSvsLXSSAVNS{} against LX-SSA-VNS and better on the case means ($\overline{\Delta L}=\NCmSSAVNSvsLXSSAVNSDL$, $p=\NCmSSAVNSvsLXSSAVNSP$). As LX-SSA costs $2N_p$ evaluations per iteration (Section~\ref{sec:ssa}) and so runs half as many iterations as SSA, these contrasts do not separate the Laplace step from this cost.
% CHECK-FINAL [C17]: Laplace step harmful: LXSSA-SSA W == 0; SSABV-LXBV L == 0; case means favour SSA and SSA-VNS
% verified 2026-09-28 from mpce_ablation_tests.csv: LXSSA-SSA case means 9 lower / 53 higher, p = 8.3e-8; SSABV-LXBV 47 lower / 13 higher, p = 5.4e-6

\emph{Choice of Phase~1.} With the same VNS phase, PSO-VNS is \NAblPSOVNSvsSSAVNS{} against SSA-VNS and \NAblPSOVNSvsLXSSAVNS{} against LX-SSA-VNS. At the switch, its global best is feasible more often (\NSwitchFeasPSOVNS\% of the runs vs.\ \NSwitchFeasSSAVNS\% and \NSwitchFeasLXSSAVNS\%) and, when feasible, has a lower wake loss (\NSwitchLossPSOVNS\% vs.\ \NSwitchLossSSAVNS\% and \NSwitchLossLXSSAVNS\%), so VNS starts from a better layout. Whether PSO helps mainly by faster feasibility or by better exploration remains open.

\emph{Budget split.}\label{sec:split} Table~\ref{tab:split} compares the preset split $\omega=0.5$, used for all other results, with $\omega=0.25$, $0.75$, $0.9$ and $\omega=1$ (plain PSO) on the \NSplitCases{} cases with the middle and the largest $N$ of each farm (per case: Table~\ref{S-tab:split-cases}). With $\omega=0.75$, the mean wake loss is lower than with $\omega=0.5$ in \NSplitSeventyFiveLower{} cases (significantly in \NSplitSeventyFiveSig; \NSplitGainSeventyFive{}~pp on average), and significantly higher in \NSplitFiftyBetterSeventyFive{} case; $\omega=0.25$ is significantly worse than the default in \NSplitTwentyFiveSig{} cases and better in \NSplitTwentyFiveBetter{} case. $\omega=0.9$ does not beat $\omega=0.75$ (W/T/L of $\omega=0.75$: \NSplitSeventyFiveVsNinety, case-mean $p=\NSplitCmNinetyP$; mean loss \NSplitLossNinety\%, rank \NSplitRankNinety). Plain PSO has a higher mean loss than $\omega=0.75$ (\NSplitLossHundred\% vs.\ \NSplitLossSeventyFive\%, rank \NSplitRankHundred; W/T/L of $\omega=0.75$: \NSplitHundredVsSeventyFive), so the best tested split is not the PSO end point. Chosen on \NSplitCases{} cases only, a split above 0.5 is a candidate to be confirmed on the target problem; we do not re-tune $\omega$ for the main comparison, which may slightly understate PSO-VNS.
