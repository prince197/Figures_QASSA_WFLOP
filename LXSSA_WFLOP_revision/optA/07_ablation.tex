\label{sec:ablation}

The eight variants of Table~\ref{tab:ablation} combine a Phase~1 (PSO, SSA, LX-SSA, random sampling or none) with or without the same VNS phase, at 6,030 evaluations and with the same seeds (convergence in Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation}); their average ranks differ (Friedman $\chi^2_F=\NAblChi$, 7 d.f., $p=\NAblP$). Holm's correction runs over the eleven contrasts of a case, not the seven comparisons of Table~\ref{tab:wtl}, so PSO-VNS vs.\ PSO is \NAblPSOVNSvsPSO{} here and \NWtlPSO{} there. Case-level $p$ values are from the Wilcoxon test on the 68 case means (Section~\ref{sec:setup}); $\overline{\Delta L}$ is the mean difference of the case means (percentage points), with its 95\% bootstrap interval over the cases.

\emph{VNS phase.} The VNS phase significantly improves both salp-swarm methods: SSA-VNS is \NAblSSAVNSvsSSA{} (W/T/L) against SSA and LX-SSA-VNS \NAblLXSSAVNSvsLXSSA{} against LX-SSA ($\overline{\Delta L}=\NAblSSAVNSvsSSADL$ and \NAblLXSSAVNSvsLXSSADL; these contrasts also compress the $\xi_1$ schedule into Phase~1). The PSO contrast is clean, since PSO-VNS and PSO share their first 3,030 evaluations (Section~\ref{sec:hybrid}): switching to VNS instead of continuing the swarm gives a small gain ($\overline{\Delta L}=\NAblPSOVNSvsPSODL$) that is not significant over all 68 cases ($p=\NPWPSO$; Section~\ref{sec:overall}).

\emph{Swarm phase.} Two controls isolate Phase~1: VNS alone, and RS-VNS, whose Phase~1 samples uniform random layouts in the bounding square (3,015 evaluations, about as many as the 3,030 of the swarms). RS-VNS is a weak, no-search control: all $N$ turbines of a uniform sample lie inside the circular site only with probability $(\pi/4)^N$, so its best sample is often just the least infeasible one (feasible at the switch in \NSwitchFeasRSVNS\% of the runs), and beating RS-VNS is a minimum requirement for a swarm phase. A PSO phase clearly meets it: PSO-VNS is \NAblPSOVNSvsRSVNS{} against RS-VNS ($\overline{\Delta L}=\NCmPSOVNSvsRSVNSDL$ \NCmPSOVNSvsRSVNSCI) and \NAblPSOVNSvsVNS{} against VNS ($\overline{\Delta L}=\NAblPSOVNSvsVNSDL$). An SSA phase gives only a small gain: SSA-VNS and RS-VNS are mostly tied at the run level (\NAblSSAVNSvsRSVNS), and on the case means SSA-VNS is better by far less than PSO-VNS ($\overline{\Delta L}=\NCmSSAVNSvsRSVNSDL$ \NCmSSAVNSvsRSVNSCI, $p=\NCmSSAVNSvsRSVNSP$). An LX-SSA phase gives no gain (LX-SSA-VNS vs.\ RS-VNS: \NAblLXSSAVNSvsRSVNS, $p=\NCmLXSSAVNSvsRSVNSP$).

\emph{Laplace step.} The Laplace step makes the salp swarm worse. Without VNS, LX-SSA is \NAblLXSSAvsSSA{} against SSA ($\overline{\Delta L}=\NAblLXSSAvsSSADL$, $p=\NCmLXSSAvsSSAP$); in the hybrid, SSA-VNS is \NAblSSAVNSvsLXSSAVNS{} against LX-SSA-VNS and better on the case means ($\overline{\Delta L}=\NCmSSAVNSvsLXSSAVNSDL$, $p=\NCmSSAVNSvsLXSSAVNSP$). As published, LX-SSA costs $2N_p$ evaluations per iteration (Section~\ref{sec:ssa}) and so runs half as many iterations as SSA; these contrasts do not separate the Laplace step from this cost.
% CHECK-FINAL [C17]: Laplace step harmful: LXSSA-SSA W == 0; SSABV-LXBV L == 0; case means favour SSA and SSA-VNS
% verified 2026-09-28 from mpce_ablation_tests.csv: LXSSA-SSA case means 9 lower / 53 higher, p = 8.3e-8; SSABV-LXBV 47 lower / 13 higher, p = 5.4e-6

\emph{Choice of Phase~1.} With the same VNS phase, PSO-VNS is \NAblPSOVNSvsSSAVNS{} against SSA-VNS and \NAblPSOVNSvsLXSSAVNS{} against LX-SSA-VNS. At the switch, its global best is feasible more often (\NSwitchFeasPSOVNS\% of the runs vs.\ \NSwitchFeasSSAVNS\% and \NSwitchFeasLXSSAVNS\%) and, when feasible, has a lower wake loss (\NSwitchLossPSOVNS\% vs.\ \NSwitchLossSSAVNS\% and \NSwitchLossLXSSAVNS\%), so VNS starts from a better layout. These data do not show whether PSO helps mainly by reaching feasibility sooner or by exploring the wake-loss landscape better.

\emph{Budget split.}\label{sec:split} Table~\ref{tab:split} compares the split $\omega=0.5$, fixed before the experiments and used for all other results, with $\omega=0.25$, $0.75$ and the end point $\omega=1$ (plain PSO) on the \NSplitCases{} cases with the middle and the largest $N$ of each farm (per-case values: Table~\ref{S-tab:split-cases} of the supplementary material). With $\omega=0.75$, the mean wake loss is lower than with $\omega=0.5$ in \NSplitSeventyFiveLower{} of the \NSplitCases{} cases, significantly in \NSplitSeventyFiveSig, and \NSplitGainSeventyFive{} percentage points lower on average, and the default is significantly better in \NSplitFiftyBetterSeventyFive{} case; $\omega=0.25$ is significantly worse than the default in \NSplitTwentyFiveSig{} cases and significantly better in \NSplitTwentyFiveBetter{} case. Plain PSO has a higher mean loss than $\omega=0.75$ (\NSplitLossHundred\% vs.\ \NSplitLossSeventyFive\%, average rank \NSplitRankHundred; $\omega=0.75$ against it: \NSplitHundredVsSeventyFive), so the best tested split is not the PSO end point. As $\omega=0.75$ is only the best of four values on \NSplitCases{} test cases, it is a candidate setting to be confirmed on the target problem; we do not re-tune $\omega$ for the main comparison, which may slightly understate PSO-VNS.
