\section{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Cases and Methods}
The 68 cases combine the two Kusiak--Song wind data sets~\cite{Kusiak2010,Bansal2017} (Table~\ref{S-tab:S-wind}) with circular farms of radii $r=500$, 750 and 1000~m and $N=2,\ldots,10$, $2,\ldots,12$ and $2,\ldots,15$ turbines ($R=38.5$~m, $\ell_{\min}=4D=308$~m, $C_T=0.8$, $K=0.075$).

The \emph{main comparison} includes eight methods ($N_p=30$ where applicable): PSO-VNS (Algorithm~\ref{alg:hybrid}, $\omega=0.5$); PSO; SSA-VNS; SSA; LX-SSA; DE/rand/1/bin~\cite{Storn1997} (scale factor 0.5, crossover rate 0.9); VNS; and MS-SLSQP, a multistart sequential least-squares programming method (SciPy's SLSQP~\cite{Kraft1988}, forward-difference gradients) that minimizes the wake loss under explicit constraints, restarting from the initial, then random layouts (Section~\ref{S-sec:S-protocol}); all others minimize $F_p$~\eqref{eq:penalty} over $[-r,r]^{2N}$. The \emph{component analysis} (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}) adds LX-SSA-VNS and two random-sampling controls (Section~\ref{sec:template}; RSD-VNS on the benchmark only): after the common initial population, \emph{RSD-VNS} samples Phase~1 uniformly in the farm disc and \emph{RS-VNS} in the bounding square, 3,015 layouts versus the hybrids' 3,030 Phase-1 evaluations. A split study varies $\omega$ on \NSplitCases{} cases (Section~\ref{sec:split}).

\subsection{Comparison Design and Statistics}
\emph{Equal budgets and paired seeds.} Unless stated otherwise, a run uses exactly $B=6{,}030$ objective evaluations, including the initial population and MS-SLSQP's gradient evaluations. Each method uses seeds 1--30 and draws its initial population first, so runs with equal seeds share it and are paired.
% verified 2026-09-28: first Curve value identical across fresh_grid/vgrid/bgrid and mpce_psoc/psobv/rsvns/slsqp (13 files) for all 792 case-seed pairs with a feasible initial best (else NaN)

\emph{Feasibility-aware ranking.} Within a case, a method is ranked by the mean benchmark objective of its feasible runs only if at least 15 of its 30 runs (5 of 10 for 10 seeds) are feasible, and otherwise below all such methods, by its number of feasible runs. This limits, but does not remove, survivorship bias.

\emph{Statistics.} All tests are two-sided ($\alpha=0.05$), with Holm's correction~\cite{Holm1979} within each family of comparisons. 1) Case ranks: the Friedman test with the Iman--Davenport F test and post hoc tests of the average ranks against PSO-VNS~\cite{Demsar2006,Derrac2011,LaTorre2021}. 2) Case means (primary, as mean-rank post hoc tests depend on the pool~\cite{Benavoli2016}): Wilcoxon signed-rank tests of PSO-VNS against each method on the per-case mean wake losses, with Hodges--Lehmann estimates for key pairs (Table~\ref{S-tab:X-hl}); a case in which only one of the two methods has at least 15 feasible runs counts as a maximal difference in its favor (cases: \NCmImputedPhrase), and one in which neither has is dropped (\NCmDropped{} cases). Mean differences (both qualified) carry 95\% bootstrap CIs. 3) Runs: per case, Holm-corrected Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on the 30 seed-paired runs (infeasible runs last, ordered by minimum spacing), with the rank-biserial correlation $r_{\rm rb}$ and counts of significant wins, ties and losses (W/T/L). 4) Equivalence~\cite{Lakens2017}: TOST (90\% interval of the mean case-mean difference within $\pm m$) at three levels (Table~\ref{S-tab:X-equiv-levels}): \emph{seed level}, cases fixed and runs resampled within them (estimand: the benchmark-average difference); \emph{case level}, cases resampled (the mean over similar cases); \emph{cluster level}, new farm clusters (CR2 interval, 5 d.f.; wild-cluster bootstrap-$t$, Webb weights). The margin $m=\NEqMargin$~pp (\NXMarginMWhTurbDsI{} and \NXMarginMWhTurbDsII~MWh/yr per turbine in Data Sets I and II) is a post hoc convention; the minimal margins $m_{\min}$ let readers apply their own. A Bayesian signed-rank test with this region~\cite{Benavoli2017} gives the posterior probability that equivalence is the most probable of three outcomes (Table~\ref{S-tab:equivalence}).

\emph{Robustness of inference.} The 68 cases form \NXClusters{} (data set, radius) clusters with nested $N$. Six qualification thresholds (\NXThrList{} feasible runs) change no verdict; cluster analyses (sign per cluster, cluster-robust $t$, cluster bootstrap, leave-one-out) qualify only the significant contrasts of the salp-swarm hybrids with the controls; one Holm family over all \NXMultN{} case-mean tests makes only \NXMultLostHolm{} nonsignificant (Tables~\ref{S-tab:X-threshold}--\ref{S-tab:X-multiplicity}); Section~\ref{sec:robustness} adds a 1\textdegree{} direction resolution.

\subsection{Tests Beyond the Benchmark and Reproducibility}
\label{sec:setup-beyond}
\label{sec:validation}
\emph{Initialization and budget.} The feasible initialization draws seeded layouts inside the site and separates the turbines with L-BFGS-B on a packing penalty, without objective evaluations (Section~\ref{S-sec:S-protocol}); all methods but RS-VNS use it at 6,030 evaluations. With random starts, all ten run at 6,030, 30,030 and 120,030 evaluations. Both studies use the six largest cases (largest $N$ per farm and data set) and the Horns Rev~1 block, with 30 seeds (10 for Horns Rev~1 at 120,030).

\emph{Other sites and models.} The Horns Rev~1 block is PyWake's 16-turbine example~\cite{PyWake}: V80 turbines ($D=80$~m), the measured 12-sector wind climate, the parallelogram through the corner turbines as boundary (Section~\ref{sec:hornsrev-site}), $\ell_{\min}=4D$ and the Jensen wake with $K=0.04$ and $C_T$ at the free-stream speed.

The 16- and 36-turbine scenarios of IEA37 Case Study~1~\cite{Baker2019,IEA37repo} (circular sites, radii 1300 and 2000~m, $\ell_{\min}=2D$, 16 directions at one speed, a simplified Bastankhah Gaussian wake) test optimizers rather than represent a site; the eight methods of the main comparison run on them at 6,030 and 30,030 evaluations (30 seeds).

\emph{Verification and code.} Our objective matches the original implementation (largest difference $8.7\times10^{-11}$ over 720 archived final objectives) and the official IEA37 calculator (relative difference ${<}10^{-11}$); our reused SSA, LX-SSA, PSO and DE code reproduces the recorded result distributions of the original code (2 of 24 Mann--Whitney tests reject at $\alpha=0.05$; 1.2 expected), and instrumented copies reproduce the stored runs (\NDVerStoredMatch{} of \NDVerRuns). Runs: Linux containers (Intel Xeon; Python~3.11.15, NumPy~2.4.6, SciPy~1.17.1); code, per-run results and scripts are public (Data Availability).
