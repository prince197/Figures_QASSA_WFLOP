\section{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Cases and Methods}
The 68 benchmark cases combine the two wind data sets of the Kusiak--Song benchmark~\cite{Kusiak2010,Bansal2017} (Table~\ref{S-tab:S-wind} of the supplementary material) with circular farms of radius $r=500$, 750 and 1000~m and $N=2,\ldots,10$, $2,\ldots,12$ and $2,\ldots,15$ turbines ($R=38.5$~m, $\ell_{\min}=4D=308$~m, $C_T=0.8$, $K=0.075$).

The \emph{main comparison} includes eight methods ($N_p=30$ where applicable): PSO-VNS (Algorithm~\ref{alg:hybrid}, $\omega=0.5$); PSO; SSA-VNS; SSA; LX-SSA; DE/rand/1/bin~\cite{Storn1997} (scale factor 0.5, crossover rate 0.9); VNS; and MS-SLSQP, a multistart sequential least-squares programming method (SciPy's SLSQP~\cite{Kraft1988}, forward-difference gradients). All methods except MS-SLSQP minimize $F_p$~\eqref{eq:penalty} over $[-r,r]^{2N}$; MS-SLSQP minimizes the wake loss under explicit constraints, restarting from the initial layouts and then from random ones, and returns its best feasible layout (settings: Section~\ref{S-sec:S-protocol}). DE and MS-SLSQP use standard, untuned settings. The \emph{component analysis} (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}) adds LX-SSA-VNS and the no-search control RS-VNS (Section~\ref{sec:template}), whose 3,015 random layouts cost about as much as the 3,030 Phase-1 evaluations of the hybrids; a split study varies $\omega$ on \NSplitCases{} cases (Section~\ref{sec:split}).

\subsection{Comparison Design and Statistics}
\emph{Equal budgets and paired seeds.} Every run on the 68 cases uses exactly $B=6{,}030$ objective evaluations, counted by a wrapper around the objective, including the initial population and the gradient evaluations of MS-SLSQP. Each method uses the seeds 1--30 and draws its initial population first, so runs with the same seed start from the same population and are paired (where logged, the best initial objective agrees across all methods, including the earlier version's runs).

\emph{Feasibility-aware ranking.} A final layout counts as feasible if all spacing and boundary constraints hold within $10^{-6}$~m. Within a case, a method is ranked by the mean benchmark objective of its feasible runs only if at least 15 of its 30 runs (5 of 10 for 10 seeds) are feasible, and otherwise below all such methods, by its number of feasible runs. This limits, but does not remove, survivorship bias: a method with 15 to 29 feasible runs is still ranked by its surviving runs.

\emph{Statistics.} All tests are two-sided ($\alpha=0.05$), with Holm's correction~\cite{Holm1979} within each family of comparisons. 1) Case ranks: the Friedman test with the Iman--Davenport F test and post hoc tests of the average ranks against PSO-VNS~\cite{Demsar2006}. 2) Case means (primary tests): since mean-rank post hoc tests depend on the pool~\cite{Benavoli2016}, Wilcoxon signed-rank tests of PSO-VNS against each method on the per-case mean wake losses; a case in which only one of the two methods has at least 15 feasible runs counts as a maximal difference in its favor (\NCmImputed{} cases), and one in which neither has is dropped (\NCmDropped{} cases). Headline mean differences of case means (cases in which both qualify) carry 95\% bootstrap confidence intervals over cases. 3) Runs: per case, Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on the 30 seed-paired runs (infeasible runs below all feasible ones, ordered by minimum spacing), Holm-corrected over the comparisons of the case, with the matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation $r_{\rm rb}$ (zero differences dropped). The counts of significant wins, ties and significant losses (W/T/L) are descriptive: Holm acts only within a case, so a few significant cases can arise by chance, and a pair's counts differ between tables with different families. The 68 cases are six site--wind clusters (data set, radius) with nested $N$, not independent draws; the p values describe this benchmark, not a population of farms, and subgroup splits are exploratory.

\subsection{Tests Beyond the Benchmark and Reproducibility}
\label{sec:setup-beyond}
\label{sec:validation}
\emph{Initialization and budget.} Random initial layouts are usually infeasible; the feasible initialization draws the initial layouts inside the site from the seeded generator and separates the turbines with L-BFGS-B on a packing penalty, without objective evaluations (Section~\ref{S-sec:S-protocol}). The nine methods other than RS-VNS, which would need a packing solve per random layout, use it at 6,030 evaluations; with random initialization, all ten methods run at 6,030, 30,030 and 120,030 evaluations. Both studies use the six largest cases (largest $N$ of each farm and data set) and the Horns Rev~1 block, with 30 seeds (10 for Horns Rev~1 at 120,030 evaluations).

\emph{Other sites and models.} The Horns Rev~1 block is PyWake's 16-turbine example~\cite{PyWake}: V80 turbines ($D=80$~m), the measured 12-sector wind climate, the parallelogram through the corner turbines as boundary (Section~\ref{sec:hornsrev-site}), $\ell_{\min}=4D$ and the Jensen wake with $K=0.04$.

The 16- and 36-turbine scenarios of IEA37 Case Study~1~\cite{Baker2019,IEA37repo} (circular sites of radius 1300 and 2000~m, $\ell_{\min}=2D$, 16 directions at one speed, a simplified Bastankhah Gaussian wake) test optimizers rather than represent a site; all ten methods run on them at 6,030 and 30,030 evaluations (30 seeds), and their layouts are compared with the submitted ones.

\emph{Verification and code.} Our objective matches the original implementation (largest absolute difference $8.7\times10^{-11}$ objective units over 720 archived final objectives) and the official IEA37 calculator (relative difference below $10^{-11}$). Our reused code of SSA, LX-SSA, PSO and DE (also in the hybrids) reproduces the result distributions of the earlier version (2 of 24 Mann--Whitney tests reject at $\alpha=0.05$, about the 1.2 expected by chance). The runs used Linux containers (Intel Xeon, 2.1~GHz, 4 virtual cores; Python~3.11.15, NumPy~2.4.6, SciPy~1.17.1); code, per-run results and scripts are public (Data Availability).
