\section{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:setup}
A comparison of metaheuristics shows differences between search strategies only if the methods differ in nothing else. The protocol below fixes the cases, budgets, starting points, ranking rule and tests for every method; it is a contribution of this paper in its own right, and its code and data are public (Section~\ref{sec:validation}).

\subsection{Benchmark Cases and Methods}
We use the two wind data sets of the Kusiak--Song benchmark~\cite{Kusiak2010,Bansal2017} (Table~\ref{S-tab:S-wind} of the supplementary material). Wind Data Set~I has the same Weibull parameters in all directions ($\zeta=2$, $\psi=13$) and a strongly directional wind (probabilities 0.2 and 0.6 for the bins $[75^\circ,90^\circ)$ and $[90^\circ,105^\circ)$); Wind Data Set~II keeps $\zeta=2$, but $\psi$ varies from 2.6 to 10~m/s and the wind is spread over more directions. With $N_\theta=24$ direction bins of $15^\circ$ and $N_s=21$ speed intervals of 0.5~m/s, each data set is combined with circular farms of radius $r=500$, 750 and 1000~m and $N=2,\ldots,10$, $2,\ldots,12$ and $2,\ldots,15$ turbines, which gives $2\times(9+11+14)=68$ cases ($R=38.5$~m, $\ell_{\min}=4D=308$~m, $C_T=0.8$, $K=0.075$).

All methods minimize $F_p$~\eqref{eq:penalty} within the bounds $[-r,r]^{2N}$, with population $N_p=30$ where applicable. The \emph{main comparison} includes eight methods: PSO-VNS (Algorithm~\ref{alg:hybrid}, $\omega=0.5$: 100 PSO iterations, i.e., 3,030 calls, then 3,000 calls of VNS); PSO~\cite{Kennedy1995,Clerc2002} with~\eqref{eq:constriction} and 200 iterations; SSA-VNS, i.e., Algorithm~\ref{alg:hybrid} with 100 SSA iterations in Phase~1 ($\xi_1$ decreasing over these iterations); SSA~\cite{Mirjalili2017} (200 iterations); LX-SSA~\cite{Solanki2023} ($\phi=0$, $\chi=1$, 100 iterations); DE/rand/1/bin~\cite{Storn1997} (scale factor 0.5, crossover rate 0.9, 200 iterations); VNS, i.e., Phase~2 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:hybrid} started from the best initial layout; and multistart SLSQP (MS-SLSQP), SciPy's SLSQP~\cite{Kraft1988} with explicit spacing and boundary constraints and forward-difference gradients (1-m step), started from the initial layouts in the order of their $F_p$ values and restarted until the budget is used up. The \emph{component analysis} (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}) adds two Phase-1 alternatives: RS-VNS, a random-sampling control that spends the Phase-1 calls on $\operatorname{round}(\omega B)=3{,}015$ uniform random layouts (including the initial population) and refines the best one by the same VNS, and LX-SSA-VNS, with 50 LX-SSA iterations (3,030 calls) in Phase~1; it also varies $\omega$ on \NSplitCases{} cases (Section~\ref{sec:split}). The \emph{baseline analysis} (Table~\ref{tab:baseline}) reuses the runs of PSO with $w=0.7$, $c_1=c_2=2$ from the earlier version of this study, made on the same cases with the same budget, seeds and evaluator, and ranks the pool LX-SSA-VNS, SSA-VNS, LX-SSA, SSA, PSO, DE, VNS and MS-SLSQP once with this setting and once with~\eqref{eq:constriction}. SSA, LX-SSA, PSO and DE use our original code of that version; VNS, RS-VNS, MS-SLSQP and the three hybrids were implemented for this study, and the hybrids call the original swarm code in Phase~1.

\subsection{Controlled Comparison}
\emph{Equal budgets.} Every run of the main comparison and the component analysis has a budget of exactly $B=6{,}030$ objective calls, counted by a wrapper around the objective function. Every call counts, including the initial population and, for MS-SLSQP, the calls of the finite-difference gradients, and all iteration counts follow from the budget.

\emph{Seed pairing.} Each method is run with the seeds 1--30. Every optimizer seeds its random-number generator and draws its initial population first, so runs with the same seed start from the \emph{same} initial population for all methods and are paired; differences between methods thus do not include differences between starting points.

\emph{Feasibility-aware ranking.} A layout is feasible if the spacing and boundary constraints hold within $10^{-6}$~m. Within a case, a method is ranked by the mean benchmark objective of its feasible runs only if at least 15 of its 30 runs (5 of 10) are feasible; methods with fewer feasible runs are ranked below all others, by their number of feasible runs. Ranking by the feasible mean alone would reward a method that is feasible in only a few favorable runs.

\subsection{Statistical Analysis}
For each run we record the final benchmark objective, the wake loss (in percent of the wake-free objective), feasibility, the convergence curve (best feasible objective at 201 equally spaced checkpoints) and the wall-clock time; means and standard deviations are taken over the feasible runs. We test at three levels ($\alpha=0.05$). 1) \emph{Case level}: a Friedman test with the Iman--Davenport correction on the ranks in the 68 cases and Holm-adjusted post hoc tests of the average ranks against PSO-VNS~\cite{Holm1979,Demsar2006}. 2) \emph{Case means}: because the outcome of a mean-rank post hoc test between two methods depends on the other methods in the pool~\cite{Benavoli2016}, we also compare PSO-VNS with each method by two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on the 68 per-case mean wake losses, Holm-adjusted over the methods. 3) \emph{Run level}: within each case, the 30 seed-paired runs of PSO-VNS and of each other method are compared by two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, Holm-adjusted over the comparisons of the case, with every infeasible run ranked below every feasible run; we count the cases with a significant win, no significant difference and a significant loss (W/T/L) and report the matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation $r_{\rm rb}$ as effect size. The component analysis uses the same run-level tests for planned contrasts (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}), Holm-adjusted over the contrasts of each case.

\subsection{Tests Beyond the Benchmark}
\label{sec:setup-beyond}
\emph{Initialization and budget.} In the main comparison, the initial layouts are drawn uniformly in the bounding square and are usually infeasible. In the feasible initialization, each initial layout is drawn inside the site and pushed apart by minimizing a smooth packing penalty with L-BFGS-B until the constraints hold, without objective calls. The nine methods other than RS-VNS are run with it at 6,030 calls; RS-VNS is not, because it would need one packing solve for each of its 3,015 random layouts per run, which costs far more than the objective calls themselves. With random initialization, all ten methods are run at 6,030, 30,030 and 120,030 calls, with all iteration counts scaled to the budget (e.g., 500 PSO iterations in PSO-VNS at 30,030 calls). Both studies use the six largest cases (the largest $N$ of each farm and data set) and the Horns Rev~1 block, with 30 seeds (10 for Horns Rev~1 at 120,030 calls).

\emph{Horns Rev~1 block.} We take the Horns Rev~1 site from PyWake~\cite{PyWake}: Vestas V80 turbines (80-m rotor, 2~MW) with tabulated power and thrust curves and a 25-m/s cut-out, the measured 12-sector wind climate, and PyWake's 16-turbine example block, with the parallelogram through its corner turbines as boundary and a minimum spacing of $4D=320$~m; the Jensen wake with $K=0.04$ gives an AEP within 0.3\% of PyWake's.

\emph{IEA37 Case Study~1.} This case study of IEA Wind Task~37~\cite{Baker2019,IEA37repo} fixes the model and leaves the optimizer free. We use its 16- and 36-turbine scenarios (circular boundaries of radius 1300 and 2000~m, minimum spacing $2D=260$~m, IEA 3.35-MW turbine with $D=130$~m, 16 wind directions at a constant 9.8~m/s, simplified Bastankhah Gaussian wake); our implementation reproduces the official AEP calculator to a relative deviation below $10^{-11}$. All ten methods minimize the penalty~\eqref{eq:penalty} applied to the AEP loss at 6,030 and 30,030 calls with 30 seeds, and their layouts are compared with the submitted ones.

\emph{Robustness re-evaluation.} Every feasible final layout of the main comparison is re-evaluated, without re-optimization, with a cubic power curve (with and without cut-out) and a Gaussian wake model (Section~\ref{sec:robustness}); the rankings are compared with the benchmark ranking by the mean Kendall correlation $\bar\tau$ within a case and the share of cases with the same best method.

\subsection{Verification, Environment and Code}
\label{sec:validation}
Our vectorized objective agrees with the original implementation within a relative difference of $10^{-6}$ and reproduces the objective values of 720 archived final layouts within $8.7\times10^{-11}$; re-running the original SSA, LX-SSA, PSO and DE code with the archived settings reproduces the archived result distributions (22 of 24 Mann--Whitney comparisons with $p\ge0.05$). All new runs used Linux containers (Intel Xeon processor at 2.1~GHz, 4 virtual cores) with Python~3.11.15, NumPy~2.4.6 and SciPy~1.17.1.
