\section{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:setup}
The following protocol fixes the cases, budgets, starting points, ranking rule and tests for every method and every study of this paper.

\subsection{Cases and Methods}
The 68 benchmark cases combine the two wind data sets of the Kusiak--Song benchmark~\cite{Kusiak2010,Bansal2017} (Table~\ref{S-tab:S-wind} of the supplementary material; Data Set~I strongly directional, Data Set~II spread over more directions; $N_\theta=24$, $N_s=21$) with circular farms of radius $r=500$, 750 and 1000~m and $N=2,\ldots,10$, $2,\ldots,12$ and $2,\ldots,15$ turbines ($R=38.5$~m, $\ell_{\min}=4D=308$~m, $C_T=0.8$, $K=0.075$).

All methods minimize $F_p$~\eqref{eq:penalty} over $[-r,r]^{2N}$, with $N_p=30$ where applicable. The \emph{main comparison} includes eight methods: PSO-VNS (Algorithm~\ref{alg:hybrid}, $\omega=0.5$); PSO~\cite{Kennedy1995,Clerc2002} with~\eqref{eq:constriction}; SSA-VNS (SSA in Phase~1 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:hybrid}); SSA~\cite{Mirjalili2017}; LX-SSA~\cite{Solanki2023} ($\phi=0$, $\chi=1$); DE/rand/1/bin~\cite{Storn1997} (scale factor 0.5, crossover rate 0.9); VNS (Phase~2 alone, from the best initial layout); and MS-SLSQP, SciPy's SLSQP~\cite{Kraft1988} with explicit constraints and forward-difference gradients (1-m step), restarted from the initial layouts, best $F_p$ first, until the budget is used up. The \emph{component analysis} (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}) adds LX-SSA-VNS (LX-SSA in Phase~1) and the random-sampling control RS-VNS, which evaluates $\operatorname{round}(\omega B)=3{,}015$ uniform random layouts (including the initial population) and refines the best one by the same VNS, and it varies $\omega$ on \NSplitCases{} cases. The \emph{baseline analysis} (Table~\ref{tab:baseline}) ranks the pool LX-SSA-VNS, SSA-VNS, LX-SSA, SSA, PSO, DE, VNS and MS-SLSQP twice, with the constriction PSO and with the PSO runs with $w=0.7$, $c_1=c_2=2$ of the earlier version of this study, made with the same code, cases, budget and seeds (Section~\ref{S-sec:S-psosetting}).

\subsection{Comparison Design and Statistics}
\emph{Equal budgets and paired seeds.} Every run of the main comparison and the component analysis has exactly $B=6{,}030$ objective calls, counted by a wrapper around the objective function; the initial population and the finite-difference gradient calls of MS-SLSQP count. All iteration counts follow from $B$ (e.g., 200 for PSO, 100 for LX-SSA at $2N_p$ calls each; 3,030 Phase-1 calls in the hybrids). Each method is run with the seeds 1--30, and every optimizer draws its initial population first, so runs with the same seed start from the \emph{same} initial population for all methods and are paired.

\emph{Feasibility-aware ranking.} A layout is feasible if the spacing and boundary constraints hold within $10^{-6}$~m; means are taken over the feasible runs. Within a case, a method is ranked by the mean benchmark objective of its feasible runs only if at least 15 of its 30 runs (5 of 10) are feasible, and otherwise below all such methods, by its number of feasible runs, so that a few favorable feasible runs cannot earn a good rank.

\emph{Statistics.} All tests are two-sided with $\alpha=0.05$ and Holm's correction~\cite{Holm1979}. 1) Over the 68 cases: the Friedman test with the Iman--Davenport correction and post hoc tests of the average ranks against PSO-VNS~\cite{Demsar2006}. 2) Because such mean-rank tests depend on the other methods in the pool~\cite{Benavoli2016}: Wilcoxon signed-rank tests of PSO-VNS against each method on the 68 per-case mean wake losses. 3) Within each case: Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on the 30 seed-paired runs (every infeasible run ranked below every feasible run), adjusted over the comparisons of the case and counted as significant wins, no differences and significant losses (W/T/L), with the matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation $r_{\rm rb}$ as effect size. The component analysis applies 3) to planned contrasts.

\subsection{Tests Beyond the Benchmark and Reproducibility}
\label{sec:setup-beyond}
\label{sec:validation}
\emph{Initialization and budget.} Random initial layouts (uniform in the bounding square) are usually infeasible; the feasible initialization draws each initial layout inside the site and pushes the turbines apart with L-BFGS-B on a smooth packing penalty, without objective calls. The nine methods other than RS-VNS are run with it at 6,030 calls; RS-VNS would need one packing solve for each of its 3,015 random layouts, which costs far more than the objective calls. With random initialization, all ten methods are run at 6,030, 30,030 and 120,030 calls, with iteration counts scaled to the budget. Both studies use the six largest cases (largest $N$ of each farm and data set) and the Horns Rev~1 block, with 30 seeds (10 for Horns Rev~1 at 120,030 calls).

\emph{Horns Rev~1 and IEA37.} The Horns Rev~1 block is PyWake's 16-turbine example~\cite{PyWake}: V80 turbines (2~MW, $D=80$~m, 25-m/s cut-out), the measured 12-sector wind climate, the parallelogram through the corner turbines as boundary, $\ell_{\min}=4D$, and the Jensen wake with $K=0.04$ (AEP within 0.3\% of PyWake's). IEA37 Case Study~1~\cite{Baker2019,IEA37repo} fixes the model and leaves the optimizer free; its 16- and 36-turbine scenarios have circular boundaries of radius 1300 and 2000~m, $\ell_{\min}=2D=260$~m, 16 directions at 9.8~m/s and a simplified Bastankhah Gaussian wake, and our AEP matches the official calculator within a relative $10^{-11}$. All ten methods minimize the penalty~\eqref{eq:penalty} on the AEP loss at 6,030 and 30,030 calls (30 seeds), and their layouts are compared with the submitted ones.

\emph{Robustness.} Every feasible final layout of the main comparison is re-evaluated, not re-optimized, with a cubic power curve (with and without cut-out) and a Gaussian wake model (Section~\ref{sec:robustness}); the rankings are compared by the mean Kendall correlation $\bar\tau$ within a case and the share of cases with an unchanged best method.

\emph{Verification and code.} Our vectorized objective agrees with the original implementation within a relative $10^{-6}$ and reproduces the objectives of 720 archived final layouts within $8.7\times10^{-11}$. SSA, LX-SSA, PSO and DE (also in the hybrids) use our original code, which reproduces the archived result distributions of the earlier version (22 of 24 Mann--Whitney tests with $p\ge0.05$); the other methods were implemented for this study. The runs used Linux containers (Intel Xeon, 2.1~GHz, 4 virtual cores; Python~3.11.15, NumPy~2.4.6, SciPy~1.17.1).
