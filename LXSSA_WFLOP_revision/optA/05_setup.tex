\section{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:setup}
The protocol below applies to all methods; it fixes the cases, budgets, starting points, ranking rule and tests, so that the comparisons reflect the search strategies and not the conditions of the runs.

\subsection{Cases and Methods}
The 68 benchmark cases combine the two wind data sets of the Kusiak--Song benchmark~\cite{Kusiak2010,Bansal2017} (Table~\ref{S-tab:S-wind} of the supplementary material; Data Set~I strongly directional with $\zeta=2$, $\psi=13$ in all directions, Data Set~II spread over more directions with $\psi$ from 2.6 to 10~m/s; $N_\theta=24$, $N_s=21$) with circular farms of radius $r=500$, 750 and 1000~m and $N=2,\ldots,10$, $2,\ldots,12$ and $2,\ldots,15$ turbines ($R=38.5$~m, $\ell_{\min}=4D=308$~m, $C_T=0.8$, $K=0.075$).

All methods minimize $F_p$~\eqref{eq:penalty} over $[-r,r]^{2N}$, with $N_p=30$ where applicable. The \emph{main comparison} includes eight methods: PSO-VNS (Algorithm~\ref{alg:hybrid}, $\omega=0.5$); PSO~\cite{Kennedy1995,Clerc2002} with~\eqref{eq:constriction}; SSA-VNS (SSA in Phase~1 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:hybrid}); SSA~\cite{Mirjalili2017}; LX-SSA~\cite{Solanki2023} ($\phi=0$, $\chi=1$); DE/rand/1/bin~\cite{Storn1997} (scale factor 0.5, crossover rate 0.9); VNS (Phase~2 alone, from the best initial layout); and MS-SLSQP, SciPy's SLSQP~\cite{Kraft1988} with explicit spacing and boundary constraints and forward-difference gradients (1-m step), restarted from the initial layouts, best $F_p$ first, until the budget is used up. The \emph{component analysis} (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}) adds LX-SSA-VNS (LX-SSA in Phase~1) and the random-sampling control RS-VNS, which evaluates $\operatorname{round}(\omega B)=3{,}015$ uniform random layouts (including the initial population) and refines the best one by the same VNS, and it varies $\omega$ on \NSplitCases{} cases. The \emph{baseline analysis} (Table~\ref{tab:baseline}) ranks the pool LX-SSA-VNS, SSA-VNS, LX-SSA, SSA, PSO, DE, VNS and MS-SLSQP twice: with the constriction PSO and with the PSO runs with $w=0.7$, $c_1=c_2=2$ of the earlier version of this study, which used the same cases, budget, seeds and evaluator.

\subsection{Comparison Design and Statistics}
\emph{Equal budgets and paired seeds.} Every run of the main comparison and the component analysis has exactly $B=6{,}030$ objective calls, counted by a wrapper around the objective function; the initial population and the finite-difference gradient calls of MS-SLSQP count. All iteration counts follow from $B$: 200 for PSO, SSA and DE, 100 for LX-SSA (at $2N_p$ calls each), and 3,030 Phase-1 calls before 3,000 calls of VNS in the hybrids. Each method is run with the seeds 1--30, and every optimizer draws its initial population first, so runs with the same seed start from the \emph{same} initial population for all methods and are paired.

\emph{Feasibility-aware ranking.} A layout is feasible if the spacing and boundary constraints hold within $10^{-6}$~m, and means are taken over the feasible runs. Within a case, a method is ranked by the mean benchmark objective of its feasible runs only if at least 15 of its 30 runs (5 of 10) are feasible; otherwise it is ranked below all such methods, by its number of feasible runs, so that a few favorable feasible runs cannot earn a good rank.

\emph{Statistics.} Over the 68 cases, we use the Friedman test with the Iman--Davenport correction and Holm-adjusted post hoc tests of the average ranks against PSO-VNS~\cite{Holm1979,Demsar2006}. Because a mean-rank post hoc test between two methods depends on the other methods in the pool~\cite{Benavoli2016}, we also compare PSO-VNS with each method by two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on the 68 per-case mean wake losses (Holm-adjusted). At the run level, the 30 seed-paired runs of a case are compared by two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, Holm-adjusted over the comparisons of the case, with every infeasible run ranked below every feasible run; we count the cases with a significant win, no difference and a significant loss (W/T/L) and give the matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation $r_{\rm rb}$. The component analysis applies these tests to planned contrasts. Throughout, $\alpha=0.05$.

\subsection{Tests Beyond the Benchmark and Reproducibility}
\label{sec:setup-beyond}
\label{sec:validation}
\emph{Initialization and budget.} Random initial layouts, drawn uniformly in the bounding square, are usually infeasible. In the feasible initialization, each initial layout is drawn inside the site and pushed apart by minimizing a smooth packing penalty with L-BFGS-B, without objective calls. The nine methods other than RS-VNS are run with it at 6,030 calls; RS-VNS would need one packing solve for each of its 3,015 random layouts, which costs far more than the objective calls. With random initialization, all ten methods are run at 6,030, 30,030 and 120,030 calls, with iteration counts scaled to the budget. Both studies use the six largest cases (largest $N$ of each farm and data set) and the Horns Rev~1 block, with 30 seeds (10 for Horns Rev~1 at 120,030 calls).

\emph{Horns Rev~1 and IEA37.} The Horns Rev~1 block is PyWake's 16-turbine example~\cite{PyWake}: V80 turbines (2~MW, $D=80$~m, tabulated power and thrust curves, 25-m/s cut-out), the measured 12-sector wind climate, the parallelogram through the corner turbines as boundary, $\ell_{\min}=4D$, and the Jensen wake with $K=0.04$ (AEP within 0.3\% of PyWake's). IEA37 Case Study~1~\cite{Baker2019,IEA37repo} fixes the model and leaves the optimizer free; we use its 16- and 36-turbine scenarios (circles of radius 1300 and 2000~m, $\ell_{\min}=2D=260$~m, 3.35-MW turbine, 16 directions at 9.8~m/s, simplified Bastankhah Gaussian wake), with an AEP that matches the official calculator within a relative $10^{-11}$. All ten methods minimize the penalty~\eqref{eq:penalty} on the AEP loss at 6,030 and 30,030 calls (30 seeds), and their layouts are compared with the submitted ones.

\emph{Robustness.} Every feasible final layout of the main comparison is re-evaluated, not re-optimized, with a cubic power curve (with and without cut-out) and a Gaussian wake model (Section~\ref{sec:robustness}); the rankings are compared by the mean Kendall correlation $\bar\tau$ within a case and the share of cases with an unchanged best method.

\emph{Verification and code.} Our vectorized objective agrees with the original implementation within a relative $10^{-6}$ and reproduces the objectives of 720 archived final layouts within $8.7\times10^{-11}$. SSA, LX-SSA, PSO and DE, also inside the hybrids, use our original code of the earlier version, which reproduces the archived result distributions (22 of 24 Mann--Whitney tests with $p\ge0.05$); the other methods were implemented for this study. All new runs used Linux containers (Intel Xeon at 2.1~GHz, 4 virtual cores; Python~3.11.15, NumPy~2.4.6, SciPy~1.17.1).
