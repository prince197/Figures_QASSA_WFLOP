\section{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Cases and Methods}
The 68 cases combine the two Kusiak--Song wind data sets~\cite{Kusiak2010,Bansal2017} (Table~\ref{S-tab:S-wind}) with circular farms of radii $r=500$, 750 and 1000~m and $N=2,\ldots,10$, $2,\ldots,12$ and $2,\ldots,15$ turbines ($R=38.5$~m, $\ell_{\min}=4D=308$~m, $C_T=0.8$, $K=0.075$).

The \emph{main comparison} includes eight methods ($N_p=30$ where applicable): PSO-VNS (Algorithm~\ref{alg:hybrid}, $\omega=0.5$); PSO; SSA-VNS; SSA; LX-SSA; DE/rand/1/bin~\cite{Storn1997} (scale factor 0.5, crossover rate 0.9); VNS; and MS-SLSQP, a multistart sequential least-squares programming method (SciPy's SLSQP~\cite{Kraft1988}, forward-difference gradients). All methods except MS-SLSQP minimize $F_p$~\eqref{eq:penalty} over $[-r,r]^{2N}$; MS-SLSQP minimizes the wake loss under explicit constraints, restarting from the initial layouts and then from random ones, and returns its best feasible layout (settings: Section~\ref{S-sec:S-protocol}). DE and MS-SLSQP use standard, untuned settings. The \emph{component analysis} (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}) adds LX-SSA-VNS and the no-search control RS-VNS (Section~\ref{sec:template}), whose 3,015 random layouts cost about as much as the 3,030 Phase-1 evaluations of the hybrids; a split study varies $\omega$ on \NSplitCases{} cases (Section~\ref{sec:split}).

\subsection{Comparison Design and Statistics}
\emph{Equal budgets and paired seeds.} Unless stated otherwise, a run uses exactly $B=6{,}030$ objective evaluations, counted by an objective wrapper, including the initial population and MS-SLSQP's gradient evaluations. Each method uses seeds 1--30 and draws its initial population first, so runs with equal seeds share the initial population and are paired (where logged, the best initial objectives agree, also with the earlier version).
% verified 2026-09-28 (05 fix agent): first Curve value identical (spread 0) across fresh_grid/vgrid/bgrid and mpce_psoc/psobv/rsvns/slsqp (13 method-files) for all 792 case-seed pairs with a feasible initial best; infeasible starts are logged as NaN

\emph{Feasibility-aware ranking.} Within a case, a method is ranked by the mean benchmark objective of its feasible runs only if at least 15 of its 30 runs (5 of 10 for 10 seeds) are feasible, and otherwise below all such methods, by its number of feasible runs. This limits, but does not remove, survivorship bias.

\emph{Statistics.} All tests are two-sided ($\alpha=0.05$), with Holm's correction~\cite{Holm1979} within each family of comparisons. 1) Case ranks: the Friedman test with the Iman--Davenport F test and post hoc tests of the average ranks against PSO-VNS~\cite{Demsar2006,Derrac2011,LaTorre2021}. 2) Case means (primary tests): since mean-rank post hoc tests depend on the pool~\cite{Benavoli2016}, Wilcoxon signed-rank tests of PSO-VNS against each method on the per-case mean wake losses; a case in which only one of the two methods has at least 15 feasible runs counts as a maximal difference in its favor (\NCmImputedPhrase{} cases), and one in which neither has is dropped (\NCmDropped{} cases). Headline mean differences (cases in which both qualify) carry 95\% bootstrap confidence intervals over cases. 3) Runs: per case, Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on the 30 seed-paired runs (infeasible runs below all feasible ones, ordered by minimum spacing), Holm-corrected over the comparisons of the case, with the matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation $r_{\rm rb}$ (zero differences dropped) and descriptive counts of significant wins, ties and losses (W/T/L). 4) Equivalence, as nonsignificance is not equivalence~\cite{Lakens2017}: TOST on the case-mean differences (90\% bootstrap interval within $\pm\NEqMargin$~pp, i.e., \NEqMargin\% of the wake-free energy, far below wake-model uncertainty; margin fixed after the primary analysis, minimal margin reported) and the Bayesian signed-rank test with this region of practical equivalence~\cite{Benavoli2017} (Table~\ref{S-tab:equivalence}).

\emph{Robustness of inference.} The 68 cases form \NXClusters{} (data set, radius) clusters with nested $N$, not independent draws; the p values describe this benchmark, and subgroup splits are exploratory. Thresholds of \NXThrList{} feasible runs and leaving out any cluster change no verdict, every significant difference has the same sign in all clusters, and one Holm family over all \NXMultN{} case-mean tests quoted unadjusted in the text makes only the post hoc subgroup \NXMultLostHolm{} nonsignificant (Tables~\ref{S-tab:X-threshold}--\ref{S-tab:X-multiplicity}).

\subsection{Tests Beyond the Benchmark and Reproducibility}
\label{sec:setup-beyond}
\label{sec:validation}
\emph{Initialization and budget.} Random initial layouts are usually infeasible; the feasible initialization draws seeded layouts inside the site and separates the turbines with L-BFGS-B on a packing penalty, without objective evaluations (Section~\ref{S-sec:S-protocol}); all methods but RS-VNS (random sampling) use it at 6,030 evaluations. With random starts, all ten run at 6,030, 30,030 and 120,030 evaluations. Both studies use the six largest cases (largest $N$ per farm and data set) and the Horns Rev~1 block, with 30 seeds (10 for Horns Rev~1 at 120,030).

\emph{Other sites and models.} The Horns Rev~1 block is PyWake's 16-turbine example~\cite{PyWake}: V80 turbines ($D=80$~m), the measured 12-sector wind climate, the parallelogram through the corner turbines as boundary (Section~\ref{sec:hornsrev-site}), $\ell_{\min}=4D$ and the Jensen wake with $K=0.04$.

The 16- and 36-turbine scenarios of IEA37 Case Study~1~\cite{Baker2019,IEA37repo} (circular sites of radius 1300 and 2000~m, $\ell_{\min}=2D$, 16 directions at one speed, a simplified Bastankhah Gaussian wake) test optimizers rather than represent a site; all ten methods run on them at 6,030 and 30,030 evaluations (30 seeds).

\emph{Verification and code.} Our objective matches the original implementation (largest difference $8.7\times10^{-11}$ over 720 archived final objectives) and the official IEA37 calculator (relative difference ${<}10^{-11}$). Our reused code of SSA, LX-SSA, PSO and DE (also in the hybrids) reproduces the result distributions of the earlier version (2 of 24 Mann--Whitney tests reject at $\alpha=0.05$; 1.2 expected by chance). Instrumented copies for the diagnostics reproduce the stored runs and match the original code bit for bit (\NDVerBitMatch{} reruns). The runs used Linux containers (Intel Xeon, 2.1~GHz, 4 virtual cores; Python~3.11.15, NumPy~2.4.6, SciPy~1.17.1); code, per-run results and scripts are public (Data Availability).
