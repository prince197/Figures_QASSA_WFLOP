\section{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:setup}
The protocol below fixes the cases, budgets, starting points, ranking rule and tests for all methods, so that the comparisons reflect differences between search strategies and not between the conditions of the runs.

\subsection{Cases and Methods}
The 68 benchmark cases combine the two wind data sets of the Kusiak--Song benchmark~\cite{Kusiak2010,Bansal2017} (Table~\ref{S-tab:S-wind} of the supplementary material; Data Set~I: $\zeta=2$ and $\psi=13$ in all directions, 80\% of the probability in the two bins between $75^\circ$ and $105^\circ$; Data Set~II: $\zeta=2$, $\psi$ from 2.6 to 10~m/s, wind spread over more directions; $N_\theta=24$ bins of $15^\circ$, $N_s=21$ speed intervals of 0.5~m/s) with circular farms of radius $r=500$, 750 and 1000~m and $N=2,\ldots,10$, $2,\ldots,12$ and $2,\ldots,15$ turbines ($R=38.5$~m, $\ell_{\min}=4D=308$~m, $C_T=0.8$, $K=0.075$).

All methods minimize $F_p$~\eqref{eq:penalty} over $[-r,r]^{2N}$, with $N_p=30$ where applicable. The \emph{main comparison} includes eight methods: PSO-VNS (Algorithm~\ref{alg:hybrid}, $\omega=0.5$); PSO~\cite{Kennedy1995,Clerc2002} with~\eqref{eq:constriction}; SSA-VNS (Algorithm~\ref{alg:hybrid} with SSA in Phase~1, $\xi_1$ decreasing over the Phase-1 iterations); SSA~\cite{Mirjalili2017}; LX-SSA~\cite{Solanki2023} ($\phi=0$, $\chi=1$); DE/rand/1/bin~\cite{Storn1997} (scale factor 0.5, crossover rate 0.9); VNS (Phase~2 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:hybrid}, started from the best initial layout); and MS-SLSQP, SciPy's SLSQP~\cite{Kraft1988} with explicit spacing and boundary constraints and forward-difference gradients (1-m step), started from the initial layouts in the order of their $F_p$ values and restarted until the budget is used up. The \emph{component analysis} (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}) adds two Phase-1 alternatives, RS-VNS, a random-sampling control that evaluates $\operatorname{round}(\omega B)=3{,}015$ uniform random layouts (including the initial population) and refines the best one by the same VNS, and LX-SSA-VNS (LX-SSA in Phase~1), and it varies $\omega$ on \NSplitCases{} cases. The \emph{baseline analysis} (Table~\ref{tab:baseline}) ranks the pool LX-SSA-VNS, SSA-VNS, LX-SSA, SSA, PSO, DE, VNS and MS-SLSQP once with the constriction PSO and once with the PSO runs with $w=0.7$, $c_1=c_2=2$ of the earlier version of this study, which used the same cases, budget, seeds and evaluator. SSA, LX-SSA, PSO and DE use our original code of that version; the other methods were implemented for this study, and the hybrids call the original swarm code in Phase~1.

\subsection{Comparison Design and Statistics}
\emph{Equal budgets and paired seeds.} Every run of the main comparison and the component analysis has exactly $B=6{,}030$ objective calls, counted by a wrapper around the objective function; the initial population and the finite-difference gradient calls of MS-SLSQP count, and all iteration counts follow from $B$ (PSO, SSA and DE: 200; LX-SSA: 100, at $2N_p$ calls each; PSO-VNS and SSA-VNS: 100 in Phase~1, i.e., 3,030 calls, then 3,000 calls of VNS; LX-SSA-VNS: 50). Each method is run with the seeds 1--30, and every optimizer draws its initial population first, so runs with the same seed start from the \emph{same} initial population for all methods and are paired.

\emph{Feasibility-aware ranking.} A layout is feasible if the spacing and boundary constraints hold within $10^{-6}$~m. Within a case, a method is ranked by the mean benchmark objective of its feasible runs only if at least 15 of its 30 runs (5 of 10) are feasible; otherwise it is ranked below all such methods, by its number of feasible runs. A method that is feasible in only a few favorable runs thus cannot obtain a good rank.

\emph{Statistics.} For each run we record the benchmark objective, the wake loss (in percent of the wake-free objective), feasibility, the convergence curve (best feasible objective at 201 checkpoints) and the time; means are taken over the feasible runs. Over the 68 cases, a Friedman test with the Iman--Davenport correction and Holm-adjusted post hoc tests of the average ranks against PSO-VNS compare the methods~\cite{Holm1979,Demsar2006}. Because a mean-rank post hoc test between two methods depends on the other methods in the pool~\cite{Benavoli2016}, we also compare PSO-VNS with each method by two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on the 68 per-case mean wake losses (Holm-adjusted). At the run level, the 30 seed-paired runs of a case are compared by two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, Holm-adjusted over the comparisons of the case, with every infeasible run ranked below every feasible run; we count the cases with a significant win, no difference and a significant loss (W/T/L) and give the matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation $r_{\rm rb}$ as effect size. The component analysis applies these tests to planned contrasts (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). Throughout, $\alpha=0.05$.

\subsection{Tests Beyond the Benchmark}
\label{sec:setup-beyond}
\emph{Initialization and budget.} Random initial layouts, drawn uniformly in the bounding square, are usually infeasible. In the feasible initialization, each initial layout is drawn inside the site and pushed apart by minimizing a smooth packing penalty with L-BFGS-B until the constraints hold, without objective calls. The nine methods other than RS-VNS are run with it at 6,030 calls; RS-VNS is not, because it would need one packing solve for each of its 3,015 random layouts per run, which costs far more than the objective calls themselves. With random initialization, all ten methods are run at 6,030, 30,030 and 120,030 calls, with the iteration counts scaled to the budget. Both studies use the six largest cases (the largest $N$ of each farm and data set) and the Horns Rev~1 block, with 30 seeds (10 for Horns Rev~1 at 120,030 calls).

\emph{Horns Rev~1 and IEA37.} The Horns Rev~1 block is PyWake's 16-turbine example~\cite{PyWake}: V80 turbines (2~MW, $D=80$~m) with tabulated power and thrust curves and a 25-m/s cut-out, the measured 12-sector wind climate, the parallelogram through the corner turbines as boundary, $\ell_{\min}=4D$, and the Jensen wake with $K=0.04$ (AEP within 0.3\% of PyWake's). IEA37 Case Study~1~\cite{Baker2019,IEA37repo} fixes the model and leaves the optimizer free; we use its 16- and 36-turbine scenarios (circles of radius 1300 and 2000~m, $\ell_{\min}=2D=260$~m, 3.35-MW turbine, 16 directions at 9.8~m/s, simplified Bastankhah Gaussian wake), and our AEP matches the official calculator within a relative deviation of $10^{-11}$. All ten methods minimize the penalty~\eqref{eq:penalty} applied to the AEP loss at 6,030 and 30,030 calls with 30 seeds, and their layouts are compared with the submitted ones.

\emph{Robustness.} Every feasible final layout of the main comparison is re-evaluated, not re-optimized, with a cubic power curve (with and without cut-out) and a Gaussian wake model (Section~\ref{sec:robustness}); the rankings are compared by the mean Kendall correlation $\bar\tau$ within a case and the share of cases with an unchanged best method.

\subsection{Verification and Reproducibility}
\label{sec:validation}
Our vectorized objective agrees with the original implementation within a relative difference of $10^{-6}$ and reproduces the objectives of 720 archived final layouts within $8.7\times10^{-11}$; the original SSA, LX-SSA, PSO and DE code reproduces the archived result distributions (22 of 24 Mann--Whitney tests with $p\ge0.05$). All new runs used Linux containers (Intel Xeon at 2.1~GHz, 4 virtual cores; Python~3.11.15, NumPy~2.4.6, SciPy~1.17.1).
