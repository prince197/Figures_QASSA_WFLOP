\section{Discussion and Recommendations}
\label{sec:discussion}
In the WFLOP, which method appears best can depend on the protocol as strongly as on the methods. \emph{Baseline configuration:} replacing the old setting, which violates the order-2 stability condition~\eqref{eq:poli}, by the constriction setting moves PSO from position \NBaseOldPSOPos{} to \NBaseNewPSOPos{} in Table~\ref{tab:baseline}, ahead of every salp-swarm method at 6,030 evaluations. Instrumented runs show why: the old swarm stays \NDSpreadRatio{} times wider, and only \NDFeasPartOld\% of its particle evaluations are feasible (constriction: \NDFeasPartNew\%). An advantage over PSO is informative only if the PSO baseline is convergent.

\emph{Component analysis:} a swarm phase must also beat random sampling of equal cost. The RS-VNS control is weak (\NDRsRunsNoFeasSample{} of its runs never sample a feasible layout), yet only the PSO phase beats it clearly (\NCmPSOVNSvsRSVNSDL~pp). The LX-SSA phase is practically equivalent to it ($\pm\NEqMargin$~pp), and the small SSA gain (\NCmSSAVNSvsRSVNSDL~pp) could not be shown to be equivalent to zero at this margin, although a Bayesian analysis~\cite{Benavoli2017} finds it more likely practically negligible (\NBaySSAVNSvsRSVNSRope). At 6,030 evaluations, the second phase is essentially one compass-search descent (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}), so this analysis measures the value of a start for a local search, not of the VNS neighborhoods.

\emph{PSO and PSO-VNS:} a well-configured PSO and PSO-VNS are practically equivalent on the case means (90\% interval \NEqPSOVNSvsPSOCI~pp; mean gain \NXEnPSOVNSvsPSOPct\% of benchmark AEP), with no difference in spread (Section~\ref{sec:overall}); the rank flip under the Gaussian model is consistent with this. PSO-VNS adds feasibility reliability (Horns Rev~1: \NHRFeasPSOVNS{} vs.\ \NHRFeasPSO{} feasible runs) and a gain in the larger Data Set~II layouts ($N\ge10$: lower loss in \NCmPSOVNSvsPSOdsIILargeWins{} of \NCmPSOVNSvsPSOdsIILargeN{} cases, all-test Holm $p=\NXHolmPSOVNSvsPSOdsIILargeP$).

For benchmark-type problems with random starts, a constriction PSO is a sound first choice and PSO-VNS a more reliable one; from feasible starts, VNS alone is competitive. For WFLOP comparisons, we recommend~\cite{BartzBeielstein2020,LaTorre2021}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item use baseline settings with documented convergence or standard defaults (for PSO, \eqref{eq:poli} or \eqref{eq:constriction}), report them, and verify their behavior with instrumented runs;
\item give all methods the same evaluation budget, counting the initial population and gradient calls, and pair runs by seed;
\item use feasibility-aware ranking and report the share of feasible runs;
\item test case means and runs with a multiplicity correction~\cite{Derrac2011}, and report robustness to the qualification threshold and, for nested cases, at the cluster level;
\item test practical equivalence with a stated margin~\cite{Lakens2017,Benavoli2017}; non-significance is not equivalence;
\item in ablations of hybrids, include the local method alone and a strong random-sampling control of equal cost;
\item report feasible-start and model-robustness checks; publish code and data.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
The benchmark uses the top-hat Jensen wake, the linear Kusiak--Song power curve, coarse direction bins that optimized layouts can exploit, circular farms, a $4D$ spacing and a prescribed $N$. The 68 cases form \NXClusters{} clusters with nested $N$, so the p-values describe this benchmark, and the SSA gain over RS-VNS, though consistent over the clusters, is borderline under a cluster-robust $t$ test ($p=\NXClSSAVNSvsRSVNSCRP$). The $N\ge10$ split is post hoc, and the equivalence margin was set after the primary analysis, so we report the smallest margin at which equivalence holds (\NEqPSOVNSvsPSOMin~pp for PSO-VNS vs.\ PSO). At 6,030 evaluations, the VNS neighborhoods are hardly exercised; the budget study covers only six cases and Horns Rev~1, and the old PSO setting was run only at 6,030 evaluations. DE, VNS and $\omega=0.5$ are untuned defaults.
% CHECK-EXTRA [X11]: all significant pairs 6/6 clusters; [X17]: SSA-VNS vs RS-VNS cluster-robust p >= 0.05 (caveat); CHECK-FINAL [C49]: minimal margin NEqPSOVNSvsPSOMin < NEqMargin; CHECK-DIAG [D08]-[D10]
The pool lacks CMA-ES~\cite{HansenOstermeier2001} or L-SHADE, and MS-SLSQP uses finite-difference gradients on a discontinuous objective. RS-VNS samples in the bounding square; a stronger control (sampling inside the site, or a feasible random search~\cite{Feng2015}) may narrow the gap to the PSO phase. From feasible starts, \NFbFeasBestRank{} ranks first and PSO and DE stall (Section~\ref{sec:feasinit}), a mechanism observed, not derived; our statements on PSO-VNS refer to random starts.

The Horns Rev~1 comparison tests the optimizers, not the installed design (Section~\ref{sec:hornsrev-site}); on IEA37, the best PSO-VNS layouts at 30,030 evaluations \NIEAPhrase. Turbulence and fatigue loads (critical at $4D$), wake steering, blockage, terrain, cables, electrical losses and cost objectives are outside our scope; all AEP values are gross.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
First, the baseline configuration changes the conclusions: the old PSO setting, which violates the order-2 stability condition and rarely samples feasible layouts, places PSO behind salp-swarm hybrids, whereas the constriction setting places it ahead of every salp-swarm method. Second, a PSO phase gives the second phase, at this budget one compass-search descent, a clearly better start than random sampling, whereas the salp-swarm phases add little or nothing. Third, a well-configured PSO and PSO-VNS are practically equivalent on average ($\pm\NEqMargin$~pp); PSO-VNS adds feasibility reliability and gains in the larger Data Set~II layouts. It is thus a consistent reference method, not a superior optimizer: from feasible starts, \NFbFeasBestRank{} alone is best. Future work will address larger budgets and farms, stronger controls, higher-fidelity wake models and economic objectives.
