\section{Discussion and Recommendations}
\label{sec:discussion}
The three main findings (Sections~\ref{sec:results}--\ref{sec:robustness}) share one implication: in the continuous WFLOP, which method appears best can depend on the comparison protocol as strongly as on the methods. Replacing the old setting, which violates the order-2 stability condition~\eqref{eq:poli}, by the constriction setting moves PSO from position \NBaseOldPSOPos{} to \NBaseNewPSOPos{} in Table~\ref{tab:baseline}, ahead of every salp-swarm method, at 6,030 evaluations; at 120,030 evaluations, SSA-VNS ranks ahead of PSO on the six largest cases (\NBudRankSSAVNSOneTwentyK{} vs.\ \NBudRankPSOOneTwentyK).
% CHECK-FINAL [C27]: old setting: at least one salp-swarm hybrid ahead of PSO; corrected: PSO ahead of every salp-swarm method
% no CHECK-FINAL id for the 120,030 clause: requires feasbudget.rank[120030] SSABV < PSOC (4.17 vs 5.83 on 2026-09-28)
An advantage over PSO is therefore informative only if the PSO baseline is convergent, and a swarm phase must beat a random-sampling control of equal cost. RS-VNS is a weak, no-search control (a uniform sample in the bounding square has all $N$ turbines inside the circle only with probability $(\pi/4)^N$), so beating it is a minimum bar: the SSA phase clears it narrowly (tied at run level, \NAblSSAVNSvsRSVNS; case-mean difference \NCmSSAVNSvsRSVNSDL{} percentage points, $p=\NCmSSAVNSvsRSVNSP$), the LX-SSA phase not detectably ($p=\NCmLXSSAVNSvsRSVNSP$), and the PSO phase by a clearly larger margin (\NCmPSOVNSvsRSVNSDL{} percentage points). PSO-VNS is a reasonable reference because it combines two standard components, adds one parameter ($\omega$) and is feasible in every run, but its difference from PSO is not significant over all cases ($p=\NPWPSO$).

% CHECK-FINAL [C04]: case-mean p >= 0.05; [C08]: feasible in every run

For benchmark-type problems of up to 36 turbines with random starts, PSO-VNS or a constriction PSO is a sound first choice ($\omega=0.75$ beat the pre-specified 0.5 on \NSplitCases{} test cases and should be confirmed on the target problem), and VNS alone is competitive from a feasible start; for engineering design, the optimizer should be re-checked on a site-calibrated objective with the site spacing and cable and cost terms. For WFLOP comparisons we recommend the following established practices~\cite{BartzBeielstein2020}, all applied in this paper:
\begin{enumerate}
\item use baseline settings with documented convergence or standard defaults (for PSO, check \eqref{eq:poli} or use \eqref{eq:constriction}) and report them;
\item give all methods the same number of evaluations, counting the initial population and all gradient calls;
\item pair the runs by seed and initial population;
\item use feasibility-aware ranking and report the share of feasible runs;
\item test the per-case means and the runs of each case with a multiplicity correction, and do not read a non-significant difference as equivalence;
\item in ablations of hybrids, include a random-sampling control of equal cost and the local method alone;
\item report feasible-start and model-robustness checks, and publish code and data.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
The benchmark uses the top-hat Jensen wake, the linear Kusiak--Song power curve, coarse direction bins that optimized layouts can exploit, circular farms, a $4D$ spacing and a prescribed $N$; we did not re-optimize with other models. The 68 cases are not independent (six farm--wind combinations with nested $N$), so the p-values describe this benchmark, not a population of farms, and the $N\ge10$ split is post hoc. The 68-case results hold at 6,030 evaluations; the budget study covers only the six largest cases and the Horns Rev~1 block, and the old PSO setting was run only at 6,030 evaluations. Only the PSO coefficients were revisited, on theoretical grounds; DE, VNS and $\omega=0.5$ are untuned defaults.
% CHECK-FINAL [C18]: split favors 75% (the pre-specified omega = 0.5 is not the best split)
The pool lacks state-of-the-art optimizers such as CMA-ES~\cite{HansenOstermeier2001} or L-SHADE, and MS-SLSQP uses finite-difference gradients on a discontinuous objective, so its rank says little about gradient-based methods. RS-VNS samples in the bounding square, and a stronger control (sampling inside the site or with a feasibility repair) may narrow the gap to the PSO phase; the component analysis does not separate faster feasibility from better exploration of the wake-loss landscape. From feasible starts, \NFbFeasBestRank{} ranks first and PSO and DE stall (Section~\ref{sec:feasinit}), so our statements on PSO-VNS refer to random starts.
% CHECK-FINAL [C28]: feasible starts: VNS alone first
The Horns Rev~1 comparison uses our Jensen model, $4D$ instead of the installed $7D$ spacing and no wakes of the rest of the farm, cables or loads, so it tests the optimizers, not the installed design; on IEA37, the best PSO-VNS layouts at 30,030 evaluations \NIEAPhrase. We did not re-run the comparisons of other studies. Wake-added turbulence and fatigue loads (critical at $4D$), wake steering, blockage, terrain, cables, grid connection, electrical losses and cost objectives are outside our scope; all AEP values are gross, wake-only values.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This paper revisited metaheuristic comparisons for the continuous WFLOP under a controlled protocol on 68 benchmark cases, a Horns Rev~1 block and IEA37 Case Study~1. First, the baseline configuration changes the conclusions: the old PSO setting violates the order-2 stability condition and places PSO behind salp-swarm hybrids, whereas the constriction setting places it ahead of every salp-swarm method at 6,030 evaluations. Second, a VNS phase significantly improves the salp-swarm methods but gives PSO only a small, non-significant gain, and a PSO phase gives VNS a clearly better start than random sampling, while salp-swarm phases add little beyond random sampling and the Laplace step makes the hybrid worse (SSA-VNS vs.\ LX-SSA-VNS, $p=\NCmSSAVNSvsLXSSAVNSP$). Third, PSO-VNS is a simple and consistent reference method: it is feasible in every run, has the best average rank (\NRankPSOVNS), also on the six largest cases at all three budgets (exploratory; first in \NBudFirstPSOVNSOneTwentyK{} of 6 cases at 120,030 evaluations), and \NHRAbovePSOVNS{} of its 30 Horns Rev~1 runs exceed the modeled AEP of the installed block. Yet it is not significantly different from PSO over the 68 cases ($p=\NPWPSO$; PSO is significantly better in \NWtlPSOL{} cases), its advantage is concentrated in Data Set~II with $N\ge10$ (\NCmPSOVNSvsPSOdsIILargeWins{} of \NCmPSOVNSvsPSOdsIILargeN{} cases; exploratory), \NFbFeasBestRank{} alone is best from feasible starts, \NBestGauss{} ranks first by a small margin under the Gaussian re-evaluation, and on IEA37 it stays below the best published layouts.
