\subsection{PSO and Its Stability Conditions}
\label{sec:pso}
We use the canonical global-best PSO~\cite{Kennedy1995} in the inertia-weight form~\cite{Shi1998}. Particle $i$ has a position $\mathbf X^i$, a velocity $\mathbf V^i$ and a personal best $\mathbf P^i$, the swarm keeps the global best $\mathbf G$, and a particle moves by
\begin{align}
\mathbf V^i&\leftarrow w\,\mathbf V^i+c_1\boldsymbol\rho_1\circ\bigl(\mathbf P^i-\mathbf X^i\bigr)+c_2\boldsymbol\rho_2\circ\bigl(\mathbf G-\mathbf X^i\bigr),\label{eq:pso-v}\\
\mathbf X^i&\leftarrow\mathbf X^i+\mathbf V^i,\label{eq:pso-x}
\end{align}
where $\boldsymbol\rho_1,\boldsymbol\rho_2$ are vectors of independent $U(0,1)$ numbers, drawn anew for every move, and $\circ$ is the componentwise product. The constriction PSO of Clerc and Kennedy~\cite{Clerc2002} with their standard choice $\varphi_1=\varphi_2=2.05$ (constriction factor $\kappa=0.7298$) is identical to \eqref{eq:pso-v} with
\begin{equation}
w=0.7298,\qquad c_1=c_2=\kappa\varphi_1=1.49618 .
\label{eq:constriction}
\end{equation}
PSO denotes \eqref{eq:pso-v}, \eqref{eq:pso-x} with these coefficients, fixed a priori from the literature, and the \emph{old setting} the same algorithm with $w=0.7$, $c_1=c_2=2$, i.e., the acceleration coefficients of the original PSO~\cite{Kennedy1995} combined with an inertia weight. Both are run as implemented: velocities start at zero and are not clamped (no $V_{\max}$), a coordinate clipped to the square (Section~\ref{sec:constraints}) keeps its velocity, and $\mathbf P^i$, $\mathbf G$ are updated after each evaluation.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:pso-stability}
Under stagnation (fixed $\mathbf P^i$, $\mathbf G$) and without active bounds, the expected position converges to $(c_1\mathbf P^i+c_2\mathbf G)/(c_1+c_2)$ for all initial values if and only if $\lvert w\rvert<1$ and $0<c_1+c_2<4(1+w)$ (order-1 stability; $(c_1+c_2)/2=\mathrm E(c_1\rho_1+c_2\rho_2)$). For $c_1=c_2$, the second moments converge as well (order-2 stability), with a limit variance $\propto(P^i_m-G_m)^2$ in coordinate $m$, if and only if, in addition~\cite{Poli2009},
\begin{equation}
c_1+c_2<\frac{24\,(1-w^2)}{7-5w}.
\label{eq:poli}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
Proofs, also for $c_1\ne c_2$ (stricter bound): Section~\ref{S-sec:S-th-pso}; for $c_1=c_2$, the region also holds under a weaker, non-stagnant assumption~\cite{Cleghorn2018}. The constriction setting is order-2 stable ($c_1+c_2=2.99<3.35$). The old setting is order-1 stable ($4<6.8$) but lies beyond the order-2 boundary ($4>3.50$; $c_1=c_2>c^*=\NSBoundC$ at $w=0.7$; Table~\ref{S-tab:S-th-settings}). In a sweep of $c_1=c_2=c$ at $w=0.7$ (Fig.~\ref{S-fig:S-csweep}, Table~\ref{S-tab:S-csweep}), $\ge\NSFeasCOneSeven\%$ of the final layouts are feasible up to $c=1.7$; beyond $c^*$, feasibility falls progressively (to \NSFeasCTwoZero\% at $c=2$), so the boundary marks approximately where this PSO starts to fail. Clamping velocities to $\NSVmaxFrac(ub_m-lb_m)$ largely rescues the old setting (\NSFeasVMax\% feasible), zeroing clipped ones does not (\NSFeasVZero\%): it fails mainly through large steps. Its swarm contracts much less (Section~\ref{sec:psosetting}); \NDStoredSpacingOnlyOld{} of its \NDStoredInfeasOld{} infeasible final layouts violate only the spacing, and \NDStoredTangentOld{} of the other \NDStoredAnyBoundOld{} have a turbine pinned at the box near a tangent point (Section~\ref{sec:constraints}). DE's $F=0.5$ exceeds Zaharie's critical value (0.14 at $\mathrm{CR}=0.9$)~\cite{Zaharie2002}, so its weak results are not due to premature variance loss.

\subsection{Salp Swarm Algorithm and Its Laplacian Variant}
\label{sec:ssa}
The SSA~\cite{Mirjalili2017} keeps the best solution found so far as the food source $\mathbf H$ and splits the sorted population into leaders and followers. A leader $i\le N_p/2$ updates each coordinate by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leader}
X_m^i = H_m\pm\xi_1\big((ub_m-lb_m)\xi_2+lb_m\big),
\end{equation}
with either sign with probability $1/2$, $\xi_2\sim U(0,1)$ and $\xi_1=2\exp[-(4t/T)^2]$ decreasing with the iteration $t$ out of $T$; a follower $i>N_p/2$ moves to the midpoint of its position and the new position of the preceding salp. The leaders thus perturb $\mathbf H$ uniformly with a shrinking radius, and the followers only average~\cite{Castelli2022}. LX-SSA~\cite{Solanki2023} gives each follower a second candidate $Y_m^i=X_m^i+\gamma(H_m-X_m^i)$ with the Laplace step~\cite{Deep2007}
\begin{equation}
\gamma=\phi-\chi\,\mathrm{sgn}\bigl(z-\tfrac12\bigr)\ln\bigl(1-2\lvert z-\tfrac12\rvert\bigr),\quad z\sim U(0,1),
\label{eq:laplace}
\end{equation}
$\phi=0$, $\chi=1$, and keeps the better candidate. As published, it evaluates both candidates of each follower and then re-evaluates the kept ones with the new population, so an iteration costs $2N_p$ evaluations and LX-SSA runs half as many iterations as SSA; comparing the two tests LX-SSA as published, not the Laplace step alone. Pseudocode: Section~\ref{S-sec:S-ssa}.

\subsection{Basic Variable Neighborhood Search}
\label{sec:vns}
The basic VNS~\cite{Mladenovic1997,Hansen2001}, in the continuous form of~\cite{Mladenovic2008}, improves one incumbent $\mathbf X$ with $k_{\max}=5$ nested $\ell_\infty$ neighborhoods $\mathcal N_k(\mathbf X)=\{\mathbf X':\delta_{k-1}<\lVert \mathbf X'-\mathbf X\rVert_\infty\le\delta_k\}$, $\delta_k=0.1\,k\,r$, of the whole layout. Shaking draws a uniform point of $\mathcal N_k(\mathbf X)$, clipped to the box, and a best-improvement local search improves it. As the top-hat wake makes $F_p$ discontinuous, this is a compass search: it evaluates all $2n$ moves $\pm h\,\mathbf e_m$ ($\mathbf e_m$ the $m$th unit vector), moves to the best improving one, and halves $h$ (from $0.05r$) when none improves, until $h<10^{-3}r$.

\section{Two-Phase Swarm--VNS Design and PSO-VNS}
\label{sec:hybrid}

\subsection{Two-Phase Template}
\label{sec:template}
All hybrids follow one sequential (relay) template. For a budget of $B$ evaluations and a split $\omega\in(0,1)$, Phase~1 starts from the seeded initial population, runs a population method for about $\omega B$ evaluations and passes its best layout to Phase~2, the basic VNS, which applies the local search to it and then iterates until $B$ evaluations are used. Only Phase~1 differs: PSO in \emph{PSO-VNS}; SSA or LX-SSA in \emph{SSA-VNS} and \emph{LX-SSA-VNS} (with $\xi_1$ compressed to the Phase-1 iterations); and, in the controls, $\operatorname{round}(\omega B)$ layouts: the initial population, then layouts drawn uniformly in the farm disc (\emph{RSD-VNS}) or in its bounding square (\emph{RS-VNS}). VNS alone is the limiting case in which Phase~1 is the initial population. PSO and SSA run for $T_1=\operatorname{round}((\omega B-N_p)/N_p)$ Phase-1 iterations, LX-SSA for $\operatorname{round}((\omega B-N_p)/(2N_p))$. A swarm phase is useful only if it gives VNS a better start than random sampling with about the same number of evaluations (at $\omega=0.5$, 3,015 for the controls and $N_p(T_1+1)=3{,}030$ for the swarms); the component analysis (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}) tests this for each Phase-1 choice against RSD-VNS, with RS-VNS as a weak minimum bar: a square sample is feasible with probability at most $(\pi/4)^N$ and, mainly because of the spacing, below $10^{-6}$ for the largest cases (Table~\ref{S-tab:S-th-box}); \NRSRunsNoFeasSample{} RS-VNS runs have no feasible sample, and sampling in the disc gives one in only \NRsSquareExplains{} of them.

\subsection{PSO-VNS}
PSO-VNS (Algorithm~\ref{alg:hybrid}) is not a new PSO variant: its Phase~1 is the plain PSO of Section~\ref{sec:pso} and, as the coefficients do not depend on the iteration, reproduces exactly, for a given seed, the first $N_p(T_1+1)$ evaluations of a PSO run; comparing PSO-VNS with PSO thus isolates the effect of Phase~2.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{PSO-VNS for a prescribed turbine count $N$}
\label{alg:hybrid}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require budget $B$, split $\omega$, $N_p$, $w$, $c_1$, $c_2$, radius $r$, $k_{\max}$
\State Draw and evaluate $N_p$ particles $\mathbf X^i$; $\mathbf V^i\gets\mathbf 0$; $\mathbf P^i\gets\mathbf X^i$; $\mathbf G\gets\arg\min_i F_p(\mathbf P^i)$ \Comment{Phase 1}
\For{$t=1,\ldots,T_1$ and $i=1,\ldots,N_p$}
    \State Move $\mathbf X^i$ by \eqref{eq:pso-v}, \eqref{eq:pso-x}; box repair; evaluate $F_p(\mathbf X^i)$; update $\mathbf P^i$, $\mathbf G$
\EndFor
\State $\mathbf X\gets\textsc{LocalSearch}(\mathbf G)$; $k\gets1$ \Comment{Phase 2}
\While{fewer than $B$ evaluations have been used}
    \State $\mathbf X'\gets\textsc{LocalSearch}(\text{uniform point of }\mathcal N_k(\mathbf X))$
    \State \textbf{if} $F_p(\mathbf X')<F_p(\mathbf X)$ \textbf{then} $\mathbf X\gets \mathbf X'$, $k\gets1$ \textbf{else} $k\gets k\bmod k_{\max}+1$
\EndWhile
\State \Return best layout evaluated
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


PSO-VNS uses $N_p=30$, the coefficients \eqref{eq:constriction} and the VNS settings above, and adds one parameter, the split $\omega$. With the default $\omega=0.5$ and $B=6{,}030$, PSO runs for $T_1=100$ iterations (99.5 rounded half to even) and VNS receives the remaining 3,000 evaluations. None of these values was tuned; Section~\ref{sec:split} studies $\omega$. At this budget, PSO-VNS is essentially PSO plus one compass-search descent: the first descent is unfinished in \NDDescentUnfinishedLargePSOVNS{} of \NDRunsLargePSOVNS{} runs with $N\ge12$, and shaking-plus-search cycles take \NDCycleEvalsPctPSOVNS\% of the Phase-2 evaluations (pooled; median run \NDCycleEvalsPctMedianPSOVNS\%) for \NDCycleSharePSOVNS\% of the Phase-2 gain (accepted cycles), so the neighborhoods matter only for small $N$.
