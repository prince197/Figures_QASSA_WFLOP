\subsection{PSO and Its Stability Conditions}
\label{sec:pso}
We use the canonical global-best PSO~\cite{Kennedy1995} in the inertia-weight form~\cite{Shi1998} (the standard PSO of~\cite{Bratton2007} uses a ring topology instead). Particle $i$ has a position $\mathbf X^i$, a velocity $\mathbf V^i$ and a personal best $\mathbf P^i$, the swarm keeps the global best $\mathbf G$, and a particle moves by
\begin{align}
\mathbf V^i&\leftarrow w\,\mathbf V^i+c_1\boldsymbol\rho_1\circ\bigl(\mathbf P^i-\mathbf X^i\bigr)+c_2\boldsymbol\rho_2\circ\bigl(\mathbf G-\mathbf X^i\bigr),\label{eq:pso-v}\\
\mathbf X^i&\leftarrow\mathbf X^i+\mathbf V^i,\label{eq:pso-x}
\end{align}
where $\boldsymbol\rho_1,\boldsymbol\rho_2$ are vectors of independent $U(0,1)$ numbers, drawn anew for every move, and $\circ$ is the componentwise product. The constriction PSO of Clerc and Kennedy~\cite{Clerc2002} with their standard choice $\varphi_1=\varphi_2=2.05$ (constriction factor $\kappa=0.7298$) is identical to \eqref{eq:pso-v} with
\begin{equation}
w=0.7298,\qquad c_1=c_2=\kappa\varphi_1=1.49618 .
\label{eq:constriction}
\end{equation}
PSO denotes \eqref{eq:pso-v}, \eqref{eq:pso-x} with these coefficients, fixed a priori from the literature, and the \emph{old setting} the same algorithm with $w=0.7$, $c_1=c_2=2$, i.e., the acceleration coefficients of the original PSO~\cite{Kennedy1995} combined with an inertia weight. Velocities start at zero and are not clamped; a coordinate clipped to the square (Section~\ref{sec:constraints}) keeps its velocity (no reflection or damping); $\mathbf P^i$ and $\mathbf G$ are updated after each evaluation.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:pso-stability}
Under stagnation (fixed $\mathbf P^i$, $\mathbf G$) and without active bounds, the expected position converges to $(c_1\mathbf P^i+c_2\mathbf G)/(c_1+c_2)$ for all initial values if and only if $\lvert w\rvert<1$ and $0<c_1+c_2<4(1+w)$ (order-1 stability; $(c_1+c_2)/2=\mathrm E(c_1\rho_1+c_2\rho_2)$). For $c_1=c_2$, the second moments converge as well (order-2 stability), with a limit variance $\propto(P^i_m-G_m)^2$ in coordinate $m$, if and only if, in addition~\cite{Poli2009},
\begin{equation}
c_1+c_2<\frac{24\,(1-w^2)}{7-5w}.
\label{eq:poli}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
Proofs, also for $c_1\ne c_2$ (stricter bound): Section~\ref{S-sec:S-th-pso} of the supplementary material; the region also holds under a weaker, non-stagnant assumption~\cite{Cleghorn2018}. The constriction setting is order-2 stable ($c_1+c_2=2.99<3.35$); the old setting is order-1 stable ($4<6.8$) but not order-2 stable ($4>3.50$): its second moments grow by the factor 1.12 per iteration (Fig.~\ref{S-fig:S-th-stability}). Neither condition implies that $\mathbf G$ is good. With bounds, a turbine with a clipped coordinate lies outside the farm (Lemma~\ref{S-lem:S-clip}). In our runs (Fig.~\ref{S-fig:D-psodyn}), the old swarm does not contract (final spread \NDSpreadRatio{} times, speed \NDVelRatio{} times the constriction values), clips \NDClipOld\% of the coordinates per iteration (constriction \NDClipNew\%) and evaluates few feasible layouts (\NDFeasPartOld\% vs.\ \NDFeasPartNew\% in the second half); the \NDStoredInfeasOld{} infeasible final layouts of its runs mostly violate only the spacing (\NDStoredSpacingOnlyOld; Section~\ref{sec:constraints}); the setting changes the ranking (Section~\ref{sec:psosetting}). Likewise, the DE scale factor 0.5 (Section~\ref{sec:setup}) exceeds Zaharie's critical value $\sqrt{(1-\mathrm{CR}/2)/N_p}=0.14$ ($\mathrm{CR}=0.9$)~\cite{Zaharie2002}.

\subsection{Salp Swarm Algorithm and Its Laplacian Variant}
\label{sec:ssa}
The SSA~\cite{Mirjalili2017} keeps the best solution found so far as the food source $\mathbf H$ and splits the sorted population into leaders and followers. A leader $i\le N_p/2$ updates each coordinate by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leader}
X_m^i = H_m\pm\xi_1\big((ub_m-lb_m)\xi_2+lb_m\big),
\end{equation}
with either sign with probability $1/2$, $\xi_2\sim U(0,1)$ and $\xi_1=2\exp[-(4t/T)^2]$ decreasing with the iteration $t$ out of $T$; a follower $i>N_p/2$ moves to the midpoint of its position and the new position of the preceding salp. The leaders thus perturb $\mathbf H$ uniformly with a shrinking radius, and the followers only average~\cite{Castelli2022}. LX-SSA~\cite{Solanki2023} gives each follower a second candidate $Y_m^i=X_m^i+\gamma(H_m-X_m^i)$ with the Laplace step~\cite{Deep2007}
\begin{equation}
\gamma=\phi-\chi\,\mathrm{sgn}\bigl(z-\tfrac12\bigr)\ln\bigl(1-2\lvert z-\tfrac12\rvert\bigr),\quad z\sim U(0,1),
\label{eq:laplace}
\end{equation}
$\phi=0$, $\chi=1$, and keeps the better candidate. As published, it evaluates both candidates of each follower and then re-evaluates the kept ones with the new population, so an iteration costs $2N_p$ evaluations and LX-SSA runs half as many iterations as SSA; comparing the two tests LX-SSA as published, not the Laplace step alone. Pseudocode: Section~\ref{S-sec:S-ssa} of the supplementary material.

\subsection{Basic Variable Neighborhood Search}
\label{sec:vns}
The basic VNS~\cite{Mladenovic1997,Hansen2001}, in the continuous form of~\cite{Mladenovic2008}, improves one incumbent $\mathbf X$ with $k_{\max}=5$ nested $\ell_\infty$ neighborhoods $\mathcal N_k(\mathbf X)=\{\mathbf X':\delta_{k-1}<\lVert \mathbf X'-\mathbf X\rVert_\infty\le\delta_k\}$, $\delta_k=0.1\,k\,r$, of the whole layout. Shaking draws a point uniformly from $\mathcal N_k(\mathbf X)$ (box-repaired), and a best-improvement local search improves it. As the top-hat wake makes $F_p$ discontinuous, this is a compass search: it evaluates all $2n$ moves $\pm h\,\mathbf e_m$ ($\mathbf e_m$ the $m$th unit vector), moves to the best improving one, and halves $h$ (from $0.05r$) when none improves, until $h<10^{-3}r$.

\section{Two-Phase Swarm--VNS Design and PSO-VNS}
\label{sec:hybrid}

\subsection{Two-Phase Template}
\label{sec:template}
All hybrids here follow one sequential (relay) template. For a budget of $B$ evaluations and a split $\omega\in(0,1)$, Phase~1 starts from the seeded initial population, runs a population method for about $\omega B$ evaluations and passes its best layout to Phase~2, the basic VNS, which applies the local search to it and then iterates until $B$ evaluations are used. Only Phase~1 differs: PSO in \emph{PSO-VNS}; SSA or LX-SSA in \emph{SSA-VNS} and \emph{LX-SSA-VNS} (with $\xi_1$ compressed to the Phase-1 iterations, so, unlike PSO, not the start of a stand-alone run); and $\operatorname{round}(\omega B)$ layouts drawn uniformly in the bounding square, including the initial population, in the control \emph{RS-VNS}. VNS alone is the limiting case in which Phase~1 is the initial population. PSO and SSA run for $T_1=\operatorname{round}((\omega B-N_p)/N_p)$ Phase-1 iterations, LX-SSA for $\operatorname{round}((\omega B-N_p)/(2N_p))$. A swarm phase is useful only if it gives VNS a better start than random sampling with about the same number of evaluations (at $\omega=0.5$, 3,015 for RS-VNS and $N_p(T_1+1)=3{,}030$ for the swarms); the component analysis (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}) tests this for each Phase-1 choice, with RS-VNS as a weak, no-search minimum bar.
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:box}
A layout drawn uniformly in the bounding square has all $N$ turbines inside the farm with probability $(\pi/4)^N$; its feasibility probability is smaller (Proposition~\ref{S-prop:S-box}).
\end{proposition}
For the largest cases, it is below $10^{-6}$ (Table~\ref{S-tab:S-th-box}): Phase~1 of RS-VNS almost never contains a feasible layout (none in \NDRsRunsNoFeasSample{} of its runs) and passes its least-violating sample to VNS.

\subsection{PSO-VNS}
PSO-VNS (Algorithm~\ref{alg:hybrid}) is not a new PSO variant: Phase~1 is the plain PSO of Section~\ref{sec:pso}. Since the coefficients do not depend on the iteration, Phase~1 reproduces exactly, for a given seed, the first $N_p(T_1+1)$ evaluations of a PSO run, so comparing PSO-VNS with PSO isolates the use of the second part of the budget.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{PSO-VNS for a prescribed turbine count $N$}
\label{alg:hybrid}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require budget $B$, split $\omega$, $N_p$, $w$, $c_1$, $c_2$, radius $r$, $k_{\max}$
\State Draw and evaluate $N_p$ particles $\mathbf X^i$; $\mathbf V^i\gets\mathbf 0$; $\mathbf P^i\gets\mathbf X^i$; $\mathbf G\gets\arg\min_i F_p(\mathbf P^i)$ \Comment{Phase 1}
\For{$t=1,\ldots,T_1$ and $i=1,\ldots,N_p$}
    \State Move $\mathbf X^i$ by \eqref{eq:pso-v}, \eqref{eq:pso-x}; box repair; evaluate $F_p(\mathbf X^i)$; update $\mathbf P^i$, $\mathbf G$
\EndFor
\State $\mathbf X\gets\textsc{LocalSearch}(\mathbf G)$; $k\gets1$ \Comment{Phase 2}
\While{fewer than $B$ evaluations have been used}
    \State $\mathbf X'\gets\textsc{LocalSearch}(\text{uniform point of }\mathcal N_k(\mathbf X))$
    \State \textbf{if} $F_p(\mathbf X')<F_p(\mathbf X)$ \textbf{then} $\mathbf X\gets \mathbf X'$, $k\gets1$ \textbf{else} $k\gets k\bmod k_{\max}+1$
\EndWhile
\State \Return best layout evaluated
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


PSO-VNS uses $N_p=30$, the coefficients \eqref{eq:constriction} and the VNS settings above, and adds one parameter, the split $\omega$. With the default $\omega=0.5$ and $B=6{,}030$, PSO runs for $T_1=100$ iterations (99.5 rounded up) and VNS receives the remaining 3,000 evaluations. None of these values was tuned; Section~\ref{sec:split} studies $\omega$. At this budget, Phase~2 is essentially one compass-search descent from $\mathbf G$ (median \NDShakesPSOVNS{} shaking step, \NDShakeEvalsPctPSOVNS\% of the Phase-2 evaluations; the first descent gives \NDDescentSharePSOVNS\% of the Phase-2 gain and is unfinished in \NDDescentUnfinishedLargePSOVNS{} of \NDRunsLargePSOVNS{} runs with $N\ge12$; Section~\ref{sec:ablation}); its neighborhoods matter only for small $N$.
