\section{Preliminaries: Phase-1 Methods and Basic VNS}
\label{sec:prelim}
This section describes the Phase-1 population methods and the basic VNS. A population method keeps $N_p$ candidate vectors $\mathbf X^i\in\mathbb R^{n}$ with components $X^i_m$ and bounds $lb_m$, $ub_m$, and it costs $N_p$ objective calls per iteration unless stated otherwise.

\subsection{Particle Swarm Optimization and Its Convergence Condition}
\label{sec:pso}
We use the standard global-best PSO~\cite{Kennedy1995} with unmodified update rules. Particle $i$ has a position $\mathbf X^i$, a velocity $\mathbf V^i$ and a personal best $\mathbf P^i$, the swarm keeps the global best $\mathbf G$, and in the inertia-weight form~\cite{Shi1998} a particle moves by
\begin{align}
\mathbf V^i&\leftarrow w\,\mathbf V^i+c_1\boldsymbol\rho_1\circ\bigl(\mathbf P^i-\mathbf X^i\bigr)+c_2\boldsymbol\rho_2\circ\bigl(\mathbf G-\mathbf X^i\bigr),\label{eq:pso-v}\\
\mathbf X^i&\leftarrow\mathbf X^i+\mathbf V^i,\label{eq:pso-x}
\end{align}
where $\boldsymbol\rho_1,\boldsymbol\rho_2$ are vectors of independent $U(0,1)$ numbers, drawn anew for every move and coordinate, and $\circ$ is the componentwise product. Clerc and Kennedy~\cite{Clerc2002} multiply the whole velocity update by the constriction factor $\kappa=2/\lvert2-\varphi-\sqrt{\varphi^2-4\varphi}\rvert$, $\varphi=\varphi_1+\varphi_2>4$, where $\varphi_1,\varphi_2$ take the place of $c_1,c_2$ and the inertia weight is 1. Their standard choice $\varphi_1=\varphi_2=2.05$ gives $\kappa=0.7298$, which is mathematically identical to \eqref{eq:pso-v} with
\begin{equation}
w=0.7298,\qquad c_1=c_2=\kappa\varphi_1=1.49618 .
\label{eq:constriction}
\end{equation}
Throughout the paper, PSO denotes \eqref{eq:pso-v}, \eqref{eq:pso-x} with these coefficients, and the \emph{old setting} the same algorithm with $w=0.7$, $c_1=c_2=2$; only the coefficient values differ. The velocities start at zero and are not clamped, the positions are clipped to the bounding square after each move (box repair), and $\mathbf P^i$ and $\mathbf G$ are updated after each particle evaluation (asynchronous update).

When the personal and global bests stay fixed (stagnation), the mean and the variance of the particle positions converge if $\lvert w\rvert<1$ and~\cite{Poli2009}
\begin{equation}
c_1+c_2<\frac{24\,(1-w^2)}{7-5w}.
\label{eq:poli}
\end{equation}
For the constriction setting~\eqref{eq:constriction}, the right-hand side equals 3.35 and $c_1+c_2=2.99$ satisfies~\eqref{eq:poli}. The old setting, which is common in PSO baselines for the WFLOP and was used in the earlier version of this study, violates it: for $w=0.7$ the bound is 3.50, whereas $c_1+c_2=4$. The variance of the positions then grows even when the bests no longer change, and the swarm keeps sampling far from its best layouts instead of contracting around them. This is a property of the setting, not of PSO; Section~\ref{sec:psosetting} shows that it changes the ranking of the compared methods.

\subsection{Salp Swarm Algorithm and Its Laplacian Variant}
\label{sec:ssa}
The SSA~\cite{Mirjalili2017} keeps the best solution found so far as the food source $\mathbf H$ and splits the sorted population into leaders and followers. A leader $i\le N_p/2$ updates each coordinate by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leader}
X_m^i = H_m\pm\xi_1\big((ub_m-lb_m)\xi_2+lb_m\big),
\end{equation}
with either sign with probability $1/2$, $\xi_2\sim U(0,1)$ and $\xi_1=2\exp[-(4t/T)^2]$ decreasing with the iteration $t$ out of $T$; a follower $i>N_p/2$ moves to the midpoint $\tfrac{1}{2}(X_m^i+X_m^{i-1})$ between itself and the preceding salp. LX-SSA~\cite{Solanki2023} gives each follower a second candidate $Y_m^i=X_m^i+\gamma(H_m-X_m^i)$ with the Laplace step~\cite{Deep2007}
\begin{equation}
\gamma=\phi-\chi\,\mathrm{sgn}\bigl(z-\tfrac12\bigr)\ln\bigl(1-2\lvert z-\tfrac12\rvert\bigr),\quad z\sim U(0,1),
\label{eq:laplace}
\end{equation}
location $\phi=0$ and scale $\chi=1$, and keeps the better candidate, so that one iteration costs $2N_p$ calls. Comparing SSA with LX-SSA isolates the Laplace step. Pseudocode is given in Section~\ref{S-sec:S-ssa} of the supplementary material.

\subsection{Basic Variable Neighborhood Search}
\label{sec:vns}
The basic VNS~\cite{Mladenovic1997,Hansen2001}, in the continuous form of~\cite{Mladenovic2008}, improves one incumbent $\mathbf X$. It uses $k_{\max}=5$ nested $\ell_\infty$ neighborhoods $\mathcal N_k(\mathbf X)=\{\mathbf X':\delta_{k-1}<\lVert \mathbf X'-\mathbf X\rVert_\infty\le\delta_k\}$, $\delta_k=0.1\,k\,r$, of the whole layout. Shaking draws a point uniformly from $\mathcal N_k(\mathbf X)$ (box-repaired), and a complete best-improvement local search improves it. Since the top-hat Jensen wake makes $F_p$ discontinuous, the local search is a derivative-free compass search: it evaluates all $2n$ moves $\pm h\,\mathbf e_m$, moves to the best improving one, and halves $h$ (from $0.05r$) when none improves, until $h<10^{-3}r$. A better local minimum replaces the incumbent and resets $k$ to 1; otherwise $k$ is increased cyclically. The stand-alone VNS starts from the best member of the initial population.

\section{Two-Phase Swarm--VNS Design and PSO-VNS}
\label{sec:hybrid}

\subsection{Two-Phase Template}
\label{sec:template}
All hybrids in this paper follow one template. For a budget of $B$ objective calls and a split $\omega\in(0,1)$, Phase~1 starts from the seeded initial population, runs a population method for about $\omega B$ calls and passes its best layout to Phase~2, the basic VNS, which applies the local search to it and then iterates until $B$ calls are used. The instances differ only in Phase~1: PSO in \emph{PSO-VNS}; SSA or LX-SSA in \emph{SSA-VNS} and \emph{LX-SSA-VNS} ($\xi_1$ decreasing over the Phase-1 iterations); and pure random sampling of $\operatorname{round}(\omega B)$ layouts, uniform in the bounding square and including the initial population, in the control \emph{RS-VNS}. VNS alone is the limiting case in which Phase~1 is the initial population. PSO and SSA run for $T_1=\operatorname{round}((\omega B-N_p)/N_p)$ Phase-1 iterations, since the initial population costs $N_p$ calls and every iteration another $N_p$, and LX-SSA for $\operatorname{round}((\omega B-N_p)/(2N_p))$. A swarm phase is useful only if it gives VNS a better start than random sampling with the same number of calls; the component analysis (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}) tests this for each Phase-1 choice and, by comparing SSA-VNS with LX-SSA-VNS, isolates the Laplace step.

\subsection{PSO-VNS}
PSO-VNS is not a new PSO variant: Phase~1 is the plain PSO of Section~\ref{sec:pso}, and its final global best $\mathbf G$ is the starting point of the basic VNS (Algorithm~\ref{alg:hybrid}). Both phases minimize $F_p$~\eqref{eq:penalty} over the box-repaired layout vectors $\mathbf X\in[-r,r]^{2N}$, so the feasibility-first rules of Section~\ref{sec:constraints} hold in every comparison, best update and acceptance step. We use PSO in Phase~1 because, with coefficients that satisfy~\eqref{eq:poli}, the swarm contracts around a good region within its share of the budget, so that VNS starts from a refined layout. Since the coefficients do not depend on the iteration, Phase~1 reproduces exactly, for a given seed, the first $N_p(T_1+1)$ calls of a PSO run; PSO-VNS and PSO differ only in how they spend the rest of the budget, and comparing them isolates the use of its second part.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{PSO-VNS for a prescribed turbine count $N$}
\label{alg:hybrid}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require budget $B$, split $\omega$, $N_p$, $w$, $c_1$, $c_2$, radius $r$, $k_{\max}$
\State Draw and evaluate $N_p$ particles $\mathbf X^i$; $\mathbf V^i\gets\mathbf 0$; $\mathbf P^i\gets\mathbf X^i$; $\mathbf G\gets\arg\min_i F_p(\mathbf P^i)$ \Comment{Phase 1}
\For{$t=1,\ldots,T_1$ and $i=1,\ldots,N_p$}
    \State Move $\mathbf X^i$ by \eqref{eq:pso-v}, \eqref{eq:pso-x}; box repair; evaluate $F_p(\mathbf X^i)$; update $\mathbf P^i$, $\mathbf G$
\EndFor
\State $\mathbf X\gets\textsc{LocalSearch}(\mathbf G)$; $k\gets1$ \Comment{Phase 2}
\While{fewer than $B$ objective calls have been used}
    \State $\mathbf X'\gets\textsc{LocalSearch}(\text{uniform point of }\mathcal N_k(\mathbf X))$
    \State \textbf{if} $F_p(\mathbf X')<F_p(\mathbf X)$ \textbf{then} $\mathbf X\gets \mathbf X'$, $k\gets1$ \textbf{else} $k\gets k\bmod k_{\max}+1$
\EndWhile
\State \Return best layout evaluated
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


PSO-VNS uses $N_p=30$, the coefficients \eqref{eq:constriction} and the VNS settings above, and adds one parameter, the split $\omega$. With the default $\omega=0.5$ and $B=6{,}030$, PSO runs for $T_1=100$ iterations ($30+100\times30=3{,}030$ calls) and VNS receives the remaining 3,000. None of these values was tuned; Section~\ref{sec:split} studies $\omega$. Both phases are dominated by the objective evaluations, so PSO-VNS has the same $\mathcal O(B\,N^2N_\theta N_s)$ cost as the other methods. Phase~1 draws the initial population first, so runs with the same seed start from the same population as all other methods and are paired.
