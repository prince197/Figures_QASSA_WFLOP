# R1 — Handling Editor assessment (MPCE)

Manuscript: "Revisiting Metaheuristic Comparisons for Continuous Wind Farm Layout Optimization: Baseline Configuration, Component Analysis and a Two-Phase PSO-VNS Design" (compiled MPCE_PSO_VNS.pdf, 12 pp.; sources optA/01_front.tex … optA/11_back.tex).

## Overall verdict (5 lines)

1. **As compiled now: desk rejection.** The PDF still has red `[TBD]` placeholders: dates, funding, corresponding author, Zenodo DOI, all four biographies, an unverified IEA37 footnote and "pending author verification" in Sec. VIII-D. An editorial office returns such a file without review.
2. **Scope risk is also real (moderate to high).** The core is a methodology study of metaheuristics on the stylized Kusiak–Song benchmark: 2–15 turbines, a linear power curve with no cut-out, 4D spacing and objective values in "benchmark units". Power-system relevance (energy yield, grid, cost) is stated in one sentence and never quantified. An MPCE editor could send it to an evolutionary-computation or wind-energy-science journal.
3. **The science is careful and the claims are unusually honest.** Equal budgets, seed pairing, Holm correction, an RS-VNS control and openly stated limits make the paper stronger than a typical "new hybrid wins" WFLOP submission. The messages M1–M3 are consistent across abstract, introduction, results and conclusion.
4. **The main framing problems:**
   - M1 rests on an uncited "earlier version of this study", i.e., the authors' own rejected MPCE submission.
   - "Common in WFLOP comparisons" has no citation.
   - A few claims go beyond the tests: "add nothing", "better for N ≥ 10", "exceeds the installed Horns Rev 1", "under a Gaussian wake model".
   - The protocol is presented as a contribution, but it is established practice.
5. **Recommendation: fix issues 1–8, then send out for review (major revision track likely).** The abstract is ~240 words: within a 250-word limit but over the internal 230-word target. At 12 pages the length is at the upper end. Merge Sections X–XII and trim the nomenclature to reach 11 pages.

## Issues (most important first)

1. **[major] optA/01_front.tex, optA/08_beyond.tex, optA/11_back.tex** — "Manuscript received: [TBD: date] … [TBD: mark corresponding author] … [TBD: Zenodo DOI] … [TBD: biography]" — **Problem:** the red placeholders are visible in the PDF. Further examples: the IEA37 footnote of Table VIII ("[TBD: verify against Baker et al. (2019)]", repeated in Sec. XI) and Sec. VIII-D ("[TBD: pending author verification of the PDFs]"). A file in this state is returned without review. The Data Availability statement without a DOI also undercuts contribution 1 ("public code and data"). **Fix:** resolve every `\TBD` before submission.
   - Dates: leave empty for the MPCE office.
   - Funding: "This work did not receive any specific grant from funding agencies."
   - Deposit the code and data on Zenodo and insert the DOI.
   - Verify the IEA37 participant and remove the TBD from Table VIII and Sec. XI.
   - Either complete the Kusiak–Song literature comparison or delete the sentence in Sec. VIII-D.
   - Write the biographies.

2. **[major] optA/06_results.tex (Table I caption and Sec. VI-A), optA/04_methods.tex, optA/02_intro.tex** — "EFFECT OF THE PSO SETTING ON THE COMPARISON OF THE PREVIOUS STUDY'S METHODS" / "used in the earlier version of this study" — **Problem:** the "previous study" and "earlier version" are the authors' own unpublished, reviewed MPCE submission. Readers and reviewers cannot see it, cannot cite it, and will be confused. It also exposes the review history inside the paper. **Fix:** remove every mention of "earlier version / previous study".
   - Table I caption: "Effect of the PSO setting on a comparison of salp-swarm hybrids with standard baselines".
   - Sec. VI-A: "We ran PSO with the setting w = 0.7, c1 = c2 = 2 on the same cases, budget and seeds."
   - Mention the history only in the cover letter.

3. **[major] optA/01_front.tex, optA/04_methods.tex, optA/06_results.tex** — "the particle swarm optimization (PSO) setting w = 0.7, c1 = c2 = 2, common in WFLOP comparisons" — **Problem:** M1 depends on this setting being "common", but no WFLOP paper that uses it is cited anywhere. Without evidence, M1 is a finding about the authors' own earlier code. Citing papers, on the other hand, can read as an attack. **Fix:** support the claim neutrally, e.g., "a setting that lies outside the convergence region (9) and has been used in WFLOP studies, e.g., [x], [y]". Cite 2–4 verified WFLOP papers without comment. If this cannot be documented, soften to: "a setting that is easy to adopt from the original PSO papers (c1 = c2 = 2) and lies outside the convergence region".

4. **[major] optA/02_intro.tex (paragraph 1), optA/01_front.tex** — "so the layout sets the wake loss and thereby the energy yield on which capacity planning and the design of the collector and grid connection rely" — **Problem:** this is the only power-system motivation. Everything after it concerns benchmarking methodology. No result is translated into energy terms. An MPCE editor will ask what a 0.33 percentage-point wake-loss difference means for a plant. **Fix:**
   - Add one sentence to the introduction and one clause to the abstract that state the practical stake in AEP. From the paper's own numbers (Eideal = 8202.7 MWh/yr per turbine; Data Set I, N = 15, farm ≈ 123 GWh/yr), 0.33 pp ≈ 0.4 GWh/yr and 0.018 pp ≈ 0.02 GWh/yr.
   - Suggested text: "An unreliable comparison can mislead a layout choice by hundreds of MWh per year even for a 15-turbine block, and for large offshore farms the effect scales with capacity."
   - Add an AEP-difference column (MWh/yr) to Table II, or a sentence in Sec. VI-C.

5. **[major] optA/01_front.tex (title and abstract), optA/02_intro.tex (contribution 1)** — "A controlled evaluation protocol for the continuous WFLOP: equal budgets … seed-paired runs … Holm correction" — **Problem:** each element is established practice in computational-intelligence benchmarking (Demšar 2006; Derrac et al. 2011; Bartz-Beielstein et al. 2020). Calling it a contribution invites the reviewer response "not new". The new part is applying it to WFLOP and showing that it changes conclusions. **Fix:** reword contribution 1 as "The application of established benchmarking practice (equal budgets…, [21], [22], [25]) to continuous WFLOP, with two WFLOP-specific elements, feasibility-aware ranking and a random-sampling control for hybrids, applied to …". Consider citing J. Derrac, S. García, D. Molina and F. Herrera, "A practical tutorial on the use of nonparametric statistical tests…," Swarm Evol. Comput., vol. 1, no. 1, pp. 3–18, 2011.

6. **[major] optA/01_front.tex, optA/02_intro.tex (contribution 4)** — "exceeds the energy yield of the installed Horns Rev 1 block" — **Problem:** this overclaims.
   - At 6,030 evaluations the mean AEP of PSO-VNS (138.73 GWh/yr) is below the installed layout (139.51 GWh/yr). Only 6 of 30 runs exceed it; the mean exceeds it only at 30,030 evaluations.
   - The comparison uses the authors' Jensen model with K = 0.04 and ignores the cabling and installation constraints that shaped the real 7D grid.
   - Power-system readers will read the sentence as "we improve Horns Rev".

   **Fix (abstract):** "…and, at 30,030 evaluations and under the same Jensen model, its mean AEP exceeds that of the installed Horns Rev 1 16-turbine block". **Fix (intro):** "its best layout at 6,030 and its mean at 30,030 evaluations exceed the modeled AEP of the installed block".

7. **[major] optA/01_front.tex, optA/02_intro.tex** — "PSO ranks first under a Gaussian wake model" — **Problem:** the layouts were only re-evaluated with the Gaussian model, not re-optimized (Sec. IX). The Conclusion and Sec. VI-B say "Gaussian re-evaluation", so the messages are inconsistent, and the abstract implies a Gaussian optimization study. **Fix:** in both places, write "PSO ranks first when the final layouts are re-evaluated with a Gaussian wake model".

8. **[major] optA/01_front.tex (abstract), optA/02_intro.tex (thesis), optA/10_limits_concl.tex** — "salp swarm phases and the Laplace step add nothing" / "add nothing beyond random sampling" — **Problem:** statistical ties (2/66/0, 3/65/0) are not proof of zero effect. LX-SSA vs. SSA is 0/61/7, so the Laplace step is worse in 7 cases rather than neutral. Because two of the authors proposed LX-SSA, the wording also reads as a verdict on the SSA literature. **Fix:** "the salp-swarm phases show no significant advantage over random sampling of equal cost, and the Laplace step gives no gain (and is worse in 7 of 68 cases without VNS)". In the thesis, use "…add no detectable benefit beyond random sampling on this benchmark".

9. **[major] optA/06_results.tex (Sec. VI-B), optA/10_limits_concl.tex (Conclusion)** — "at least as good as a correctly configured PSO and better for N ≥ 10" / "is better than PSO for N ≥ 10" — **Problem:** "better for N ≥ 10" is a post hoc subgroup statement. It rests on 9 wins and 2 losses in 20 cases, with a mean gain of 0.08 pp, and no subgroup test is reported. **Fix:** either report a Holm-consistent case-mean Wilcoxon test for N ≥ 10, or write "tends to be better for N ≥ 10 (significantly better in 9 of the 20 cases, worse in 2; mean gain 0.08 percentage points)".

10. **[major] scope/benchmark — optA/03_model.tex, optA/05_setup.tex** — "it is slightly negative just above the cut-in speed, jumps from 1472 to 1500 kW at the rated speed and has no cut-out speed" — **Problem:** a power-systems audience will question conclusions drawn from a physically defective power curve and 2–15 turbine circular farms. The cubic re-evaluation shifts the energy by 17–37%. The paper keeps the benchmark for comparability, but the main tables stay entirely in benchmark units. **Fix:**
    - In the introduction, justify the benchmark explicitly: "We keep the Kusiak–Song benchmark because most published WFLOP metaheuristic comparisons use it, so its conclusions are the ones at stake."
    - Move the statement that the ranking is stable under the cubic curves (τ̄ = 0.95) earlier, into Sec. VI-B.
    - Present Horns Rev 1 and IEA37 as the practically relevant cases.

11. **[major] optA/01_front.tex (abstract), optA/10_limits_concl.tex (Sec. X)** — "with it, PSO ranks fifth … whereas with the constriction setting PSO ranks second" — **Problem:** the abstract mixes two method pools. "Fifth" and "second" refer to the eight-method main comparison (Table II), while Table I, the actual M1 evidence, shows positions 5 → 1 in the other pool. Sec. X adds to the confusion: "PSO ranks second in the main comparison, PSO ranks first in that pool, and PSO is ahead of every salp-swarm method in both". **Fix:** use one pool in the abstract, preferably Table I: "with it, PSO ranks fifth of eight methods and is feasible in 86.0% of runs, and a salp-swarm hybrid ranks first; with the constriction setting, the same comparison ranks PSO first, ahead of every salp-swarm method". Rewrite Sec. X to: "Replacing the old setting by the constriction setting moves PSO from fifth to first in the same method pool (Table I)."

12. **[major] optA/05_setup.tex, optA/10_limits_concl.tex (recommendation 1)** — "configure every baseline inside its convergence region … and report all settings" — **Problem:** the paper's central lesson is applied only to PSO. DE (F = 0.5, CR = 0.9) is feasible in only 71.4% of runs and ranks last. MS-SLSQP uses forward differences on a discontinuous top-hat objective. Reviewers will ask whether DE and SLSQP are handicapped in the same way PSO was. **Fix:** add one or two sentences in Sec. V-A or XI: explain why these settings are standard (DE: Storn–Price defaults), state that DE's low feasibility comes from penalty-dominated search rather than from divergence, and say that DE and SLSQP were not tuned. Soften recommendation 1 to "use settings with documented convergence or standard defaults".

13. **[minor] optA/01_front.tex (title)** — "Revisiting Metaheuristic Comparisons for Continuous Wind Farm Layout Optimization: Baseline Configuration, Component Analysis and a Two-Phase PSO-VNS Design" — **Problem:** the title is 21 words with two subtitles. "Revisiting" suggests a re-examination of specific papers, which conflicts with "not a finding about any particular study". The brief specifies an en dash (PSO–VNS), but the PDF shows a hyphen. **Fix:** "Baseline Configuration and Component Analysis in Metaheuristic Wind Farm Layout Optimization: A Controlled Comparison with a PSO–VNS Reference Method" (18 words), or shorter: "Controlled Comparison of Metaheuristics for Wind Farm Layout Optimization and a PSO–VNS Reference Method".

14. **[minor] optA/01_front.tex (abstract length and first sentences)** — "The wind farm layout optimization problem (WFLOP) places a prescribed number of turbines to minimize wake loss…" — **Problem:** the abstract is ~240 words, over the 230-word target in the brief; check it against the current MPCE author guideline. It opens with a definition instead of a power or energy motivation. **Fix:** open with "Wind farm layouts fix the energy yield of a plant for its lifetime, yet published comparisons of layout optimizers are hard to trust." Delete "under boundary and spacing constraints" and "Eight methods are compared on", and merge the protocol list into one clause to save ~20 words.

15. **[minor] optA/02_intro.tex** — "Many of these studies propose a new or modified metaheuristic and show that it outperforms a set of reference methods." — **Problem:** together with the Sörensen "metaphor exposed" citation [21], this reads as a sweeping critique of the WFLOP literature, which includes MPCE papers such as [10]. **Fix:** "Many of these studies introduce a new or modified metaheuristic and compare it with reference methods; the benchmarking literature notes that the outcome of such comparisons depends on how they are set up [21], [22]."

16. **[minor] optA/06_results.tex (Sec. 18. **[minor] optA/08_beyond.tex (Sec.
