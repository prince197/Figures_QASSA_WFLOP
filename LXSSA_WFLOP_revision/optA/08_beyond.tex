\emph{Feasible starts.} With feasibility-preserving initialization, \NFeasInitPhrase. \NFbFeasBestRank{} alone is then best, with the lowest mean wake loss and the best average rank; PSO-VNS has an average rank of \NFbFeasRankPSOVNS{} and a mean wake loss of \NFbFeasLossPSOVNS\% (\NFbRandLossPSOVNS\% with random starts; Table~\ref{S-tab:feasinit-cases}); RS-VNS was not run with this initialization. PSO and DE never improve on the best initial layout in these runs. As the turbine labels are arbitrary, velocity and difference moves that combine two feasible layouts typically make turbines overlap; the feasibility-first comparison rejects them, and the global best of PSO does not move. The single-coordinate moves of VNS are not affected. From random starts, the value of PSO as Phase~1 may therefore lie mainly in reaching feasibility under the penalty rather than in a better search of the wake-loss landscape, and the advantage of PSO-VNS depends on the initialization.

\subsection{Horns Rev~1 16-Turbine Block}
\label{sec:hornsrev-site}
\input{analysis/mpce_tab_hr16.tex}

For this block, the parallelogram is enlarged by 0.1\% so that the installed turbines lie inside it, the boundary violation in~\eqref{eq:penalty} is the distance (m) outside it, and the search box has a half-width of 943~m, which is also the $r$ of the VNS radii; our model agrees with PyWake~\cite{PyWake} within \NHRPyWakeDiff\% for the full 80-turbine farm. The installed layout, a regular $7D$ grid, reaches \NHRInstalled~GWh/yr (wake loss \NHRInstalledLoss\%; Table~\ref{tab:hr-site}; layouts: Fig.~\ref{S-fig:S-hr16} of the supplementary material). At 6,030 evaluations, PSO-VNS is feasible in \NHRFeasPSOVNS{} runs and has the highest mean AEP, \NHRMeanPSOVNS~GWh/yr (SD \NHRSDPSOVNS); in the seed-paired run-level test (infeasible runs ranked last) it is significantly better than every other method ($p_{\rm Holm}\le\NHRMaxP$). Its best run reaches \NHRBestPSOVNS~GWh/yr, and \NHRAbovePSOVNS{} of its 30 runs exceed the installed layout; \NHRAboveOthersPhrase. At 30,030 evaluations, the loss of PSO-VNS falls to \NHRLossThirtyKPSOVNS\% (mean \NHRMeanThirtyKPSOVNS~GWh/yr; \NHRAboveThirtyKPSOVNS{} of 30 runs above the installed layout). At 120,030 evaluations (10 seeds), its loss of \NHRLossOneTwentyKPSOVNS\% is no longer the lowest. PSO finds a feasible layout in only \NHRFeasPSO{} runs from random starts (\NHRFeasFeasInitPSO{} with feasible starts). The optimized layouts may use the benchmark spacing $4D$, whereas the installed grid keeps $7D$ (no run with $7D$ was made); the block is evaluated without the wakes of the other 64 turbines; and cables, foundations, loads and navigation, which also shaped the installed grid, are not modeled. The comparison therefore tests the optimizers; it does not show that the installed layout could be improved.

In IEA37 Case Study~1 the participants chose their own optimizers and budgets~\cite{Baker2019}. None of our methods reaches the best feasible published layouts (Table~\ref{tab:iea37}; layouts: Section~\ref{S-sec:S-iea37} of the supplementary material). The gap is larger for 36 turbines, where the best of our layouts comes from \NIEABestOfThirtySixThirtyK. On this external benchmark the methods compared here thus stay below the best published layouts; as the participants' budgets are not reported comparably, the size of the gap is indicative only. The 64-turbine scenario was not run, and, like the published layouts, ours are optimized for 16 discrete directions and one wind speed.
