\opening{Dear Editors-in-Chief,}
We present for consideration the manuscript \textit{``Baseline Configuration and Random-Sampling Controls in Metaheuristic Comparisons for Wind Farm Layout Optimization''}, by Prince Solanki, Pulkit Dwivedi, Vanita Garg and Vinay Shukla.

The paper examines how baseline settings, hybrid-component controls and the treatment of unsuccessful runs change comparisons of swarm and evolutionary algorithms. On a 68-case wind farm layout benchmark with 30 paired seeds, it combines evaluation-budget accounting, equal-cost random-sampling controls, feasibility-qualified and all-run rankings and interval-based equivalence analyses, and it tests the conclusions on Horns Rev~1, Lillgrund and IEA37 cases, adding a real-coded GA (benchmark, Horns Rev~1, IEA37) and exact-gradient baselines (IEA37). A PSO coefficient sweep connects baseline behaviour to established second-moment stability theory under stagnation; the paper applies existing theory and proposes no new optimizer or convergence theorem.

Beyond the main comparison, the study includes an all-run paired endpoint on all 68 benchmark cases, equivalence tests calibrated by null simulation, a constraint-handling study (Deb's feasibility rules with box clipping or radial projection), direct optimization with 1$^\circ$ direction bins, method pools on Horns Rev~1 and IEA37 that include the GA and the exact-gradient methods, and an audit of the feasibility labels of all stored runs, with full-precision reruns of those the rounded coordinates cannot decide. The findings are bounded accordingly: PSO-VNS stays first when the search itself uses 1$^\circ$ bins (eight cases), GA's Horns Rev~1 lead at the larger budget holds under 1$^\circ$ bins and PyWake, and the conclusions depend partly on constraint handling (six cases, five methods), so they concern the implemented penalty and repair.

All code, the per-run records of every optimization run and the analysis scripts form a versioned reproducibility package. Re-executing its analysis pipeline regenerates every table, figure and number of the article and its supplement, and reruns of stored runs of the gradient-free methods reproduce the records bit for bit apart from elapsed time (SLSQP-based runs are not bit-reproducible across CPU and BLAS builds). The package (release 1.0.0, with SHA-256 checksums of every file) is provided to the editor and reviewers as supplementary material with this submission and will be deposited publicly with a DOI upon acceptance.
