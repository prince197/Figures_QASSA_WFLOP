\opening{Dear Editors-in-Chief,}
We present for consideration the manuscript \textit{``PSO-VNS: A Two-Phase Particle Swarm Optimization with Variable Neighborhood Search for Wind Farm Layout Optimization''}, by Prince Solanki, Pulkit Dwivedi, Vanita Garg and Vinay Shukla.

The paper proposes PSO-VNS, a new two-phase variant of particle swarm optimization with variable neighborhood search, for wind farm layout optimization. A constriction PSO first moves whole layouts towards the small, nonconvex feasible region; the basic VNS then refines the best layout by derivative-free compass search. The sequential design adds a single parameter, gives both phases exactly counted budgets and, as its first phase reproduces a PSO run, lets the gain of the local search be measured directly against PSO.

PSO-VNS is evaluated under a controlled protocol: the Kusiak--Song benchmark with two wind data sets (direction-independent and direction-dependent wind speeds) on circular farms of three sizes, Horns Rev~1 and Lillgrund wind farm blocks and the IEA37 case study; equal evaluation budgets that charge every gradient, 30 paired seeds, feasibility-aware and all-run rankings, equivalence tests and equal-cost random-sampling controls for the hybrid phases, with a real-coded GA and exact-gradient SLSQP as additional baselines. PSO-VNS ranks first on the benchmark, gains 0.19 percentage points of wake loss over PSO when optimizing with 1$^\circ$ direction bins (eight cases) and reaches feasible layouts more often than PSO on Horns Rev~1 and Lillgrund; the paper also states where it does not lead (the GA at the larger Horns Rev~1 budget, exact-gradient SLSQP on IEA37). The evaluation further shows that baseline settings and controls change such comparisons, linking a PSO coefficient sweep to established second-moment stability theory under stagnation, and it includes a constraint-handling study and an audit of the feasibility labels of all stored runs.

All code, the per-run records of every optimization run and the analysis scripts form a versioned reproducibility package. Re-executing its analysis pipeline regenerates every table, figure and number of the article and its supplement, and reruns of stored runs of the gradient-free methods reproduce the records bit for bit apart from elapsed time (SLSQP-based runs are not bit-reproducible across CPU and BLAS builds). The package (release 1.0.0, with SHA-256 checksums of every file) is provided to the editor and reviewers as supplementary material with this submission and will be deposited publicly with a DOI upon acceptance.

Funding, contribution, interest and AI-use statements are provided in the declarations document. The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere, and all authors have approved its submission.
