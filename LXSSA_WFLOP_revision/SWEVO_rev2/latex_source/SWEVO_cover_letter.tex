\opening{Dear Editors-in-Chief,}
We present for consideration the manuscript \textit{``PSO-VNS: A Two-Phase Particle Swarm Optimization with Variable Neighborhood Search for Wind Farm Layout Optimization''}, by Prince Solanki, Pulkit Dwivedi, Vanita Garg and Vinay Shukla.

The paper proposes PSO-VNS, a new two-phase variant of particle swarm optimization with variable neighborhood search, for wind farm layout optimization. A constriction PSO first moves whole layouts towards the small, nonconvex feasible region; the basic VNS then refines the best layout by derivative-free compass search. The sequential design adds a single parameter, gives both phases exactly counted budgets and, as its first phase reproduces a PSO run, lets the gain of the local search be measured directly against PSO.

PSO-VNS is compared with eight methods (PSO, SSA-VNS, SSA, LX-SSA, DE, a real-coded GA, VNS and multistart SLSQP) on four wind farm types: the Kusiak--Song benchmark with two wind data sets on circular farms of three sizes, blocks of the Horns Rev~1 and Lillgrund wind farms, and the IEA37 case study. All methods receive equal evaluation budgets and 30 paired seeds, and the results of each farm type are reported in tables with statistical tests. PSO-VNS ranks first on the benchmark (statistically level with PSO and GA), on the Lillgrund block, on the Horns Rev~1 block at the smaller budget and on IEA37 with 16 turbines, and gains 0.19 percentage points of wake loss over PSO when optimizing with 1$^\circ$ direction bins (eight cases); the paper also states where it does not lead (GA on Horns Rev~1 at the larger budget and on IEA37 with 36 turbines). An ablation study shows that the PSO phase is the component that drives the gain.

All code, the per-run records of every optimization run and the analysis scripts are kept in a versioned archive. Re-executing its analysis pipeline regenerates every table, figure and number of the article and its supplement, and reruns of stored runs of the gradient-free methods reproduce the records bit for bit apart from elapsed time (SLSQP-based runs are not bit-reproducible across CPU and BLAS builds). The code, the wind data sets and the generated results are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

Funding, contribution, interest and AI-use statements are provided in the declarations document. The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere, and all authors have approved its submission.
