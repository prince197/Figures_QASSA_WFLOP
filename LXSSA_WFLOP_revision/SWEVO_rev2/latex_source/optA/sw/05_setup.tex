\subsection{Wind Farm Types}
\label{sec:setup-cases}
\label{sec:setup-beyond}
The methods are compared on four wind farm types (Table~\ref{tab:farm-types}):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Type~I}, the Kusiak--Song benchmark~\cite{Kusiak2010,Bansal2017}: 68 cases of circular farms with radii of 500, 750 and 1000~m and 2--15 turbines, under two synthetic wind roses, Data Sets~I and~II (Section~\ref{sec:wind}; Table~\ref{S-tab:S-wind});
\item \emph{Type~II}, a 16-turbine block of the offshore farm Horns Rev~1 with its measured 12-sector Weibull wind climate;
\item \emph{Type~III}, a 16-turbine ($4\times4$) block of the offshore farm Lillgrund with its measured 12-sector Weibull wind climate;
\item \emph{Type~IV}, IEA37 Case Study~1~\cite{Baker2019,IEA37repo}: circular farms with 16 and 36 turbines and a 16-direction wind rose with one wind speed; it tests optimizers rather than represents a site.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Compared Methods}
\label{sec:setup-methods}
The main comparison runs the same nine methods on every wind farm type; Table~\ref{tab:params} (Section~\ref{sec:hybrid}) lists their settings and budgets ($N_p=30$ for the population methods):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{PSO-VNS}, the proposed two-phase method (Algorithm~\ref{alg:hybrid}, $\omega=0.5$);
\item \emph{PSO}, the standard PSO of Kennedy and Eberhart~\cite{Kennedy1995} with the constriction coefficients of Clerc and Kennedy~\cite{Clerc2002};
\item \emph{SSA-VNS}, the two-phase template of PSO-VNS with SSA in Phase~1 (Section~\ref{sec:template});
\item \emph{SSA}, the salp swarm algorithm~\cite{Mirjalili2017};
\item \emph{LX-SSA}, the Laplacian SSA~\cite{Solanki2023};
\item \emph{DE}, DE/rand/1/bin~\cite{Storn1997};
\item \emph{GA}, a standard real-coded genetic algorithm (binary tournament, simulated binary crossover, polynomial mutation, elitism; settings in Table~\ref{tab:params}, untuned);
\item \emph{VNS}, the basic VNS alone (Algorithm~\ref{alg:vns});
\item \emph{MS-SLSQP}, multistart sequential least-squares programming (SciPy's SLSQP~\cite{Kraft1988}, forward-difference gradients), which minimizes the wake loss under explicit constraints and restarts from the initial, then from random layouts (Section~\ref{S-sec:S-slsqp}).
\end{enumerate}
All methods except MS-SLSQP minimize $F_p$~\eqref{eq:penalty} (Type~I: over $[-r,r]^{2N}$). GA uses the same protocol, seeds and budgets as the other methods on all four types (Table~\ref{tab:design}). Grid-based GAs~\cite{Mosetti1994,Grady2005} optimize a discrete formulation, and ACO~\cite{Eroglu2012} and BBO~\cite{Bansal2017} were not re-implemented; the pool is a controlled set of baselines, not a survey of WFLOP optimizers (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

The \emph{ablation study} of PSO-VNS (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}) keeps its two-phase template and changes only Phase~1 (Section~\ref{sec:template}): SSA-VNS and \emph{LX-SSA-VNS} use SSA or LX-SSA, and the random-sampling controls \emph{RS-VNS} and \emph{RSD-VNS} draw, after the common initial population, uniform layouts in the bounding square or in the farm disc (3,015 Phase-1 evaluations versus the hybrids' 3,030). A split study varies $\omega$ on twelve cases (Section~\ref{sec:split}). Table~\ref{tab:design} lists all studies; the re-evaluations and diagnostics use stored layouts and runs.

\subsection{Comparison Design and Statistics}
\label{sec:setup-stats}
All comparisons follow the rules below. The scoring of runs, the rounding and the Holm families are detailed in Section~\ref{S-sec:S-stats}, and the equivalence analysis in Section~\ref{S-sec:S-th-equiv}.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Equal budgets.} Every run uses exactly the budget $B$ of its study ($B=6{,}030$ objective evaluations unless stated otherwise). The budget counts every call of the objective: the initial population, repeated evaluations of a layout and the finite-difference calls of MS-SLSQP; an exact gradient (IEA37 only) is charged as $c_g=3$ evaluations (Section~\ref{sec:iea37}). Work outside the objective is not counted: the optimizer's own arithmetic, the constraint checks, the internal subproblems of SLSQP and the geometric packing of the feasibility-preserving initialization.
\item \emph{Equal budgets do not mean equal elapsed times.} Two methods with the same budget make the same number of objective calls, but the uncounted work differs between them (MS-SLSQP, for example, also solves the SLSQP subproblems and needs more than twice as long per evaluation as PSO), and the elapsed time also depends on the machine and its load. Elapsed times are therefore reported separately (Section~\ref{sec:runtime}).
\item \emph{Paired seeds.} Every method uses seeds 1--30 and draws its initial population first, so runs with the same seed start from the same population and are paired.
% verified 2026-09-28: first Curve value identical across fresh_grid/vgrid/bgrid and mpce_psoc/psobv/rsvns/slsqp (13 files) for all 792 case-seed pairs with a feasible initial best (else NaN)
\item \emph{Feasibility.} A final layout is feasible if it meets the spacing and boundary constraints within $10^{-6}$~m (Section~\ref{S-sec:S-tol}). Method $a$ is \emph{qualified} in case $c$ if at least half of its runs are feasible (15 of 30; 5 of 10 with 10 seeds); $\bar L_{a,c}$ is the mean wake loss of its feasible runs. For two methods $A$ and $B$, $d_c=\bar L_{A,c}-\bar L_{B,c}$ is the case-mean difference (percentage points, pp; negative: $A$ better) and $\overline{\Delta L}$ the mean of $d_c$ over the cases in which both are qualified.
\item \emph{Ranking.} Within a case, the qualified methods are ranked by the mean benchmark objective of their feasible runs (rounded to $10^{-6}$; ties share their average rank), and the other methods below them by their number of feasible runs. The rank thus measures solution quality above a success threshold, not reliability (15 of 30 feasible runs can outrank 30 of 30); reliability is reported separately, by the feasible-run counts and an all-run paired outcome (Section~\ref{sec:archive-reliability}).
\item \emph{Tests.} All tests are two-sided ($\alpha=0.05$); Holm's correction~\cite{Holm1979} is applied over the comparisons of one table.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Friedman test on the case ranks (with the Iman--Davenport statistic) and post hoc $z$ tests of each method's average rank against that of PSO-VNS, $z=(\bar R_a-\bar R_{\text{PSO-VNS}})/\sqrt{k(k+1)/(6n)}$ for $k$ methods and $n$ cases~\cite{Demsar2006,Derrac2011,LaTorre2021}.
\item[(b)] Holm-adjusted Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the case means ($d_c$), the primary test for differences~\cite{Benavoli2016}; a case in which only one method is qualified counts as a maximal difference in its favor (the test then concerns a combined score of wake loss and qualification), and one in which neither is, is dropped. $\overline{\Delta L}$ is given with a 95\% percentile bootstrap CI (10{,}000 resamples of the cases) and, for key pairs, with the Hodges--Lehmann estimate (Table~\ref{S-tab:X-hl}).
\item[(c)] Per case, Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the 30 seed-paired runs, scored feasibility first, with the rank-biserial correlation $r_{\rm rb}$; W/T/L counts the cases with a significant win, no significant difference and a significant loss.
\item[(d)] Exact McNemar test on the seed-paired feasibility outcomes (feasible or not) of two methods in one case.
\item[(e)] Practical equivalence by two one-sided tests (TOST)~\cite{Lakens2017} at the margin $m=0.05$~pp, decided by interval inclusion (Definition~\ref{def:S-equiv}), and the Bayesian signed-rank test~\cite{Benavoli2017}, which reports $P_{\rm rope}$ ($P_{\rm A}$, $P_{\rm B}$), the posterior probability that practical equivalence ($A$ better, $B$ better by more than $m$) is the most probable of the three outcomes (Section~\ref{S-sec:S-th-equiv}).
\end{enumerate}

\item \emph{Inference hierarchy.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] Differences rest on the case-mean Wilcoxon tests~(b); the 68 cases are designed, not sampled, so their $p$ values describe this benchmark, not a population of farms.
\item[(ii)] Two equivalence questions are primary, each assessed on its own at $\alpha=0.05$ and decided at the seed level (Definition~\ref{def:S-equiv}(a)): whether the VNS phase adds a practically relevant gain to PSO (PSO-VNS vs.\ PSO, two-sided equivalence), and whether an SSA first phase gives a practically relevant gain over disc sampling (SSA-VNS vs.\ RSD-VNS, non-superiority). The case and cluster levels are sensitivity analyses; all other equivalence results are exploratory.
\item[(iii)] Average ranks with their Friedman and post hoc tests, and the W/T/L counts, are descriptive summaries; the ranks depend on the method pool.
\item[(iv)] The all-run paired outcome is a secondary endpoint: it counts wins and does not measure the size of a difference.
\item[(v)] The additional experiments and sensitivity studies of Table~\ref{tab:design} are secondary analyses.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}

\begin{definition}[Practical equivalence at three levels of inference]
\label{def:S-equiv}
Let $A$ and $B$ be two methods, $d_c$ their case-mean difference over the $n$ cases in which both are qualified, $\overline{\Delta L}=\frac1n\sum_c d_c$, and $m>0$ a margin (not to be confused with the coordinate index $m$ of Section~\ref{sec:prelim}). $A$ and $B$ are \emph{practically equivalent at margin $m$} at a given level if both one-sided hypotheses $H_0^-\colon\Delta\le-m$ and $H_0^+\colon\Delta\ge m$ about the estimand $\Delta$ of that level are rejected at $\alpha=0.05$, i.e., if the 90\% interval for $\Delta$ lies inside $(-m,m)$. The three levels are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \emph{Seed level} (the fixed benchmark). Estimand: the benchmark average of the expected case-mean differences; only the run-to-run variability is random. Interval: percentile bootstrap that resamples the 30 seed-paired runs within every case (the same seeds for both methods; case means over the feasible resampled runs).
\item[(b)] \emph{Case level} (exchangeability sensitivity). Estimand: the mean case difference under a hypothetical exchangeable distribution represented by the 68 designed cases. Interval: percentile bootstrap over cases, treating them as independent; a sensitivity analysis, as the cases are a designed benchmark, not a random sample of farms.
\item[(c)] \emph{Cluster level} (benchmark-group sensitivity). Estimand: the case-weighted mean difference allowing dependence within the six designed (data set, radius) groups with nested $N$. Interval: cluster-robust $t$ interval with the bias-reduced CR2 variance and a $t$ distribution with $G-1=5$ degrees of freedom ($G=6$ clusters), and a restricted wild-cluster bootstrap-$t$ TOST (CR2-studentized, all $6^6$ draws of Webb's six-point weights enumerated); equivalence at this level requires both.
\end{enumerate}
The \emph{minimal margin} of a level, $m_{\min}=\max(\lvert\ell\rvert,\lvert u\rvert)$ for the 90\% interval $[\ell,u]$, is the smallest margin at which equivalence holds: it holds for every $m>m_{\min}$ and for none smaller.
\end{definition}
Non-superiority (``$A$ gives no gain larger than $m$ over $B$'') needs only one of the two tests: the lower end of the 90\% interval must lie above $-m$. A result that is neither equivalent nor significantly different is inconclusive. The margin $m=0.05$~pp is a post hoc convention; the minimal margins let readers apply their own. The calibration of the bootstrap TOST, the joint resampling of the seeds (seed $k$ seeds the same random stream in every case) and an equal weighting of the clusters are reported in Section~\ref{S-sec:S-th-equiv} and Tables~\ref{S-tab:S-sa-tostaudit}, \ref{S-tab:S-sa-syncseed} and~\ref{S-tab:S-sa-eqclus}.
