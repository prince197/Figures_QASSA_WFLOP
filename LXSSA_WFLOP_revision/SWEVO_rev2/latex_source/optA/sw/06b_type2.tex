\subsection{Experimental Setting}
The Horns Rev~1 block is PyWake's 16-turbine example~\cite{PyWake}:
\begin{itemize}
\item V80 turbines ($D=80$~m) and the measured 12-sector wind climate;
\item the parallelogram through the corner turbines as boundary (the boundary violation in~\eqref{eq:penalty} is the distance in m outside it) and $\ell_{\min}=4D$ (installed layout: a regular $7D$ grid);
\item the Jensen wake with $K=0.04$, a hub-center in-wake test and the thrust coefficient $C_T$ at the free-stream speed (Section~\ref{S-sec:S-hrmodel});
\item 5$^\circ$ direction and 1-m/s speed bins (wake-free AEP 148.81~GWh/yr);
\item 6{,}030 and 30{,}030 evaluations, 30 seed-paired runs, random initialization.
\end{itemize}
For the installed 80-turbine farm at identical bins, PyWake~2.6.20's NOJ model~\cite{PyWake} gives 661.39~GWh/yr and ours 666.75~GWh/yr (0.81\% more; wake loss 10.39\% (ours) vs.\ 11.11\% (PyWake); Table~\ref{S-tab:F-pywake}); with $C_T$ at the local speed, our model reproduces PyWake's hub-center NOJ (momentum-theory induction) exactly, and rotor averaging does not explain the difference.

\subsection{Numerical Results}
The installed layout reaches 139.82~GWh/yr (wake loss 6.04\%; Table~\ref{tab:farm-II}). At 6,030 evaluations from random starts, the robust result is feasibility: PSO-VNS is feasible in 30/30 runs, PSO in only 19/30 and DE in 0/30. The best layout of all methods at this budget, a PSO-VNS layout (139.78~GWh/yr; Figs.~\ref{S-fig:S-hr16} and~\ref{S-fig:S-layouts-sites}), stays below the installed block, like every random-start run at this budget (GA included).
% CHECK-FINAL [C20]: PSO feasible in fewer than 30 of 30 random-start runs ("only"); [C31]: 6,030 PSO-VNS mean AEP below the installed block; no 6,030 random-start run above the installed block with 5-deg bins (Table S-F-hr; 16 runs above at 5 deg over all budgets, 0 at 1 deg: [F21])

PSO-VNS ranks first at 6,030 evaluations, GA at 30,030 (139.64~GWh/yr), where PSO-VNS ranks second.

The finer rose costs the installed block 0.32~GWh/yr and the optimized layouts 1.08~GWh/yr on average, the VNS-based methods most (GA: 0.99 and 1.45~GWh/yr at the two budgets): like those of the benchmark (Section~\ref{sec:direction}), the optimized layouts exploit the gaps between the coarse direction bins. With 1$^\circ$ bins, none of the 961 feasible optimized layouts (all methods and budgets, GA and the ablation variants included) exceeds the installed block (22 with 5$^\circ$ bins), although the looser spacing $4D$ (installed: $7D$) enlarges their feasible set.

\subsection{Statistical Analysis}
The feasibility difference between PSO-VNS and PSO at 6,030 evaluations is significant (exact McNemar $p=\ensuremath {9.8\times 10^{-4}}$).
% CHECK-EXTRA [X52]: HR feasibility PSO-VNS 30/30 vs PSO 19/30, 11 discordant seeds all favour PSO-VNS, exact McNemar p = 9.8e-4
In the seed-paired tests of Table~\ref{tab:farm-II}, GA does not differ from PSO-VNS at 6,030 evaluations ($p_{\rm Holm}=0.529$), and PSO-VNS is better than the other seven methods ($p_{\rm Holm}\le5.1\times10^{-4}$); at 30,030 evaluations GA is better than PSO-VNS ($p_{\rm Holm}=2.2\times10^{-4}$), and PSO-VNS is better than the remaining seven ($p_{\rm Holm}\le0.009$). We re-evaluated every final layout with 1$^\circ$ bins and with PyWake's NOJ at the same bins (Tables~\ref{S-tab:F-hr} and~\ref{S-tab:S-sa-hr-eval}). At 6,030 evaluations, GA and PSO-VNS do not differ under any evaluator ($p\ge0.36$), and the AEP advantage of PSO-VNS over PSO on the 19 jointly feasible seeds (+0.54~GWh/yr, $p=0.016$) vanishes with 1$^\circ$ bins (\ensuremath {-}0.05, $p=0.65$) and under PyWake. At 30,030 evaluations, GA's lead holds with 1$^\circ$ bins (+0.48~GWh/yr over PSO-VNS, $p=2.8\times10^{-4}$) and under PyWake (+0.43, $p=4.2\times10^{-4}$); PSO-VNS has the highest mean AEP after GA under all three evaluations and beats PSO on the 28 jointly feasible seeds also with 1$^\circ$ bins (+0.28~GWh/yr, $p=\ensuremath {6.5\times 10^{-4}}$) and under PyWake.
