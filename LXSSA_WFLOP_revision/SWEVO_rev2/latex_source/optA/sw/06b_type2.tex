\section{Wind Farm Type II: Horns Rev~1 Block}
\label{sec:hornsrev-site}
\label{sec:farm-II}
The benchmark of Section~\ref{sec:farm-I} uses synthetic wind roses and a prescribed power curve. Type~II moves the comparison to a real site: a 16-turbine block of the Horns Rev~1 offshore wind farm, the first of the two farm types with a measured wind climate. This section gives its setting, the numerical results and their statistical analysis.

\subsection{Experimental Setting}
Type~II differs from Type~I in the site, the turbines, the wind data, the wake parameters and the budgets (Table~\ref{tab:farm-types}; details: Section~\ref{S-sec:S-hrmodel}):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Site and layout.} The 16-turbine corner block of PyWake's Horns Rev~1 example~\cite{PyWake}; its installed layout, a regular $7D$ grid, is the reference.
\item \emph{Turbines.} V80 ($D=80$~m) with the power and thrust curves of the example.
\item \emph{Wind data.} The measured 12-sector Weibull climate of the example, discretized into 5$^\circ$ direction bins (six per sector) and 1-m/s speed bins (3--25~m/s), gives a wake-free AEP of 148.81~GWh/yr.
\item \emph{Wake model.} Jensen with $K=0.04$, a hub-center in-wake test, root-sum-square superposition and the thrust coefficient $C_T$ at the free-stream speed.
\item \emph{Constraints.} The boundary is the parallelogram through the corner turbines, enlarged by 0.1\%, and the boundary violation in~\eqref{eq:penalty} is the distance (m) outside it. The minimum spacing is $\ell_{\min}=4D$, as on Type~I (installed layout: $7D$).
\item \emph{Budgets, seeds and initialization.} 6{,}030 and 30{,}030 evaluations; 30 seed-paired runs (seeds 1--30) per method and budget; random initialization.
\item \emph{Methods.} The nine methods use the settings of Table~\ref{tab:params}, except that the search box is the square of half-width 943~m centered on the block, and this half-width is the $r$ of the VNS radii. The ablation variants LX-SSA-VNS and RS-VNS were also run (Table~\ref{S-tab:S-sa-hr-eval}).
\end{enumerate}
To check the wake model of this setting, we evaluated the installed 80-turbine farm at identical bins with both models. PyWake~2.6.20's NOJ model~\cite{PyWake} gives 661.39~GWh/yr and our model 666.75~GWh/yr, 0.81\% more (wake loss: ours 10.39\%, PyWake 11.11\%; Table~\ref{S-tab:F-pywake}). With $C_T$ at the local speed, however, our model reproduces PyWake's hub-center NOJ (momentum-theory induction) exactly, and rotor averaging does not explain the difference.

At 6,030 evaluations, the methods differ mainly in feasibility: PSO-VNS is feasible in all 30 runs, PSO in only 19 and DE in none (Table~\ref{tab:farm-II}). PSO-VNS ranks first at 6,030 evaluations and GA at 30,030, where PSO-VNS ranks second. Moreover, at 6,030 evaluations no random-start run of any method, GA included, reaches the installed block (best layouts: Figs.~\ref{S-fig:S-hr16} and~\ref{S-fig:S-layouts-sites}).

To see how much of the AEP depends on the coarse direction bins, we re-evaluated the final layouts with 1$^\circ$ bins (Table~\ref{S-tab:F-hr}). The installed block then loses 0.32~GWh/yr and the optimized layouts 1.08~GWh/yr on average, the VNS-based methods most. Thus, as on the benchmark (Section~\ref{sec:direction}), the optimized layouts exploit the gaps between the coarse bins: none of the 961 feasible optimized layouts (all methods and budgets) exceeds the installed block with 1$^\circ$ bins, whereas 22 do with 5$^\circ$ bins.

\subsection{Statistical Analysis}
The seed-paired tests show which of these differences are systematic over the seeds. In the tests of Table~\ref{tab:farm-II}, PSO-VNS is better than seven of the eight methods at both budgets; it does not differ from GA at 6,030 evaluations, and GA is better at 30,030. For GA and PSO, the methods ranked closest to PSO-VNS, Table~\ref{tab:farm-II-tests} adds the feasibility test against PSO and repeats the AEP tests with the final layouts re-evaluated. All outcomes hold under the three evaluators except the AEP advantage of PSO-VNS over PSO at 6,030 evaluations, which is significant only with the 5$^\circ$ bins used in optimization.
