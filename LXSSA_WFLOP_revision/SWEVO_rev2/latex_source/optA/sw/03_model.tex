\section{Problem Modeling}
\label{sec:model}

This section states the problem that all methods solve, with its wind, wake and power models and its constraint handling. The problem has four elements (Fig.~\ref{fig:model}).
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Decision variables.} For a prescribed number $N$ of identical turbines with rotor radius $R$ (diameter $D=2R$), the $n=2N$ coordinates of the layout $\mathbf X=(x_1,y_1,\ldots,x_N,y_N)$.
\item \emph{Objective.} The expected farm power, and hence the annual energy production (AEP), is maximized, i.e., the wake loss is minimized, under the Jensen wake model~\cite{Jensen1983,Katic1986} and the power curve of~\cite{Kusiak2010} (Sections~\ref{sec:wind}--\ref{sec:powermodel}).
\item \emph{Wind direction.} The direction $\theta$ has the probability density $p_\theta$; it is the direction \emph{towards} which the wind blows, counter-clockwise from east (Fig.~\ref{fig:model}a). The meteorological directions $\theta_{\rm met}$ of Horns Rev~1 and IEA37 (wind \emph{from}, clockwise from north) are converted by $\theta=270^\circ-\theta_{\rm met}$.
\item \emph{Constraints} (Section~\ref{sec:constraints}). The turbine distances $\ell_{ij}=\lVert(x_i-x_j,\,y_i-y_j)\rVert_2$ are at least $\ell_{\min}=4D=8R$~\cite{Kusiak2010,Bansal2017}, and the farm is a disc of radius $r=500$, 750 or 1000~m.
\end{itemize}
The spacing $4D$ is the convention of the Kusiak--Song benchmark and is kept for comparability. It lies at the dense end of practice, as the installed Horns Rev~1 layout is a regular grid of about $7D$ (Section~\ref{sec:hornsrev-site}); our results therefore concern a packing-constrained regime, and Section~\ref{sec:spacing} examines larger spacings.

We use the Kusiak--Song benchmark model unchanged and claim no modeling novelty; the contributions are the PSO-VNS algorithm and its controlled evaluation. The model rests on the following assumptions, which the subsections below state in detail:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[A1.] All $N$ turbines are identical, and their number is prescribed.
\item[A2.] The terrain is flat, and the farm boundary is a circle of radius $r$ (blocks of the Horns Rev~1 and Lillgrund farms: their own boundaries).
\item[A3.] Turbines must be at least $\ell_{\min}=4D$ apart.
\item[A4.] In each direction bin, the wind speed follows a Weibull distribution; the wind rose is discretized into $N_\theta$ direction bins.
\item[A5.] Wakes follow the Jensen top-hat model with a constant thrust coefficient $C_T$; a turbine is waked if its hub center lies in the wake cone, and the deficits of several wakes are combined by the root-sum-square rule.
\item[A6.] The power curve is that of the Kusiak--Song benchmark: linear between cut-in and rated speed, constant above.
\item[A7.] Only the wake loss is optimized; the electrical collector-system layout and multi-objective formulations (e.g., AEP against cost or the levelized cost of energy) are not considered.
\end{enumerate}
The comparison protocol, in contrast, does not depend on the model: its equal budgets, seed pairing, equal-cost controls, feasibility-aware ranking and equivalence tests apply to such formulations as well.

\subsection{Wind Model and Direction Discretization}
\label{sec:wind}
For each direction, the wind speed $s$ has the Weibull density
\begin{equation*}
p_s(s;\zeta,\psi)=\frac{\zeta}{\psi}\Bigl(\frac{s}{\psi}\Bigr)^{\zeta-1}\exp\Bigl[-\Bigl(\frac{s}{\psi}\Bigr)^{\zeta}\Bigr]
\end{equation*}
with shape $\zeta(\theta)$ and scale $\psi(\theta)$. The benchmark rose is piecewise constant: $N_\theta=24$ direction bins $[15(l-1),15l)^\circ$, $l=1,\ldots,24$, with probability $p_l$, shape $\zeta=2$ and scale $\psi_l$ (Table~\ref{S-tab:S-wind}). The benchmark provides two wind data sets:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Data Set~I}, nearly unidirectional, with the same wind-speed distribution in all directions: $\psi=13$~m/s in all bins, and 80\% of the probability in bins 6 and 7, i.e., wind from 165$^\circ$ to 195$^\circ$;
\item \emph{Data Set~II}, a broad rose with direction-dependent wind speeds: scales from 2.6 to 10~m/s and the highest probability in bin~13.
\end{itemize}
Both are synthetic wind roses of the Kusiak--Song benchmark~\cite{Kusiak2010,Bansal2017}, not measurements at a site. The measured wind climates in this study are the 12-sector Weibull roses of the Horns Rev~1 block (Section~\ref{sec:hornsrev-site}) and of a Lillgrund block (Section~\ref{sec:lillgrund}), both optimized in 5$^\circ$ bins; IEA37 Case Study~1 uses 16 directions at one speed (Section~\ref{sec:setup-beyond}).
% verified: analysis/wflop_model.py (DS1_W, DS1_PSI, DS2_PSI, DS2_W; k = 2); bins 6+7 of DS I: 0.2+0.6 = 0.8, towards 75-105 deg = from 165-195 deg

Because a top-hat wake is narrow, the direction bins interact with the wake model: with 15$^\circ$ bins the wake of a distant pair of turbines can fall between two bin centers and not enter the objective; optimized layouts exploit these gaps (Section~\ref{S-sec:S-model-details}). Section~\ref{sec:direction} therefore reports which conclusions survive a 1$^\circ$ rose.

\subsection{Wake Effect Model}
The wake model determines the wind speed that reaches each turbine. In the Jensen model (Fig.~\ref{fig:model}b,c), the wake of turbine $j$ is a half cone with half-angle $\alpha=\arctan K$ (wake-spreading constant $K$), and its virtual vertex lies $R/K$ upstream of the rotor. Let $u_{ij}=(x_i - x_j)\cos\theta + (y_i - y_j)\sin\theta + R/K$ be the along-wind distance of turbine $i$ from this vertex and $v_{ij}=-(x_i - x_j)\sin\theta + (y_i - y_j)\cos\theta$ its lateral offset; turbine $i$ then lies in the wake if $\beta_{ij}=\cos^{-1}\!\bigl(u_{ij}/\sqrt{u_{ij}^2+v_{ij}^2}\bigr)<\alpha$. Its downstream distance is $d_{ij}=\lvert u_{ij}-R/K\rvert$, and with the thrust coefficient $C_T$, the free-stream speed $s_{\text{up}}$ and the wake speed $s_{\text{down}}$, the velocity deficit is
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_{ij} = 1-\frac{s_{\text{down}}}{s_{\text{up}}}=\frac{1 - \sqrt{1 - C_T}}{\left(1 + K d_{ij}/R\right)^2}.
    \label{eq:deficit}
\end{equation}
The numerator is the deficit behind the rotor from actuator-disc momentum theory, and the factor $R^2/(R+Kd_{ij})^2$ spreads it over the growing wake (Fig.~\ref{fig:model}b). With $C_T=0.8$ and $K=0.075$, a turbine $\ell_{\min}=308$~m directly behind another loses 21.6\% of the wind speed, and one 770~m ($10D$) behind loses 8.8\%.
% verified: (1-sqrt(0.2))/(1+0.075*308/38.5)^2 = 0.2159; (1-sqrt(0.2))/(1+0.075*770/38.5)^2 = 0.0884
As in~\cite{Kusiak2010}, the top-hat wake is tested at the hub center only. The deficit therefore jumps at the cone edge, and it also reaches a turbine less than $R/K$ \emph{upstream} of $j$ near its axis (between $A$ and the rotor in Fig.~\ref{fig:model}c); this effect is non-physical but kept for comparability with the published results. The deficits of several wakes are combined by the root-sum-square rule,
\begin{equation}\label{eq5}
\Delta_i(\theta) = \Bigl( \textstyle\sum_{j \ne i,\ \beta_{ij} < \alpha} \Delta_{ij}^2 \Bigr)^{1/2} .
\end{equation}
Because $C_T$ is constant, which overstates the deficits above rated speed, $\Delta_i(\theta)$ depends only on the layout and the direction, not on the free-stream speed $s$. The local wind speed in a direction bin, $s_{\rm local}=[1-\Delta_i(\theta)]\,s$, is therefore a scaled Weibull variable with the same shape $\zeta(\theta)$ and the scale $\psi_i(\theta)=\psi(\theta)[1-\Delta_i(\theta)]$. The hub-center test, finally, makes the objective jump wherever a hub crosses a cone edge in an evaluated direction. It motivates the derivative-free local search of VNS (Section~\ref{sec:vns}) and handicaps the finite-difference gradients of MS-SLSQP.

We use the Jensen model for comparability with the published Kusiak--Song results; Sections~\ref{sec:iea37} and~\ref{sec:gaussian} test a Gaussian wake (see Section~\ref{S-sec:S-model-details}).

\subsection{Power Model and Expected Energy}
\label{sec:powermodel}
\label{sec:S-power}
The local wind speeds determine the power of each turbine through the power curve of the Kusiak--Song benchmark~\cite{Kusiak2010},
\begin{equation*}
f(s)=\begin{cases}
0, & s<s_{\text{cut-in}},\\
\lambda_1 s+\lambda_0, & s_{\text{cut-in}}\le s\le s_{\text{rated}},\\
P_{\text{rated}}, & s>s_{\text{rated}},
\end{cases}
\end{equation*}
with $s_{\text{cut-in}}=3.5$~m/s, $s_{\text{rated}}=14$~m/s, $P_{\text{rated}}=1500$~kW, slope $\lambda_1=140.86$ and intercept $\lambda_0=-500$. We use it as published (Section~\ref{S-sec:S-model-details}); Section~\ref{sec:powercurve} tests a cubic curve. The expected power of turbine $i$, whose power output $P_i=f(s_{\rm local})$ depends on its local wind speed,
\begin{equation}\label{eq8}
E(P_i) = \int_0^{360} p_\theta(\theta) \int_0^{\infty} f(s)\,
       p_s\bigl(s; \zeta(\theta), \psi_i(\theta)\bigr) \, ds \, d\theta ,
\end{equation}
is evaluated, as in~\cite{Kusiak2010}, by a Riemann sum over 24 direction bins of 15$^\circ$ and 21 speed bins of 0.5~m/s between $s_{\text{cut-in}}$ and $s_{\text{rated}}$ (Eq.~\eqref{S-eq:S-power-disc}).
Like the earlier studies, we keep the bin width ($15^\circ$) in degrees, so the benchmark objective $\sum_i E(P_i)$ is 15 times the expected farm power in kW, and the gross (wake-only) annual energy production is $\mathrm{AEP}\,[\mathrm{MWh}] = (\text{objective}/15)\times 8.76$. The wake-free benchmark objective of one turbine, $E_{\rm ideal}$, is the value of~\eqref{eq8} with $\psi_i=\psi$; for Data Set~I, $E_{\rm ideal}=14{,}045.74$, which corresponds to 936.38~kW (a capacity factor of 62\%, far above real sites) or 8,202.7~MWh/yr.

\subsection{Optimization Problem and Constraint Handling}
\label{sec:constraints}
These components define the objective of the optimization problem. With the wake loss $L(\mathbf X)=N E_{\rm ideal}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}E(P_i)$, reported in percent of the wake-free value, $100\,L/(NE_{\rm ideal})$, the optimization problem reads
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\mathbf X\in[-r,r]^{2N}}\quad & L(\mathbf X)\\
\text{subject to}\quad & \ell_{ij}\ge\ell_{\min}, && 1\le i<j\le N \quad \text{(spacing)},\\
& x_i^2+y_i^2\le r^2, && 1\le i\le N \quad \text{(boundary)},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:wflop}
\end{equation}
which is equivalent to maximizing the expected farm power and hence the AEP. It is a problem in $2N$ variables ($2N\le30$ on the benchmark) with a discontinuous objective and $N(N-1)/2$ nonconvex spacing constraints; these two features make it hard and shape the methods of Sections~\ref{sec:prelim}--\ref{sec:hybrid}.

All optimizers except MS-SLSQP handle the constraints by repair and penalty. After every position update, each coordinate is clipped to $[-r,r]$ (box repair; the square, not the circle). The boundary and spacing violations are
\begin{equation*}
g^{\rm b}_{i}=x_i^2+y_i^2-r^2,\qquad g^{\rm s}_{ij}=\ell_{\min}-\ell_{ij};
\end{equation*}
these optimizers minimize the penalized wake loss
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
F_p(\mathbf X)={}&\Bigl(N E_{\rm ideal}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}E(P_i)\Bigr)+\sum_{i:\,g^{\rm b}_i>0}\bigl(1+\mu_{\rm p} g^{\rm b}_i\bigr)^2\\
&+\sum_{i<j:\,g^{\rm s}_{ij}>0}\bigl(1+\mu_{\rm p} g^{\rm s}_{ij}\bigr)^2,\qquad \mu_{\rm p}=10^{10},
\end{split}
\label{eq:penalty}
\end{equation}
whose first term is the wake loss $L(\mathbf X)$ reported in our tables. Three properties of this treatment matter for the comparisons.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Feasibility first.} Any violation above about $4.6\times10^{-8}$ (m or m$^2$) makes the penalty exceed any possible wake loss, so comparing $F_p$ values is, up to smaller violations, a feasibility-first rule (feasible before infeasible, then wake loss or total penalty).
\item \emph{Scaling of the violations.} The scaling ($g^{\rm b}_i$ in m$^2$, $g^{\rm s}_{ij}$ in m) favors infeasible layouts with turbines slightly too close, or with a turbine clipped to the box near a tangent point $(\pm r,y_i)$, where $g^{\rm b}_i=y_i^2$.
\item \emph{Feasibility of the final layouts.} MS-SLSQP, in contrast, minimizes the wake loss under explicit constraints. A final layout counts as feasible if $\ell_{ij}\ge\ell_{\min}$ and $\sqrt{x_i^2+y_i^2}\le r$ hold within $10^{-6}$~m (Section~\ref{sec:setup}), a looser tolerance than the penalty's. With random starts, no optimizer guarantees a feasible final layout; infeasible final layouts are therefore counted, reported and ranked below feasible ones (Section~\ref{sec:setup-stats}).
\end{itemize}
Section~\ref{sec:boundary} tests an alternative: Deb's feasibility rules~\cite{Deb2000} on dimensionless violations, with box clipping or radial projection.
