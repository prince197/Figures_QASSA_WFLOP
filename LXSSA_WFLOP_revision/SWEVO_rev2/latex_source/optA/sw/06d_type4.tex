\section{Wind Farm Type IV: IEA37 Case Study~1}
\label{sec:iea37}
\label{sec:farm-IV}
This section compares the nine methods on the 16- and 36-turbine scenarios of IEA37 Case Study~1~\cite{Baker2019,IEA37repo}, which test optimizers rather than represent a site.

\subsection{Experimental Setting}
The settings specific to Type~IV are (Table~\ref{tab:farm-types}; details: Section~\ref{S-sec:S-iea37}):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Sites.} Circular sites of radii 1300~m (16 turbines) and 2000~m (36 turbines); there is no installed layout, and the published layouts of the case study serve as references.
\item \emph{Turbines.} The reference turbine of the case study with its power curve.
\item \emph{Wind data.} The official wind rose: 16 directions at one wind speed.
\item \emph{Wake model.} The official simplified Bastankhah Gaussian wake; AEP in MWh as computed by the official calculator.
\item \emph{Constraints.} Circular boundary; minimum spacing $\ell_{\min}=2D$.
\item \emph{Budgets, seeds and initialization.} 6{,}030 and 30{,}030 evaluations; 30 seed-paired runs (seeds 1--30) per method and budget; random initialization.
\item \emph{Methods.} The nine methods with the settings of Table~\ref{tab:params}; as on Type~I, the search box is $[-r,r]^{2N}$ with the site radius $r$, which is also the $r$ of the VNS radii. The ablation variants LX-SSA-VNS and RS-VNS were also run (Table~\ref{S-tab:iea37-detail}); the two exact-gradient variants of SLSQP are described in Section~\ref{sec:iea-grad}.
\end{enumerate}
In the case study the participants chose their own optimizers and budgets~\cite{Baker2019}, so the published layouts are references, not results under the same budget.

With 36 turbines, MS-SLSQP is feasible in only 4 and 14 of 30 runs: every forward-difference gradient costs $2N+1$ evaluations, so 6,030 evaluations allow at most 82 gradients. None of the methods reaches the best feasible published layouts (all gaps: Table~\ref{S-tab:F-iea-gap}); as the participants' budgets are not reported comparably, the size of the gap is indicative only.

\subsection{Statistical Analysis}
In the seed-paired tests of Table~\ref{tab:farm-IV}, PSO-VNS is better than all eight other methods with 16 turbines at 6,030 evaluations and than all but GA at 30,030. With 36 turbines GA is better than PSO-VNS at both budgets, and PSO-VNS does not differ significantly from VNS at either budget.

\subsection{Exact Gradients on IEA37}
\label{sec:iea-grad}
As the IEA37 model has an analytic AEP gradient, we also ran SLSQP with exact objective and constraint derivatives, as multistart (exact-gradient MS-SLSQP) and after the PSO phase of PSO-VNS (PSO-SLSQP), each gradient charged $c_g=3$ evaluations, its measured cost (30 seeds, random starts, both budgets; Section~\ref{S-sec:S-xp-grad}, Tables~\ref{S-tab:S-xp-grad} and~\ref{S-tab:S-xp-grad-calls}). In a joint pool of 13 methods (the nine methods, LX-SSA-VNS, RS-VNS and the two exact-gradient variants; Table~\ref{S-tab:S-sa-iea-pool}), an exact-gradient variant ranks first in both scenarios and at both budgets and is better than each of the 11 methods without exact gradients in all 30 seed pairs ($p_{\rm Holm}=2.2\times10^{-8}$). Three qualifications apply:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The comparison tests complete implementations of the second phase, not the AEP derivative alone: the variants also differentiate the constraints (MS-SLSQP of the main comparison uses finite differences), PSO-SLSQP also restarts from the incumbent and personal bests, and the objective is only piecewise smooth.
\item The budget is equal in charged evaluations, not in time: per run the exact-gradient methods take 2.3--5.6 times as long as PSO-VNS (medians; Table~\ref{S-tab:S-sa-time-sites}).
\item The result rests on the feasibility labels reported by the runs: the rounded stored coordinates cannot decide 220 of the 240 strict labels, and none is decided infeasible at any tolerance (Section~\ref{sec:archive-reliability}; Table~\ref{S-tab:S-sa-feasbounds}). If every open label were infeasible, PSO-VNS (16 turbines) or GA (36 turbines) would lead (Table~\ref{S-tab:S-sa-feasworst}).
\end{enumerate}
