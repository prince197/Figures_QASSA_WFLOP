The ablation isolates the components of PSO-VNS on the Type~I farms (68 cases, 6,030 evaluations, 30 seed-paired runs from random starts). It keeps the two-phase template (Section~\ref{sec:template}) and changes one component at a time:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Phase~1}: PSO is replaced by SSA (SSA-VNS), by LX-SSA (LX-SSA-VNS) or by random sampling of equal cost, uniform in the bounding square of the farm (RS-VNS) or in the farm disc (RSD-VNS), or removed (VNS alone);
\item \emph{Phase~2}: VNS is removed (PSO alone; SSA and LX-SSA alone serve as references for the VNS phase after a salp swarm);
\item \emph{budget split}: $\omega$ takes five values on twelve cases (Section~\ref{sec:split}).
\end{enumerate}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} gives the average ranks of the nine variants, which differ significantly (Friedman test), and the planned contrasts; Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation} shows the convergence. A 90\% bootstrap interval of $\overline{\Delta L}$ inside $\pm0.05$~pp means practical equivalence at the case level (Table~\ref{tab:equiv-main}).

\subsection{The VNS Phase}
\label{sec:abl-vns}
The VNS phase clearly improves both salp-swarm methods (SSA-VNS vs.\ SSA and LX-SSA-VNS vs.\ LX-SSA in Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). After PSO, which PSO-VNS repeats for its first 3,030 evaluations (Section~\ref{sec:psovns-props}), its gain is small, not significant and practically equivalent to zero at the case level (Section~\ref{sec:psovns-pso}).
% CHECK-FINAL [C33]: VNS phase significant for SSA and LX-SSA (W > L, L = 0; case-mean p < 0.05, DL < 0), not for PSO (case-mean p >= 0.05, so also Holm); [C13]: PSOBV-PSOC W > L; [C49]: case-level 90 % CI \NEqPSOVNSvsPSOCI inside +-\NEqMargin

% Page budget: kept in the supplementary material: \AblTable{tab:D-vns}
At this budget the VNS phase is essentially one compass-search descent (Table~\ref{S-tab:D-vns}; 12 split cases $\times$ 10 seeds):
\begin{enumerate}
\item the first descent from the switch point gives nearly all of the Phase-2 improvement (PSO-VNS: 97.5\%);
\item the shake-and-search cycles take 21.5\% of the Phase-2 evaluations of PSO-VNS but give 0.5\% of its gain, and the few accepted cycles use only the first neighborhood ($k=1$);
\item for $N\ge12$ the median run never shakes and the first descent is unfinished in 39 of 40 runs, so the local-minimality guarantee (Proposition~\ref{S-prop:S-ls}) does not apply there;
\item VNS starts from the best feasible Phase-1 layout when one exists and never makes a feasible incumbent infeasible.
\end{enumerate}
The ablation thus measures the value of a local-search phase, a truncated compass search after Phase~1, in which shaking and neighborhood change can matter only for small $N$.

\subsection{Random-Sampling Controls}
\label{sec:abl-controls}
RSD-VNS, RS-VNS and VNS alone are the controls. Sampling in the disc is the stronger control: RSD-VNS beats RS-VNS and ranks third of the nine variants (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}).

\subsection{Swarm First Phases}
\label{sec:abl-swarm}
A PSO phase clearly beats every control; the salp-swarm phases do not (Tables~\ref{tab:ablation} and~\ref{tab:equiv-main}):
\begin{enumerate}
\item PSO-VNS is significantly better than RSD-VNS, RS-VNS and VNS alone in most cases and significantly worse in at most one, and its gain over RSD-VNS lies wholly beyond the 0.05~pp margin;
\item SSA-VNS is significantly better than RSD-VNS in no case, and the case means, not the scenario clusters, slightly favor RSD-VNS (Hodges--Lehmann estimate $+0.025$~pp): a gain of the SSA phase larger than the margin is ruled out at the seed and the case level, whereas equivalence remains inconclusive;
\item LX-SSA-VNS is worse than RSD-VNS;
\item the small SSA gain over RS-VNS only reflects that control's weakness; LX-SSA-VNS and RS-VNS are practically equivalent.
\end{enumerate}
Re-evaluated with 1$^\circ$ direction bins (Table~\ref{S-tab:F-abl}), these findings hold, except that the SSA gain over RS-VNS is not significant with 1$^\circ$ bins.

\emph{Choice of Phase~1.} With the same VNS phase, PSO-VNS is significantly better than SSA-VNS and LX-SSA-VNS in most cases and significantly worse in none (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). At the switch, its global best is more often feasible and, when feasible, has a lower wake loss (Table~\ref{S-tab:switch}). Whether PSO helps mainly by faster feasibility or by better exploration remains open (cf.\ the feasible starts in Section~\ref{sec:feasinit}).
% CHECK-FINAL [C16]: PSOBV-SSABV, PSOBV-LXBV: L == 0, W >= 34; switch feasibility higher and switch loss lower for PSOBV

\emph{LX-SSA as published.} LX-SSA costs $2N_p$ evaluations per iteration (Section~\ref{sec:ssa}) and so runs half as many iterations as SSA. As published, it makes the salp swarm worse, alone and after VNS (last two contrasts of Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). An ablation on the twelve split cases separates the Laplace step from this cost (Section~\ref{S-sec:S-xp-lap}, Table~\ref{S-tab:S-xp-laplace}):
\begin{enumerate}
\item without its redundant re-evaluation, LX-SSA has a lower wake loss, so part of its deficit against SSA is its doubled cost;
\item at the cost of SSA, the Laplace candidate gives no lower wake loss than the SSA midpoint;
\item a uniform step with the same mean absolute value performs like the Laplace step, and smaller scales are worse.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Practical equivalence.} Table~\ref{S-tab:equivalence} of the supplementary material gives, for all 18 pairs, the TOST decision~\cite{Lakens2017} at $\pm0.05$~pp, the minimal margin and the Bayesian signed-rank probabilities~\cite{Benavoli2017}; Table~\ref{tab:equiv-main} summarizes the primary questions and the sampling-control contrasts. At the case level (a sensitivity analysis):
\begin{enumerate}
\item only PSO-VNS vs.\ PSO (a primary question; Section~\ref{sec:psovns-pso}) and LX-SSA-VNS vs.\ RS-VNS (exploratory, like all other pairs) are equivalent; no pair is equivalent over the scenario clusters;
\item PSO-VNS gains more than the margin over every control, as does the VNS phase after SSA and LX-SSA;
\item LX-SSA as published is worse than SSA by more than the margin;
\item over RS-VNS, a negligible SSA gain is the more probable outcome, though equivalence is not shown.
\end{enumerate}

For SSA-VNS vs.\ RSD-VNS the primary question is one-sided: does the SSA phase give a better start than disc sampling by a relevant amount? \subsection{Budget Split}
\label{sec:split}
% Page budget: kept in the supplementary material: \AblTable{tab:split}
Of the five splits tested on twelve cases, from $\omega=0.25$ to plain PSO ($\omega=1$; Table~\ref{S-tab:split}), $\omega=0.75$ has the lowest mean wake loss and average rank, and $\omega=0.9$ does not beat it.
% CHECK-FINAL [C47]: 0.75 best of the \NSplitNSettings settings (lowest mean loss and rank); [C59]: omega = 0.9 vs 0.75 W = L = 0, case-mean p >= 0.05 ("does not beat"); CHECK-EXTRA [X36]: 0.9 beats 0.75 at no level. SWEVO: the split table is back in the main text (tab:split; logged in optA/sw/moved.md)
Per case (Table~\ref{S-tab:split-cases}):
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\omega=0.75$ has a lower mean loss than $\omega=0.5$ in eleven cases (significantly in four) and a significantly higher one in none, and $\omega=0.25$ is significantly worse than $\omega=0.5$ in five cases;
\item plain PSO has a higher mean loss than $\omega=0.75$, so the best split is interior;
\item $\omega=0.9$ differs from $\omega=0.75$ in no case, and its mean loss lies between those of $\omega=0.75$ and $\omega=0.5$.
\end{enumerate}
The results of items~1 and~2 hold in all six farm clusters. We keep the preset $\omega=0.5$, which may slightly understate PSO-VNS; a larger split is a candidate to confirm on the target problem.

\subsection{Summary of the Ablation}
\label{sec:abl-summary}
The ablation shows that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the PSO phase is the component that matters: with the same VNS phase, PSO-VNS beats VNS alone, both random-sampling controls, SSA-VNS and LX-SSA-VNS (Section~\ref{sec:abl-swarm});
\item the VNS phase, at this budget essentially one compass-search descent, adds little after PSO (not significant, practically equivalent at the case level) but clearly improves SSA and LX-SSA (Section~\ref{sec:abl-vns});
\item at the benchmark spacing $4D$, the salp-swarm phases give no gain of practical size over sampling in the farm disc, and LX-SSA-VNS is worse than RSD-VNS (Section~\ref{sec:abl-swarm});
\item square sampling (RS-VNS) is a weak control, mainly because random layouts rarely satisfy the spacing constraint (Section~\ref{sec:abl-controls});
\item LX-SSA as published is worse than SSA, in part because of its doubled cost per iteration (Section~\ref{sec:abl-swarm});
\item the best split is interior: $\omega=0.75$ was best of the five splits tested, so the preset $\omega=0.5$ may slightly understate PSO-VNS (Section~\ref{sec:split}).
\end{enumerate}
