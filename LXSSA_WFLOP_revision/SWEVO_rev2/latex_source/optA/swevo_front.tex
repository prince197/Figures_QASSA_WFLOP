\begin{abstract}
The wind farm layout optimization problem (WFLOP) places turbines to minimize wake losses under boundary and spacing constraints. To solve it, we propose PSO-VNS, a new two-phase variant of particle swarm optimization (PSO) with variable neighborhood search (VNS). A constriction PSO first moves whole layouts towards the small, nonconvex feasible region; the basic VNS then refines the best layout by derivative-free compass search. The sequential design adds a single parameter and, because its first phase reproduces a PSO run exactly, isolates the gain of the local search. We evaluate PSO-VNS on the Kusiak--Song benchmark (two wind data sets, circular farms of three sizes), on Horns Rev~1 and Lillgrund wind farm blocks, and on the IEA37 case study. Comparisons use equal evaluation budgets, paired seeds, equivalence tests and equal-cost random-sampling controls. PSO-VNS ranks first on the benchmark. Its mean advantage over a well-configured PSO lies within a post hoc margin of $\pm0.05$ percentage points (pp) of wake loss, but grows to 0.19~pp when layouts are optimized with 1$^\circ$ direction bins (eight cases). It also reaches feasible layouts more often than PSO on Horns Rev~1 and Lillgrund. However, a real-coded genetic algorithm is best on Horns Rev~1 at a larger budget, and SLSQP with exact gradients is best on IEA37. Finally, the PSO baseline needs care: with $w=0.7$, $c_1=c_2=2$, unstable under stagnation, PSO falls behind salp-swarm hybrids; with constriction coefficients, it ranks ahead of them.
\end{abstract}
