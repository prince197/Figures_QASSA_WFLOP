\begin{abstract}
We propose PSO-VNS, a new two-phase variant of particle swarm optimization (PSO) with variable neighborhood search (VNS), for wind farm layout optimization. A constriction PSO first moves whole layouts towards the small, nonconvex feasible region; the basic VNS then refines the best layout by derivative-free compass search and neighborhood shaking. The sequential design adds a single parameter, gives both phases exactly counted budgets and, as its first phase reproduces a PSO run, isolates the gain of the local search. PSO-VNS is evaluated on the Kusiak--Song benchmark, with two wind data sets (direction-independent and direction-dependent wind speeds) on circular farms of three sizes, and on Horns Rev~1 and Lillgrund wind farm blocks and the IEA37 case study, with equal evaluation budgets, paired seeds, equivalence tests and equal-cost random-sampling controls. PSO-VNS ranks first on the benchmark; its mean advantage over a well-configured PSO lies within a post hoc $\pm0.05$ percentage point (pp) wake-loss margin, but reaches 0.19~pp when optimizing with 1$^\circ$ direction bins. It reaches feasible layouts more often than PSO on Horns Rev~1 and Lillgrund. A real-coded genetic algorithm leads at the larger budget on Horns Rev~1, and SLSQP with exact gradients leads on IEA37. The evaluation also shows that baselines change comparisons: the PSO setting $w=0.7$, $c_1=c_2=2$, variance-unstable under stagnation, puts PSO behind salp-swarm hybrids, and against equal-cost sampling in the farm an SSA first phase gains at most 0.05~pp.
\end{abstract}
