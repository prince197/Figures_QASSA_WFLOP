\begin{abstract}
We study how baseline configuration, component controls and treatment of unsuccessful runs change wind farm layout optimizer comparisons on the discretized Kusiak--Song benchmark (68 cases, 30 paired seeds, 6,030 evaluations), with random-sampling controls, all-run rankings and equivalence analyses. A real-coded genetic algorithm (GA) and exact-gradient baselines extend the eight-method pool. The PSO setting $w=0.7$, $c_1=c_2=2$ is variance-unstable under stagnation and puts PSO behind salp-swarm hybrids; a coefficient sweep bounds this explanation. An SSA first phase gains at most 0.05 percentage points (pp) of wake loss over disc sampling at $4D$ and $5D$, unlike at $6D$. PSO followed by VNS (PSO-VNS) ranks first, also when optimizing with 1$^\circ$ bins (eight cases); its mean advantage over well-configured PSO lies within a post hoc $\pm0.05$~pp margin at seed and exchangeable-case levels with 15$^\circ$ bins (not over scenario clusters), but 0.19~pp under direct 1$^\circ$ optimization. It is more often feasible than PSO on two measured-wind blocks; conditional quality rankings may miss the most reliable method. GA leads at the larger budget on Horns Rev~1, also under 1$^\circ$ bins and PyWake, and among gradient-free methods with 36 IEA37 turbines. On the piecewise-smooth IEA37 model, SLSQP with analytic gradients beats every metaheuristic at equal budget. In a constraint-handling study (six cases, five methods), Deb's rules with box clipping change the ranking; with radial projection the ranking and PSO-VNS's conditional advantage over PSO hold, but its all-run advantage is not significant. Conclusions concern the implemented configurations and models, not universal superiority.
\end{abstract}
