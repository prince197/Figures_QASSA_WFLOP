\begin{abstract}
We study how baseline configuration, component controls and treatment of unsuccessful runs change wind farm layout optimizer comparisons on the discretized Kusiak--Song benchmark (68 cases, 30 paired seeds, 6,030 evaluations per run), using random-sampling controls, feasibility-qualified and all-run rankings and equivalence analyses. A real-coded genetic algorithm (GA) and exact-gradient baselines extend the eight-method pool. The PSO setting $w=0.7$, $c_1=c_2=2$ is variance-unstable under stagnation and puts PSO behind salp-swarm hybrids; a coefficient sweep and clamping study bound this explanation. An SSA first phase gains at most 0.05 percentage points (pp) of wake loss over disc sampling at $4D$ and $5D$, unlike at $6D$. PSO followed by VNS (PSO-VNS) ranks first, also when optimizing with 1$^\circ$ bins; its mean advantage over well-configured PSO lies within a post hoc $\pm0.05$~pp margin at seed and exchangeable-case levels with 15$^\circ$ bins (not over scenario clusters) but reaches 0.19~pp with 1$^\circ$ bins. It is more often feasible than PSO on two measured-wind blocks; conditional quality rankings may miss the most reliable method. GA performs better at the larger budget on Horns Rev~1, also re-evaluated with 1$^\circ$ bins and PyWake, and with 36 IEA37 turbines. On the piecewise-smooth IEA37 model, SLSQP with analytic gradients outperforms the tested metaheuristics at the charged budget. Conclusions depend partly on constraint handling (Deb's feasibility rules with box clipping change the ranking; radial projection keeps it except PSO-VNS versus PSO) and concern the implemented configurations and models, not universal optimizer superiority.
\end{abstract}
